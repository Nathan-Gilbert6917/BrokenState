# Broken State — State Map Prototype

A browser prototype of the design doc, built to answer one question: *is running a hub, sending teams across a dangerous state and dealing with other groups fun?* Scavenging and hub building are abstracted; fights play out as XCOM-style tactical battles.

## Run it

```bash
node server.cjs
```

Then open http://localhost:5173. No install step and no dependencies; only Node.js is needed.

Headless tools (no browser):

```bash
node tools/simtest.js 120 42 8
```

Runs 120 in-game days on seed 42 with 8 AI groups and prints a summary (for balance checks). Add `size=small`, `size=large` or `size=huge` for another world size (Normal by default); `autotest.js`, `timelapsetest.js` and `maptest.js` take the same argument, `balancesim.js` takes `--size huge`.

```bash
node tools/cardcount.js
```

Counts which event cards fire across five 90-day runs, then lists the hub events (`src/sim/hubevents.js`) by id, recruit cards by source and warnings before desertion.

```bash
node tools/pacing.js 365 all
node tools/pacing.js log 120 balanced 3
```

Pacing and decision quality (findings in `review/pacing.md`; or `node tools/pacing.js [days] [cautious|balanced|aggressive|all] [seeds...]`, seeds 1–14 by default, about 8 minutes for 42 full years): plays autopilot campaigns and catches every card as it is added, whatever module adds it (the module, the hour, its choices and the autopilot's answer). Reports cards per in-game day (mean, p90, max, per 30-day bucket; all cards and decisions only), cards and clock stops per real minute at 1× and 3× (an hour is 3 s at 1×; every card pauses the clock), other pausing screens (search, trade, battle), bursts (most cards in 6 and 24 hours), quiet stretches, crises stacking (raid, horde at the walls, illness, a team in trouble within 3 days), team cards against base cards, the low-stakes share and the rate without it, big moments from the log next to the cards that warn of them (raids, hordes at the walls, groups turning hostile, hives, short rations), the mix by module, the top 25 cards (repeats within a week, the autopilot's answers) and the decisions with one answer 90%+ of the time. Fails on a crash or a card with no choice that can be taken; flags duplicate cards and cards answered after their subject was gone. `log` prints one run's full log day by day (the log panel keeps only 200 lines).

```bash
node tools/retreattest.js 40 7 all
```

Sounding the retreat (`combat.js` `soundRetreat`; or `node tools/retreattest.js [battles per matchup] [seed] [cautious|balanced|aggressive|all]`, 0 battles for the checks only): offered on the player's turn of a battle with an evac strip, refused in the enemy turn, with no way out and in a raid, the strip read through `inEvac` / `evacAt`; escape odds by distance, each enemy next to them (−25), a shooter in sight (−6), wounds, armor, Scouting, night and carrying, and the chance a caught unit goes down; the plan has the one next to a downed teammate carry them and can leave them; sounding it (the one on the strip always gets out, a stabilized teammate carried out lives, a dropped one is lost, a wounded unit next to the dead is often caught and sometimes dragged down, the outcome reaches the handler as "retreat"); a downed teammate on the strip goes with the last one out by the per-unit Retreat; the AI retreats against six elite soldiers, not against four of the dead, Cautious before Aggressive with one down and one hurt, Aggressive's numbers for a base defence, never where there is no way out; the same battle plays out the same; the panel's confirm (the odds, Leave / Carry, Esc, Shift+R, Space does nothing, Enter gives the order, the result's summary). Then the measurement: nine matchups (even raiders, an enemy ambush, 3 vs 4 raiders at power 5, soldiers at 7, 3 vs 6 raiders at 6, 12 and 16 of the dead, mixed, a hive with its Broodmother) on maps with an evac strip, each battle played twice from the same start (same team, map and dice), once with the AI never retreating and once in the given style; per matchup and style the wins, deaths and team wipes both ways, how often and on which turn the AI retreated, and how many of its retreats were from a fight the other run won. Fails if a battle it did not retreat from plays differently, if the retreat costs lives or wipes, if over a quarter of its retreats are from fights it would have won, or if it gives up more than 5% / 3% / 2% (Cautious / Balanced / Aggressive) of the wins. Seed 7, 40 a matchup: deaths 411 → 104 (Cautious), 414 → 182 (Balanced), 403 → 251 (Aggressive); team wipes 79 → 2, 75 → 3, 76 → 10; wins 218 → 205, 215 → 209, 218 → 217. In autopilot campaigns the only battles it plays on the map with a way out are hive assaults: over 16 three-year runs (Balanced and Aggressive, seeds 1–8; `state.autoRetreat = false` switches the AI's retreat off for a comparison) it stormed 36 hives, won every one and never retreated, and the runs ended identically with and without it.

Markers: a grave (with the gear still lying there), your abandoned truck or sedan (dusty, a red jerry can when it needs fuel, smoke when badly damaged), a rusted wreck (up on blocks once stripped), a cache of crates and a duffel (opened once emptied), an abandoned camp (collapsed once picked through). Bridges are railed decks along the road. The **Zombies** button (top bar) sets palette, gore, variants, eyes and clothes, saved in the browser.
- **Bases (Manage):** the hub and outposts share the same rules: roles (farm, scavenge, operate, build, guard, rest, medic, craft, teach, read), Heat (people, plus 3 per workshop station with an operator at work), defense, beds, healing, morale and desertion. Farmers tend the crop plots (see *Farming, water and food*). Under a week of food left, everyone goes on short rations (60%, costs mood, not health). A base that runs out of food starves: everyone there loses health every day it lasts (6, then 8, 10 … up to 20 a day; armor and Tough do not help) and nobody heals until there is food again, so about a week without food kills. Stock never goes below zero. Trade-route escorts waiting between trips live at the hub: they eat, heal and can be treated there, but do no base work. If the leader dies you pick a new one from the six best free people by Leadership. The run ends when nobody is left alive: deaths, desertion and a captive changing sides all count. Tabs for overview (with a food forecast line: days of food left and the next harvest, and water per day, stock and days left; the Bases list shows the same), farming (see below), workshops (base scavenging, stations with operators and running status), buildings and their bonuses, a construction queue with builders and timers, an armory with everyone's equipment (skill-gap rule shown), and vehicles.
- **Expeditions:** trip planner (crew, vehicle or on foot, stance, supplies with round-trip estimate). Packed supplies count against carry space (people, worn packs, the vehicle): a launch over the limit is refused, as is a crew member who is dead, away or already on a team, or an outpost on water. Food, water and fuel drain hour by hour; running out hurts (armor and toughness do not help against hunger and thirst). A team keeps the name it left with. If the last member of a team dies or leaves, the team is lost: what it carried lies at a death site on that tile and its vehicle stands abandoned there, both marked on the map; a team that loses people drops what the rest cannot carry on the spot (also recoverable).
- **Team orders:** when a team finishes a job it waits for orders: act on that tile, move to any tile (click or right-click), return home, or hold. Teams in radio range can be redirected while moving. Giving an order never changes the speed: an order given while the game is paused is armed and starts when you unpause (the pause banner counts the waiting orders, and the *Running orders* list and the team panel show "starts when unpaused"). When the order is done the team reports in with a "Done" card offering the next steps (an order that could not be done, or was stopped, reports in with a plain orders card saying why; a team cut off by water gets "Cannot go home").
- **Trip plans (`src/sim/plans.js`, planner section in `src/ui/plan.js`):** the planner's PLAN section builds the team's order queue before it leaves: the trip to the target with its mission, then steps (look around, scavenge here, scavenge the best sites nearby (up to N within R tiles, richest by what is known, picked when the step starts so the plan adapts), camp N hours, gather, wait N hours, move to a known site, return home), reordered or removed freely (nothing after Return home). Presets: *Recon and return*, *Scavenge and return*, *Explore* (recon, then the best 2 sites within 3 tiles, then home), *Camp N days, then return*, *Custom*. Missions that come back start with Return home after them. The planner shows the whole plan's time (every leg stepped hour by hour like the trip; searches count 6 hours, Look around 2, a recon's 2 hours are paid by the next move) and packs and checks food, water and fuel for it (plus the way home if the plan ends away), and a Heat look-ahead for camp and wait steps. *If … then come home*: anyone hurt below N health, food below N days, cargo full; checked every hour, the team turns home by itself and a "Coming home" card says why (continue home, resume the plan, or stop). While a plan runs the team raises a card, once each per trip, on a horde (15+) in sight, a rich site in sight (loot 55%+) that is not on the plan and not known to be rich (with *Search it next*), and food short of the way home; each offers continue, change the plan (team panel) or come home. The Teams tab and team panel show the plan, its steps left and its rule.
- **Radio rule:** a team in radio coverage (stations and linked relays) takes new orders at any time. Out of contact it follows its plan (the team panel and *Running orders* say "following its plan (no radio)") and you cannot give it orders, except through the cards it raises: while its orders or report card is open, and for the rest of the hour you answer one (its panel opens with orders then). A team holding out of contact checks in again with an orders card every 12 hours, an open-ended camp once a day. Your own base tiles count as contact.
- **Heat in the field (`src/sim/fieldheat.js`):** a team that stays on one tile (waiting, holding, camping, gathering, building a boat, looking around, crafting, repairing, treating) builds Heat from noise, smoke and smell, per hour: activity (waiting, looking around, treating 1; gathering, crafting, repairs 1.5; boat building 1.8; camp 2) × place (countryside 1, suburbs 1.4, city 1.8, × 1 + 0.6 × infection) × hive land 1.4 (2 on the ring next to a hive) × team size (0.7 + 0.1 a person) × night (a camp fire 1.3, otherwise 0.8) × stealthy 0.8, capped at 100. It falls 8 an hour on the move and is 0 within a tile of your bases. Each hour on the spot the chance of trouble is 12% × (Heat / 100)^1.5 × danger, where danger = 0.15 + 3 × the road's conflict chance there (groups, AI parties, infection, hordes, traffic) + 0.3 city / 0.15 suburbs + 0.3 hive land / 0.8 next to a hive + 0.2 a horde within 3 tiles (cap 2). Trouble is a road conflict card (the dead, a horde close by drawn in as a bigger surge, or people from the groups around; camp lookouts spot it a little more often) and halves the Heat; after it the team goes back to its camp or work. At most one conflict per 20 hours, as on the road. A quiet countryside camp for a night reaches Heat ~25 with about 1% risk; a night in an infected city is about a coin flip and three days there bring trouble every day or so. The team panel shows a Heat bar with the hourly chance and the reasons, the map a small bar under the team's sprite (hover the tile for the text). The autopilot gives up waiting at a site for another team's search past Heat 60.
- **Order queue and team panel:** click a team (map, Teams tab, or the *Running orders* list in the map's top-left corner, which shows each team's current order and time left) to open its panel: people (health, infection, skills, gear), packed supplies and found cargo against carry space, the vehicle's condition and fuel, and the queue. Actions: move, return home, scavenge, look around (2h), camp (8h / 24h / until broken; the hurt heal slowly, infected wounds need meds), roadside repair (1 part, 3h, better with Mechanics), treat a member with carried meds, field crafting, and boat crossings. A waiting team starts an action now; a team on the move (or with queue mode on) adds it to its queue, which runs by itself (e.g. move, then scavenge). A team stopped on the way with orders queued (after a road fight, say) puts the unfinished trip back at the front of the queue, so *Resume queued orders* carries on to the right tile; a queued job on the spot (a mission, look around, gathering) remembers the tile it was planned for and is dropped with a message if the team ends up elsewhere. A camp with no food or no water heals nobody. Arrival estimates (planner, panels, queue) step through the same hour-by-hour movement as the simulation.
- **Boat crossings:** water blocks travel and routes never cross it, but a team on a shore can build a one-use boat and cross one water tile to the land straight across (team panel, the orders card, or right-click the far tile). Cost: scrap = 4 + 2 per person + 1 per 20 carry units of supplies and cargo (+14 scrap and 2 parts to raft a pickup, +9 and 1 for a sedan, or leave the vehicle on the shore, marked for recovery); build hours = 2 + 0.5 per person + 1 per 40 load (+4 / +3 for a raft), cut 5% per Mechanics point (up to 50%), then 1h on the water (2h with a raft) with a small mishap risk (a crate lost, someone bruised, the raft vehicle damaged). Hours and materials are fixed when the order is given, so queue estimates hold. *Gather materials* (3h) turns up scrap, only on a shore with a crossing; a shore holds about 10 scrap and gets 1 back every 3 days. A boat order given before the team's vehicle changed (gained or lost one) is planned again when it starts. Delays on cards and the 2 hours of a recon are real hours at any pace.
- **Vehicles:** own fuel tanks and condition; worn vehicles break down more. A vehicle left behind (out of fuel, broken down, no road home, on a shore, at a raid site) is marked on the map and can be driven again when its tank plus the fuel the team carries reach 2. Found cargo the people cannot carry on foot stays inside it (logged, noted on its marker) and is back on board when it is recovered; a team that cannot restart it, or has a vehicle already, can still take what it can carry. Wrecked cars can be restored into new vehicles.
- **Markers and map layers:** teams roll perception to spot caches, wrecked cars and abandoned camps, marked with how long ago they were seen. Toggle layers (locations, territory, teams, hordes, death sites, vehicles, finds, infection, weather) in the map corner.
- **Tactical combat:** fights open an XCOM-style battle on a grid built from the tile (cover, two actions per unit, hit chances, flanking, overwatch, hunker, melee, meds, line of sight, retreat zone, ambush rules, enemy AI, zombies drawn by gunfire). Survivors and human enemies move 5 tiles an action; the dead move 3, the strong (power 5+, drawn as runners) 4 (review C-10's middle ground); the Wallbreaker and the Broodmother 2. Injuries and deaths carry back to the map. After a win the dead are bodies to loot (see Inventory). Every group fields its own people, humans only (raiders, fighters, guards, militia, soldiers, zealots); the dead fight beside humans only where both are found, as at a scavenge site. A sleeping enemy that is hit and lives wakes its group, whatever the weapon (silent kills stay silent). Makeshift gear wears when the battle ends: only on the units that took the field, and only the items they took it with. Battles that start in the same hour wait their turn; one whose team is gone by then is dropped.
  - *Sounding the retreat* (`combat.js` `soundRetreat`, `escapeOdds`, `retreatPlan`; any battle with an evac strip, on your turn): everyone still standing runs for the strip at once. On the strip with no enemy next to them, or anywhere with no enemy next to them, in sight or close: safe. Otherwise an escape check: 100 − 3 a tile of walking distance to the strip − 25 for each enemy next to them − 6 for each armed person who sees them and 4 for each of the dead (or a brawler) within two moves (those two at most 35) − 30 × the share of health lost − 2 a point of armor worn + 1 a point of Scouting + 10 at night (the hour the battle began), between 5 and 95%. Carrying a downed teammate who lies next to them: −10, and 4.5 a tile instead of 3 (each carries one; by default the one with the best odds carries, and the confirm can leave someone instead). Caught: one blow for each enemy next to them (one if none), 18–32 each before armor; at 0 health they go down and are left behind, otherwise they tear free and get out hurt; a caught carrier drops whoever they carried. A downed teammate carried out, or dragged onto the strip before the last one leaves (also with the per-unit Retreat), comes home like the downed after a win (stabilized: lives; else 50% + 2% a point of the best Medicine among those who got out). The outcome is "retreat" (a defeat if nobody got out) and goes to the battle's handler as before; the result screen lists who got out, who was caught and who was carried. The confirm (and the odds over each unit's head on the map) shows each one's chance, the chance of being lost when caught, the expected number out and who is left behind.
  - *The AI retreats* (`autoPlayerTurn`, `fightOutlook`, `RETREAT_AI`): at the start of its turn it weighs the two sides Lanchester-style (total firepower × total health ÷ armor; a gun counts its hit chance, −20 for cover against people, × damage; melee and the dead 40%) and sounds the retreat when the ratio falls below 0.45 (Cautious) / 0.3 (Balanced) / 0.18 (Aggressive), or when a third / half / two thirds of the team is down and the ratio is under 1. The style is the autopilot's (or `setup.aiStyle`, or the caller's); without one, Balanced. Defending a base on a break-in map that has a way out, every style uses the Aggressive numbers (giving up means abandoning the base). Human enemies already break and run when beaten badly (a third of them left and outnumbered 2 to 1; zealots never); the dead never flee.
- **Travel events:** about 18 choice cards with skill checks (hazards, the dead, raiders, traders, discoveries, crew drama), plus forced horde encounters. One card per team per hour; a card is dropped when its team is gone or the person it is about has died or left; costs are checked against what the team carries when the choice is made.
- **Fog of war and recon:** geography is known; current conditions are not. Teams report only when they get home, unless they are in radio coverage (live position and sight).
- **Radio network (`src/sim/radio.js`):** a radio station covers 8 tiles. A *radio relay* (outposts only: 15 scrap, 3 parts, 24 work-hours, a Mechanics 3+ survivor to build) covers 6 tiles around its outpost. It is an unmanned repeater, so it needs no operator, but it uses 1 part every 10 days (from the outpost, else the hub) and goes off the air without one. A relay works only when linked: within range of a station or of another linked relay, so relays chain coverage outward. Coverage is the union of every station and linked relay; the map draws each circle (red: not linked or off the air) and the base screen says what a base has (station, relay linked / not linked / offline, or covered from another base).
- **Radio to other groups:** groups whose base is within coverage plus 4 tiles (they have radios too) can be called from the Groups tab (*Radio…*). *Scan the airwaves* (once a day) finds groups in reach you have not met ("heard on the radio", their base marked). Calls: greet (+2 goodwill, every 3 days), ask for news (every 5 days; a report of 2 / 3 / 4 tiles around their bases for neutral / friendly / allied groups, and every horde within 10 tiles of them), ask for a trade caravan (every 4 days; neutral or better, aggressive types at goodwill 0+; yes with a chance of 45% + goodwill%, +25% traders, +15% commune), make a deal ("we want 20 meds, paying 60 ammo": accepted when it is fair for them and they can spare it; the panel shows their lowest price), call for help (friendly or a pact, every 10 days: a raid on its way to you loses 30% of its strength, or 20–30 food if the hub has under 3 days; costs 4 goodwill), and pacts. Hostile groups refuse business. Groups call you too: offers (their surplus for something you have) and requests (something they need, paying with their surplus), as cards, at most one every 6 days per group (traders 15% a day, a commune 10%, others 6%).
- **Caravans (`src/sim/caravans.js`):** a group's caravan (their wagon or truck on the map) walks to your base along a land route (no route, no caravan) with 2–5 guards; hordes and raiders can catch it on the way like any group party. At your base the trade window opens with any radio deal filled in; the caravan stays a day (Bases tab: *Trade* reopens the window) and then goes home. A radio deal not done by then costs 3 goodwill. You can attack it (trade window, with a confirmation): a battle at your base against its guards, the goods are yours if you win, and either way the group turns hostile (goodwill −70, pact torn up), other groups you know that are not aggressive lose 5 goodwill, and for 60 days the group sends raids whatever its type.
- **Your trade caravans:** Groups tab → *New trade caravan…*: crew, vehicle or on foot, stance, goods to trade, items from the hub's armory, supplies to live on (auto-fill), and an ordered list of destinations (known groups' bases, your outposts) with the hours of each leg. It runs on the normal order queue (a move, then the caravan stop order). At each stop a card offers: trade there (the trade window), unload the goods (your outposts), stay a day, go on to the next destination, or return home, each with its ETA; *Other orders…* gives the usual orders card, which has a *Trade caravan* entry to get back.
- **Scavenging / raids:** an interactive raid on a generated 28×18 map built from the site (buildings and outdoor areas set by location type, e.g. a hospital has clinics, a pharmacy and offices). Search containers next to you (S) for loot drawn from the area's table, scaled by how picked over the site is and by Scavenging; what does not fit stays inside. Guards patrol and the dead wander, unaware until they spot you (Stealth) or hear gunfire. Heat rises each turn and with noise; each stage brings more dead. Leave from a green extraction zone (X); a vehicle you bring waits in its own zone, and extracting elsewhere leaves it at the site. A raid ends when everyone still standing has left: a downed teammate who was stabilized is carried out (at 5–15 health) if anyone extracts, one still bleeding has the same chance as the downed in a lost battle, and with nobody out everyone on the map is lost. Raid time passes on the map clock afterwards. Headless tools still use the older hour-by-hour abstraction.
- **Death sites:** the dead keep their gear where they fell (someone who dies at their own base leaves no death site: their things go to that base's armory); scavengers strip the most valuable items first; looted gear turns up with nearby groups; recovery missions take what fits in the cargo and say what was left. A site with nothing left on it is removed with its marker, and a team that finds nothing there (or finds an abandoned vehicle gone) clears the marker when it reports.
- **AI groups:** 7 types with starting attitudes, growth, expansion, raids on you and each other, convoys, attitude drift from your expansion, calls for help (instant by radio, late by runner), collapse and refugees. Only a hostile group (goodwill −40 or below) raids you (`factions.js` RAIDS): aggressive types roll their daily chance after an 8–16 day cooldown (raiders 10.5%, zealots 7.5%, expansionists 6%), peaceful groups you provoked into hostility rarely (military 4.5%, fortress 3%, traders and communes 1.5%; left alone, no base of yours within 5 tiles and no new offence for 30 days, they cool off by 0.1 goodwill a day); from their base nearest to yours, within 30 tiles. Your base's weight among their targets: 2.5 × greed (stock value / 1500, 0.4–3) × (1 + 0.2 per outpost) × (1 + 0.5 per point of grudge, at most 4: +1 for each raid of theirs you beat, fading 1% a day) × their size (strength / 35, 0.6–2) ÷ (1 + tiles / 12). The raid is sized to your base's prepared defence (0.6–1.0 ×, × escalation: +25% for each raid of theirs you beat, up to 2×, reset when one gets in), at most the group's whole strength. The 8-hour siege runs faster against a strong defence (each hour counts defence / strength / 1.2, 1–4); then held at 1.2×, a break-in at 0.8×, else overrun (what they take goes into their stores, and a raid that got in wins them 1–2 recruits). Tribute's +6 goodwill fades again at 0.5 a day from an aggressive group. A card after each raid says what was taken, who was hurt and what was smashed (`breakdowns.js` raid damage). Growth: aggressive groups recruit drifters (12% + 2% a base a day when the usual growth roll adds nobody, below 45 people, with food for two days a head) and expand at their rate + 2% (× people / 18) a day up to 2 + people / 10 bases (at most 6; others 4), within 7 tiles, toward rich sites and toward you (never within 3 tiles of your bases); a group whose raid or war raid breaks another takes over its bases, 2–4 of its people and half its stores (a group broken by hordes or by you: the strongest aggressive group within 15 tiles, 50%); a won raid between groups takes the base 80% of the time for an aggressive attacker and carries home half of what the defenders lose; with fewer than 2 aggressive groups left hostile to you (from day 60) a new gang from beyond the border settles 12–30 tiles from your hub (2% a day, 12–18 people). The raid warning gives the number of attackers and what *Prepare defenses* really does (non-guards count 1.3× instead of 0.8×); the alert ends when the raid is resolved, bought off or never arrives. *Defend in person* fields that many attackers (count × power = the raid's strength, at least one); a lost defence is the break-in itself, with no second raid by the numbers. Tribute is 30% of the food on hand when it is paid. A collapsed group's land is free at once and its raids and convoys vanish. Groups you have not met are not named in the log.
- **Sites run dry; other groups scavenge too** (`src/sim/aiscav.js`): site loot (`tile.loot`) is one finite stock for everyone and nothing grows back after the map is made (the only new loot is a crash site or supply drop, which burns out again; a boat crew's driftwood on a shore is separate and still washes up, 1 scrap every 72 hours). Your searches and raids lower it, and so do the groups: each group with 6+ people sends one scavenging party at a time, every 6–10 days when it is short of something (need = 1 − stock ÷ want; wants per head: food 10, ammo 20 × arms, scrap 6, fuel 2, meds 2, preserved 1, parts 3 + 0.2; it goes when a need reaches 0.25, else it looks again in 3 days). The party (strength pop × 0.12, at least 2, on foot; on the map as walkers in the group's look with packs, a crowbar and a stuffed sack) goes to the best site within 10 tiles of one of its bases by its needs × loot left ÷ (1 + distance ÷ 5): never a base, a hive, a crash site, a horde's tile, your land or another group's, or a site another party is working. It searches 8 hours, taking 0.03 loot an hour × its strength ÷ 3 (0.5–1.5) down to 3%, finds per point of loot what one of your teams would (the hour-by-hour search: 6.5 × loot left per 0.05, by the site's loot table) × 0.8, and walks home, where the haul goes into the group's stock. A horde on the way or at the site costs a quarter of its strength and of its haul (at the site they pull out); raiders near their base may ambush it on the way home (12% a tile) and take the haul; below strength 1 it is gone. Sites inside a group's own land lose 0.3% of their loot a day into its stock at half the yield. In exchange, groups make only 40% of the ammo, scrap, fuel and meds they used to make from nothing, and their fields feed them 2% of a head a day less. Your team on a site their party is working (not in the middle of its own search: then they wait) gets a *Contested site* conflict card (once per party and team; a friendly or allied group, or one with a pact, keeps to its side); win the fight and they leave, dropping their haul into your cargo as far as it fits. You only learn a site was stripped when you see it (in view, a team passing or reporting, recon, news over the radio): the map and the tile panel show what you last saw. Radio news: when a party took 0.12+ loot from a site (or dropped it a band) within 15 tiles of one of your bases and you have a radio station, the log says *On the radio: Red Hand has been stripping the Calder Mall.* (in view: *Red Hand scavengers are stripping …*), at most one a day and one a site every 10 days, and the loot you know there is updated (only the loot; the rest keeps its age). Over a year (five seeds, Balanced autopilot) about 68% of the site loot is left on day 120, 54% on day 240 and 48% on day 365 (93% without scavenging groups); the yearly group collapse rate stays the same within noise (0.40 → 0.42 a group a year; `tools/depletetest.js`).
- **Outsiders (`src/sim/visitors.js`, Groups tab section and alerts in `src/ui/visitors.js`):** people from beyond the State of Calder who are not one of its groups (`state.visitors`). They enter on a land tile of the map's border (the border hordes' tiles, `hives.js borderTiles`: not within 6 of your bases; merchants on a road), travel the map and leave from a border tile (at the latest a few days after their stay is up). Spawns are rolled daily on their own dice (`state.visitorInfo.rng`). *Raiders:* the first band between days 20 and 26, then every clamp(30 − 0.06 × day, 10, 30) × 0.75–1.25 days (about 29 days early, 23 at day 120, 16 at day 240, 10 from day 334; at most 1 band on the map, 2 from day 120, 3 from day 240); clamp(3 + day / 30 + 0–2, 3, 14) people of power 2.6 + min(3.4, day / 80) (clubs and old pistols → assault rifles and plate). They pick what is weakly defended against what it holds: your bases (only when their strength is at least 0.6 × its defence), your teams (not within 2 tiles of a base; at 0.9 × the team), your convoys, a group's base (at 1.2 × 8 + 0.8 × its strength, one per band), groups' caravans on foot, merchant caravans and rich sites (loot 0.45+, not within 8 tiles of a group's base: their scavenging grounds; 10–18% taken). Your base: a warning card when they are 4 tiles out and seen or heard (prepare, pay them off: 4 × their strength in goods, talk them down: Speech 10% + 3.5% a point up to 75% for half the price, defend in person on the deploy map, hold); after 8 hours the numbers decide like a group's raid (held at 1.2, a break-in at 0.8 taking 20% of the stock (10% with a storehouse), else overrun: 50% (25%) and an outpost lost); what they take they carry, and beating them later gives it back. Your teams: a card when a band that thinks it can win is within a tile (fight, pay a toll of 35% of the load up to 3 × their strength, talk, run: 30% + 2.5% per Scouting, +20% a vehicle, +10% stealthy), or when you saw them first (slip away, ambush, lie low). A group's base: a 12-hour siege, then `factions.js resolveRaidOnFaction` (they keep 60% of what the group lost). Over the radio (Groups tab, their tile in coverage) you can pay them to leave the state (1.25 × the tribute, 2% less per point of Speech, at most 30%). They leave when they carry 10 × their strength, lost half the band, were paid or their 8–14 days are up. *Merchants:* the first on days 8–14, then every 10–16 days (1 at a time, 2 from day 150): 3–5 guards (+1 every 90 days, at most 7, power 3.5 + min(2.5, day / 120)) and 2 traders with a wagon train, carrying fuel 15–40, meds 8–22, preserved food 20–50, parts 3–9, ammo 60–160, a manual or blueprint (75%, a second 25%, unstudied ones first), 1–3 books and 2–4 pieces of good gear; they want food, water, scrap and jars (`trade.js` prices: their stock per head sets their need, with their own profile in `TRADE_PREFS` "merchants": food, water, scrap and jars wanted, valuables and collectibles prized for resale, fuel, meds and ammo a little cheaper, and a 10% margin on what they charge; travellers use "explorers"; they trade with the groups by demand on both sides). They visit up to 3 groups (never raider gangs or zealots), trading what each is short of for what they lack, and one of your bases when invited over the radio ("they can stop at your hub in about 30 hours if you want", when they come into coverage) or unannounced 55% of the time; there they stay 24 hours and the trade window opens (a team that meets them on the road can trade too). Rob them and merchant reputation drops 45 (new merchants come with goodwill 15 + reputation, hostile and no trade at −40, and skip your bases from −30; +0.25 a day back to 0), known traders lose 15 goodwill and communes and fortresses 8. *Explorers:* the first on days 5–10, then every 6–12 days (at most 2): scouts (40%, 2–3), refugees (35%, 2–5) or wanderers (25%, 1–2) walking between 2–3 ruins away from the groups' grounds (4–8 hours each, 3–6% of the loot) and, refugees 75% of the time (wanderers 45%, scouts 30%), to your hub. At the gate or on the road: news (another state's rumour, and the tiles along their way put on your map dated when they passed: the fog lifts only there), the scouts' maps (2 tiles either side, for 12 + 0.5 a tile visited in goods), trade (the trade window), who wants to stay (a recruit card, `recruits.js`: refugees 1–3, wanderers 0–1), shelter for 3 days (1 food and 1 water each a day, mood +3 at the base; then a gift half the time and someone asking to stay a third of the time). 8% are thieves who rob the base that shelters them (12% of its food, meds and ammo) or a team they can take. *Seen* only when your bases or teams in radio contact can see them (state.live) or *heard* in radio coverage (faded on the map); the Groups tab's **Outsiders** section lists those you know (what they are doing, attitude, last position) with Locate / Trade / At the gate / Invite to trade / Ask for news / Pay them to leave; alerts for raiders within 8 tiles of a base and merchants at one; log and diary lines; the map draws raiders from outside in dark red (banner, biker), merchants as a gold wagon train with oxen and travellers in grey; the timelapse records them and captions bands crossing the border and striking groups. The autopilot answers their cards by style (Cautious pays a band it may not hold, Aggressive defends in person), trades with merchants (a manual nobody has studied, then what the hub is short of), takes their news and maps and decides recruits by its bed and food rule.
- **What things are worth to whom (`src/sim/valuation.js`, tables in `data.js` `CATEGORIES` / `TRADE_PREFS`):** every supply and item has a hidden base value the player never sees. The trade window, the radio deal and the caravan planner show only what a given group pays or charges, with short *why* lines (▲ wanted, ▼ little wanted, ✕ refused: "At war: rifles wanted", "Winter is coming: fuel wanted", "Zealots: they will not touch liquor", "They already have spare armor"); the window's header says what the group's kind prizes and shuns and its situation right now. Elsewhere (inventory and armory tooltips, crafting) an item shows only a worth tier: junk (< 4), cheap (< 12), decent (< 35), valuable (< 90), rare. A price = worth × kind × state × holdings × spread. **Kind** (`TRADE_PREFS`, by category: × on both what they pay and charge, 0 = refused): military rifles 1.35, automatics 1.5, ammo 1.3, armor 1.35, electronics 1.5, batteries 1.3, drugs 0.4, comforts 0.6; raiders guns 1.2–1.4, fuel 1.4, liquor 1.6, cigarettes 1.6, drugs 1.7, jewelry 1.5, books 0.45, farm goods 0.5; commune food 1.25, seeds and farm tools 1.6, meds 1.3, books 1.35, jars 1.4, coffee and sweets 1.35, batteries 1.3, gold 0.6, automatics 0.5; fortress scrap 1.5, parts 1.4, defences 1.6, armor 1.35, tools 1.3; expansionists fuel 1.5, vehicle parts 1.6, guns 1.15–1.35; traders everything 1.1 (valuables and collectibles up to 1.4: they resell) and a 10% margin on what they charge; zealots refuse liquor, drugs and cigarettes, gold 0.4 (vanity), books 0.35, comforts 0.5, meds 1.4, food 1.3; merchants from outside food 1.35, water 1.3, scrap 1.4, jars 1.5, collectibles 1.4, fuel / meds / ammo 0.85–0.9, margin 10%; travellers food, water, camp gear. **State** (live, no dice; × held to 0.35–2.6): at war weapons and armor ×1.3, ammo and meds ×1.35, fine things ×0.85; hordes (20+ within 5 tiles of a base, or a horde attack in 14 days) weapons ×1.2, ammo ×1.25, armor ×1.15, defences ×1.4; raided in 10 days weapons and armor ×1.15, defences ×1.3, fine things ×0.85; a base lost in 20 days weapons ×1.25, armor ×1.2, defences ×1.4, fine things ×0.75; sickness (8-day spells, 15% of 30-day cycles, 30% in winter) meds ×1.5; winter fuel and warm clothes ×1.4, food ×1.15; the last 10 days of autumn fuel ×1.25; harvest jars ×1.25, food ×0.9; summer water ×1.2, camp gear ×1.3; spring seeds ×1.4; starving (under 2 food a head) fine things ×0.6 ("they cannot eat gold"); rich (supplies worth 1.6× the normal a head, at peace) fine things ×1.2; poor ×0.8. **Holdings:** supplies by the stock curve below, and starving or thirsty groups pay up to 60% more again for food or water as their stock runs out; items by the spare ones of the same category on their shelf (books by title): 1.15 with none, −0.5 per comfortable amount (per head: rifles 0.25, armor 0.4, books 0.04…), down to 0.25. Every unit of a deal is priced halfway through it, so buying low from one group and selling high to another pays (that is what trade routes and caravans are for), but each sale fills the buyer up until it is a loss. **Valuables** (barter goods: jewelry, cigarettes, liquor, coffee and sweets, phones and cameras, collectibles, batteries and household goods) are good for nothing but trade and light (their own `weight` in carry space); five examples are in `data.js` (gold ring, cigarettes, whiskey, coffee, batteries) and no site drops them yet. **Tagging a new item:** give it `cat:` from `CATEGORIES` (an untagged item gets a guess from its slot and fields); a new kind of thing gets a `CATEGORIES` entry and, if groups care, numbers in `TRADE_PREFS`. The autopilot buys from and sells to the groups that pay best (a trade visit goes to the buyer that pays most for its load for the walk, pays with what the group wants most, radio deals pick the cheapest group, and at your gates it sells valuables and spare weapons to groups that prize them). Groups remember raids (`hitDay`, `hordeHitDay`) and lost bases (`lostBaseDay`) on the faction; old saves simply have none.
- **Trade and diplomacy (`src/sim/trade.js`, window in `src/ui/tradewindow.js`):** one price model for visits, caravans, radio deals and trade routes. Items: `itemValue` = base value × (0.2 + 0.8 × condition) × (makeshift gear: 0.3 + 0.7 × uses left / full uses); a pack adds its contents; × kind, state and holdings (above). Supplies: base value × the group's need (stock per head against a reference: 0.5 with plenty, up to 2.2 with none) × kind and state, each unit priced along the curve (a big sale pays less per unit), so selling and buying back always loses. Spread: they pay 75% (+0.8% per point of your best Speech, up to +12%; ± goodwill/1000, up to ±10%) and charge 130% (minus the same). Example: 10 meds to a group short of them (need 2) at Speech 5, goodwill 20 pays 10 × 6 × 2 × 0.81 ≈ 97; buying 10 from a group with plenty (need 0.5) costs 10 × 6 × 0.5 × 1.24 ≈ 37. A deal goes through when your side, at their buying price, covers theirs at their selling price; radio deals are honoured as agreed. Groups keep a reserve (food 2, water 1.5, ammo 3, meds 0.3, fuel 0.5 a head) and have water now (wells refill to 6 a head), so water is no longer top price. Goodwill from trading grows with the deal's size and what you overpay, at most 4 a day per group; gifts up to 15 a day. The trade window: two columns (you give / you get), every supply with −10 / − / amount / + / +10 / All, every item one by one (condition, uses left, price), running totals and a balance bar, *Balance with* ammo, scrap or food, the reason a deal is refused, gifts, pacts, and (their caravan) an attack button. Carry space: a team carries what it gets, their caravan what it takes. Intel: buy a report of the area around them (3 / 6 / 10 tiles, 0.4 a tile at their price; only those tiles lose their fog: owner, loot left, infection, hordes, hives), horde and hive sightings within 12 tiles, or sell your own maps (0.15 a tile you know well near them, up to 40, once every 10 days). A team carries a report home, or radios it in; at your base it goes on the map at once. One trade screen at a time: a second team or caravan waits its turn. A group that turns hostile tears up its pact (log line).
- **Trade routes:** recurring convoys with Light / Standard / Heavy escorts following the design doc's risk table. Terms follow the group's barter prices (`routeProblem` / `createRoute` in `trade.js` refuse a route that asks for more than the load is worth and say why), and each convoy is paid what its load is worth that day, up to the agreed amount and what they have. A route with no escort left pauses itself.
- **Supply runs:** convoy jobs between your own bases (hub ↔ outpost, outpost ↔ outpost), set up in a base's *Supply runs* tab: crew (with their current gear), optional vehicle, goods to deliver, goods to bring back, stance, and once or every X days. At the destination the convoy comes back, holds there (a parked convoy: the crew do not work that base but eat and drink from its stock; send it home or disband it later), or disbands (crew join that base, vehicle parks there, the job stays as a template you can recreate; recreating from the far end runs the other way with the lists swapped). Runs travel like trade convoys (escort table by crew size, stance, fuel, wear, deaths). Editing a job changes the next run, not the convoy already on the road. If the vehicle is missing, broken or short of fuel the convoy goes on foot when the load fits, otherwise it waits; every blocked case is logged. Outposts hold back the stock their runs need instead of shipping it to the hub.
- **Medical:** everyone at a base heals slowly (faster with Medicine). Build a hospital and upgrade it step by step: Makeshift infirmary (2 beds) → Field hospital (4) → Clinic (6) → Trauma hospital (10). Each tier needs people on the Medic role (1/1/2/3); with too few it runs as the highest tier its medics can staff (a Clinic with one medic works as a Field hospital, with none as no hospital), and an upgrade that finishes understaffed logs a warning. Beds go to people below the base's admit line (Medical tab, "Admit below N health", 30–100 in steps of 10, default 70) and to anyone with a severe (below 30) or infected wound or too ill to work (work factor below 35%), worst off first; medics are never patients. Patients in beds heal faster, get +3 mood a day and do not work while admitted, and meds work better; everyone else keeps working and heals slowly. **Treatment limits:** one wound treatment per patient per day (at a base or in the field; illnesses one per condition a day), and every treatment takes a medic's time: each Medic-role survivor at a base gives 3 + Medicine/4 treatments a day (fewer while ill), with nobody on the role the best at Medicine gives 2 a day, and surgery takes 3 of one medic's (most of a day; with no medic that free the patient gets a plain treatment). **Automatic care** (one rule for every automatic dose, each night): worst off first, it treats wounds below 70 health or infected and illnesses that matter, never takes the base's meds below its reserve ("Keep N meds in reserve", default 0; your own Treat buttons can use the reserve) and never beyond the medics' time; it can be switched off per base. The Medical tab shows medic time used today, what tonight's care will do, each patient's bed status and why, when they can next be treated (and why a Treat button is disabled), and a health-per-day figure that is the expected change over the next day (passive healing hour by hour, tonight's treatment, illness). Severe wounds (below 30 health) heal at 40% speed without a bed and at the tier's speed in one; from the Clinic up, surgery makes treatments of severe wounds 50% bigger and med stations make +1 meds a day (staffed only, like everything a hospital does). A kit in the treater's off hand adds to their Medicine for every treatment, at a base, in the field or in battle: bandage and splint kit +2 (each treatment uses it up a little), surgical kit +4 (full at Medicine 10, less below, nothing below 3). The base's Medical tab lists the injured with a Treat button (uses base meds). People out on a team are treated with the team's carried meds (weaker than base care): a 1-hour team order from the team panel or the People tab, and only while the team is in contact. Without contact the People tab does not show a team's health either. Wounds below 40 health can get infected (half healing at a base, worse on the road); a field hospital bed prevents it, and from the Clinic up a treatment cleans it for sure (night care treats infected wounds first).
- **Illness and injuries** (`src/sim/health.js`): named conditions with a severity (0–100) that drift each day (better with rest, a hospital bed and Medicine; worse with hunger or thirst), cost health while they last, cut how much the person can work (builders, crafters, farmers, station operators; pneumonia stops work), and cost meds to treat (one treatment per condition a day, bigger with a better hospital). Deep cut (work accidents, wounds: an untreated cut can become an infected wound, the existing `woundInfected`), sprain, fever (heat, hunger, filth on a site; can turn into pneumonia), flu (cold, crowding; contagious; pneumonia in the cold), dysentery (dirty water: a water filter makes it rarer; thirst; contagious; +1 water a day), pneumonia (kills without meds). Where they come from: work at a base (farming 0.6% a day, building 1.2%, crafting 0.8%, a workshop 0.6%, base scavenging 2%), searching a site (0.8% a cut and 0.25% a fever per person per search hour), a hit of 10+ that does not kill (25% a deep cut), the weather (`exposureRisk`: in full on the road, 60% for outdoor roles, much less indoors), hunger or thirst (6% a day at a base), dirty water (0.4% a day without a filter), crowding (more people than beds) and contagion (more when crowded, less in quarantine; medics catch it more often). Three or more contagious cases bring an *Outbreak* card (quarantine for 10 days, treat everyone now, or carry on). Each night a base's automatic care (see Medical) treats the sick along with the wounded, worst off first, keeping the meds reserve and within the medics' time (pneumonia ranks first, but the reserve holds for it too: set it to 0 to spend everything), and teams use their carried meds on anything serious. The People tab, survivor screen and Medical tab show conditions (with a Treat button each); people too ill to work get hospital beds.
- **Newcomers ask first** (`src/sim/recruits.js`): everyone who would join is a card: a stranger at the gate, refugees after a group collapses (1–3), a loner on the road, survivors saved or freed after a road fight (rescued from the dead, a robbery's victims, prisoners bought or freed), a lost teenager (hub event) and a deserter coming back. The card window shows each candidate (portrait, health, best skills, traits, what they carry) and the hub's free beds, days of food and water, now and with them (and on the road the team's food and water against the way home). At a base: *Take them in* (5 food each on arrival), *Take only X in* (several at once), *Send them to* an outpost (they walk: about 1 day per 8 tiles, 97% − 1.2% per tile to make it, at least 60%; free beds there shown), *Give them supplies and part ways* (3 food and 3 water each: +2 mood for everyone there, +2 goodwill with known groups that are not hostile within 12 tiles), *Turn them away*. On the road: *Take them along* (only when the team's food and water cover everyone on the way home), *Send them to* the nearest base or the hub on foot, part ways (2 food and 2 water each from the team, +2 team mood, the same goodwill), turn away. Walkers arrive (or never do, logged) after their days on the road.
- **Morale, crowding and desertion** (`src/sim/morale.js`): beds: +1 mood a day when everyone has one; over the beds, −(2 + 8 × over) a day up to −16, where over = (people − beds) / beds (11 in 10 beds −2.8, 15 in 10 −6, 20 in 10 −10). The leader at a base adds 0.15 × Leadership, but while it is crowded at most half the crowding penalty, so a good leader softens crowding and never cancels it. The base panel shows *Beds: 10 for 13 · Overcrowded (130%) mood −4.4/day* (with no leader there) (Room to spare, Full, Crowded, Overcrowded, Packed, Crisis); `crowding(state, base)` (people per bed) is there for illness spread. Below 30 mood someone *talks about leaving* (a log line, and a card for the worst case at each base each day), below 20 they are *close to leaving* (log line and card); the card offers *Talk to them* (the leader there, or the best Speech: 30% + 3.5% per point, up to 95%, mood +25, else +5), *Give them 3 days' rest* (the Rest role, +5 now, then back to their role), *A gift from the stores* (4 food, 1 meds: +20 mood, +30 for the Greedy) or *Let them be*. The People tab shows the risk next to the mood (Unhappy, Talks about leaving, Close to leaving, May walk out any night). Desertion: below 15 mood, only after the second warning on an earlier night, 8% a night (+0.6% per point below 15); leaders never desert. A deserter keeps everything they carry (worn, held, pockets and pack) and takes 2–4 food, 2–3 water and maybe 1–2 meds and 5–12 ammo (Greedy twice as much and likelier, a Coward half the food and water; never more than a quarter of a stock); the log line names it all. Between 8 and 90 days later a deserter may turn up at the gate again (0.6% a day) as a recruit card. The run still ends when the last one leaves.
- **Mood that matters** (`src/sim/relations.js` `moodFactors`, `morale.js` `moodWork`): mood sets how much work people do: 70% at 0, 80% at 20, 100% from 45 to 80, 110% at 100 (straight lines between; `health.js` `workFactor` multiplies it with illness, so builders, crafters, farmers, station operators, medics, base scavengers and readers all follow it). New daily factors besides beds, the leader, food, rest and the dog: nothing but preserved food for 7 days −1 (21 days −2), hot meals from a kitchen +1, a **Common room** (22 scrap, 30 work-hours, up to 2, no operator) +1.5 for up to 15 people a room, a **Chapel** (16 scrap, 24 work-hours, no operator) +1 and grief weighs half, books in a pack +0.5 each up to +1 (books on the shelf count up to 2 a person but are shared, each copy going round 5 people, so a big hub needs a bigger shelf; manuals and blueprints from `blueprints.js` are study, not comfort), the **Inspiring** perk +0.5 from each inspiring person at the base (up to +1), cabin fever after 14 days of winter −0.5 (21: −1), three grey days in a row −0.5, friends nearby +0.4 each (up to +1.2), a partner +1.5 (away: −0.5), rivals −0.6 each (up to −1.8), grieving −(0.5 + bond) a day, after a rough trip (a death, someone badly hurt or downed, 10+ days out: −4 at once) +3 a day resting or −1 a day working for 4 days, and high spirits fade by 10% of the mood above 50 a day (a hub with only beds and food settles around 70–80; one with a kitchen, a common room and friends stays near 100, a crowded or grieving one well below). Hover any mood bar (People tab, survivor screen) for every factor with its number, the recent one-off changes and the work it gives. **Breaks:** below 12 mood, 10% + 2% per point below 12 a day (at most one in 5 days): they sit the day out or wander off (no work), binge on 3 food from the stores, or pick a fight (the other loses 3–8 health, their opinion drops); afterwards mood +8; a card (talk to them, 2 days' rest, let them be) for a fight or a second break within 10 days.
- **Friends, rivals and grief** (`src/sim/relations.js`): every pair who share a base, a team, a job or a fight has an opinion (−100..100) that drifts each day toward 100 × compatibility (seeded chemistry, temper: Warm / Easy-going / Prickly, traits: Brave vs Coward −30, Loud and Greedy −10 each, Former Medic +10, Speech) plus shared history (a fight together +6, +10 if someone went down or died; a trip +3, a rough one +6; hungry days; quarrels, arguments, gifts; it fades 1% a day) plus 0.6 × the leader's Leadership where the leader is; 5% of the gap a day at a base, 2.5× on a team, more at the same job. Friends at 40, close friends at 70, rivals at −30. Partners come from the hub event *two survivors grown close* (it now picks the closest friends) and split up below 15. In battle +3 aim per friend alongside (up to +6) and −2 per rival (up to −4); by the numbers a team's strength ×(1 + 3% per friend pair − 3% per rival pair). Rivals make hub quarrels likelier (and are the ones quarrelling 70% of the time) and argue on the road (weight 5 instead of 2); friends on a team tell stories by the fire (+2 team mood, +4 for them). A death: friends and a partner lose 6 + 14 × bond mood at once and grieve for 3 + 7 × bond days (bond = opinion/100, a partner 1), working up to 20% slower; acquaintances −3; friends of someone killed by a group want revenge (+5 aim against them for 60 days, +6 mood when avenged). A friend who deserts costs half as much. On the survivor screen: their mood and its factors, people (partner, friends, rivals with opinion and why; *Talk it out* with the leader or best Speech, or a *Shared meal* for 3 food, once in 10 days), the **service record** (days with the group, trips, sites searched, the dead put down, people killed, people treated, buildings built, food harvested, hives destroyed, times downed, rescues) and **notable moments** ("Held the gate during the 300-horde break-in at Dunmore Holdout, day 147"). The People tab shows a chip (♥ partner, +friends, −rivals; Grieving, On a break, Shaken). `survivorSummary(state, s)` gives all of it for other screens. **Memorial wall** (base panel → *Memorial wall*): everyone who died, with portrait, days with the group, cause and place of death, record highlights, moments and who grieved them; *Hold a memorial service* (1 food per person, once a week): everyone +3 (+2 more in a chapel), the grieving +8 more and 2 days less grief; also a hub event (*Remembering the dead*: a proper service, a few words, or no time for it). The autopilot keeps friends together and rivals apart among near-equal crew picks, rests people low after a rough trip, builds a common room once the hub has 8+ people and spirits are below 85 (saving up for it below 75), or as planned comfort once the hub has 12+ people and the scrap (saving up for it at 15+), and holds a memorial service when people grieve or spirits are low.
- **Hub events** (`src/sim/hubevents.js`): decisions for the days at home: a daily chance of 4% + 2.6% per person at the hub (8–40%, up to 30% more when overcrowded), never two days running and never while one is open: about one every 8 days at 4 people, 5 at 10, 4 at 20. Weighted by season, weather, crowding, mood and size: a **fire** in a bunkhouse, workshop, garage, watchtower or rain catcher (fight it with 15 water and every hand: 45% + 4% per person to save it; sand and blankets: 20% + 2.5% per person; or let it burn; someone may be burned for 8–18), a **quarrel** (side with one: +8 / −12, mediate with the leader or best Speech: both +6 or both −6, stay out: both −5 and 30% it comes to blows), a **peddler** (two swaps at fixed amounts), a **lost teenager** (a recruit card: +4 mood to all if taken in, −4 if turned away), a **wandering preacher** (everyone +5 for 2 food, but anyone below 35 mood may follow them away; or feed them and send them on), **food missing** (the food is gone when the card comes; find out who: half back, the thief −10 mood; lock the stores for 4 scrap: far less theft for 30 days; or let it go: likelier for 20 days), a **stray dog** (eats 0.5 food a day, +0.5 mood a day for everyone), **storm damage** after storms or rain (a leaking roof over the stores: patch for 4 scrap, a tarp for 1 that holds 60%, or lose 8% of the food; a broken well pump, now a breakdown (`breakdowns.js`, see *Things break*): repair it (a mechanic, 6 work-hours, 3 scrap and 1 part), jury-rig it (2 h, 1 scrap, breaks again sooner) or leave it on the Repairs list (6 less water a day until it is fixed)), a **feast** (2 food each: +10, 1 each: +5, not now: −2), a **personal errand** (away 3–5 days with 3 food and 3 water: back with +25 mood and maybe a find, 8% never come back; refused: −12) and **two survivors grown close** (a corner of their own for 3 scrap: both +15; a party for 4 food; leave them to it; either way they are partners now), and **remembering the dead** after a death nobody has marked (a memorial service, a few words, no time for it). Quarrels and couples follow relationships (see *Friends, rivals and grief*). The autopilot scores each choice.
- **Farming, water and food** (`src/sim/farming.js`, `src/sim/economy.js`): crops do not give food every day. Each base has crop plots (hub 6, outpost 2, +2 per Farm plot building up to 10, +2 sheltered plots per Greenhouse). A crop takes its days to ripen, slowed by the weather (`growthFactor`: cold, dry soil, snow; nothing out of season), and is harvested in one go: yield = base yield × care × ground (farmland 1.3, forest 0.9, hills 0.85), where care averages, over the days the crop stood, tending (farmers per planted plot: 0.5 → ×1.0, 1 → ×1.23, 2 → ×1.4) × skill (Farming of the tending farmers: 0 → ×0.75, 7 → ×1.0, 20 → ×1.45). Crops: potatoes 28 days / 65 food per plot / seed 4 / water 0.2 (spring–autumn), beans 18 / 36 / 3 / 0.2 (spring–summer, tender), corn 32 / 80 / 3 / 0.35 (spring–summer, tender, thirsty, storms flatten it), wheat 42 / 72 / 4 / 0.15 (spring–autumn, hardy), squash 24 / 52 / 3 / 0.3 (summer–autumn), greens 12 / 19 / 2 / 0.2 (spring–autumn, wilts in heat), winter roots 30 / 40 / 3 / 0.15 (autumn sowing that overwinters and ripens early in spring), and three winter crops, each with a catch: **kale** 16 / 22 / 2 / 0.15 (frost- and snow-proof, stands ripe for 24 days, but small and it hardly grows in the cold: sow it in the first half of autumn), **winter wheat** 40 / 88 / 6 / 0.1 (sown in autumn only, sleeps under the snow and ripens in late spring: the biggest grain harvest, but it ties up the plot for months, costs the most seed and comes in after the worst of the hungry gap), **dry beans** 32 / 40 / 4 / 0.25 (twice as slow as green beans, thirstier and tender, but the harvest keeps). Wheat, winter wheat and dry beans go into the *preserved* stock when harvested (threshed grain and dry beans keep a year and need no cellar room). Crop climates are registered with weather.js (`registerCrop`). *Planning by the climate:* farmers judge a crop by the usual weather for the time of year (`climateTable`: average growth, kill chance and food need per day of the year over 12 years of other seeds, never the real days ahead or the play RNG): `cropForecast` gives the days to ripen and the chance it lives that long, so tender crops are no longer sown into the first frosts; food that comes in during the hungry gap counts more (1.6× in its first half, 1.25× after), fresh food grown for the winter store counts what will be left of it by midwinter, and each plot already growing the same crop marks it down 15% (a mix beats one big bet). Nothing new is sown while the base cannot water what is in the ground, and seed comes out of the fresh food, then the preserved stock. Frost, hard freezes, blizzards, storms, heat and (on unwatered plots) drought can kill a standing crop (`cropDamage`); wet weather rots a ripe one, and a ripe crop not harvested within its window (3–14 days) is lost. Farmers harvest 3 ripe plots and sow 2 a day each (planting costs seed food, never the last two days' food), replanting by themselves with the best crop for the season, or what you picked for the plot (*Replant automatically* toggle). The Farming tab lists the plots (crop, growth bar, days to harvest, expected yield, weather effect and warnings, Plant / Harvest / Clear, next crop), the food and water outlook (food in store as fresh + preserved, the storage line, spoilage, the **winter outlook**) and a crop guide (with where the harvest goes and *Sow now*: days to ripen and the chance it survives if sown today). **Food storage** (`src/sim/storage.js`, replacing the old flat spoilage): every base has room for fresh food: the hub a small underground cellar (200), an outpost a pantry (40); a Root cellar adds 200 (up to 2), a Storehouse 150 (dry goods; raiders still steal half as much), a Cold store 150 (an ice house). Fresh food fills the best storage of the day first and spoils slowly there: cellar 0.5% a day (0.3% in the cold, more in heat), storehouse 0.8%, cold store 0.1–0.2% in winter, 0.5% in spring and autumn, 1.2% in summer. Food above the room is *stored outside* and spoils at 3% a day (×1.6 in heat, ×1.4 in rain, half in the cold, 0.3× frozen); the log warns when food starts spoiling outside (outposts after three days, as they ship their surplus daily). **Preserved food** (new supply key `preserved`: jarred, dried, canned, grain and dry beans; value 4, weight 0.6) needs no room and loses 0.1% a day; eating takes fresh food first and preserved only once the fresh is gone; teams can pack it as rations (lighter) and eat it after their packed food; it is a good trade good (groups price it like any supply, with a reference of 2 a head). It comes from the **Preserving kitchen** (18 scrap, 1 part; a workshop station: an operator jars 9 food + 3 water + 1 scrap + 4 empty jars into 8 preserved a day, 9 with Farming 8+), **Drying racks** (6 scrap, up to 3, no operator: 4 surplus food into 3 preserved a day, half on wet days, taking food stored outside first, else only food above 15 days of eating), dry crops, loot (supermarkets, groceries, ranches, cabins, lodges, truck stops) and trade. **Jars** (supply key `jars`, value 0.5, weight 0.4, kept out of the top bar) are found in kitchens, shops and farms (towns, farms, feed stores, diners, churches, groceries…, by the box); a jar holds 2 rations and 85% come back when jarred food is eaten at a base. The base panel shows *Food storage*: "in cellar X/Y, outside Z (spoiling N/day)" plus the preserved stock and empty / filled jars; the top bar's food tooltip and the Farming tab show the same. Water: everyone living at a base drinks 1 a day (1.5 on hot days), crops are watered on dry days, people first. The hub's well gives 12 a day, an outpost's spring 3, a base by a river or lake +3 (less in a dry spell, `wellFactor`); a Well building +6, a Rain catcher +2 per unit of rain (`rainWater`), a water filter +3; the well and rain fill a base up to its capacity (hub 80, outpost 40, +150 per Cistern; water brought home is kept). A base that runs dry goes thirsty: everyone loses 10, 14, 18 … up to 25 health a day (scaled by how short it is; armor does not help) and nobody heals. Outposts keep a week of water. Picking around a base (Scavenge role) usually finds nothing: about a 7% chance a day of a small find (better with Scavenging and a crowbar, less as the surroundings get picked over: 0.4% less per scavenger a day down to 20%, and it never comes back); expeditions are where supplies come from. Fuel is scarce: gas stations, truck stops, the airport and military bases are the real sources (little elsewhere, in raid containers, events and other groups' stock); the hub starts with 12. Start stock: 80 food (was 60) plus 10 preserved and 12 jars, 50 water (was 40).
- **Hand crafting and makeshift gear:** the base's Crafting tab has a craft queue like construction: materials are paid when queued, people on the Craft role put in work-hours (1 + 3% per skill point each) on the first job they are skilled enough for, and finished gear lands in the armory (equip it in the Armory tab). No station needed; sturdier recipes (scrap crossbow, pipe shotgun, scrap plate vest) need a workshop (ammo bench or machine shop) at that base. Makeshift weapons (spiked bat, pipe spear, saw-blade machete, silent ammo-free crossbow, pipe shotgun), armor (magazine arm guards, duct-tape shin guards, tire-rubber vest, scrap plate vest; football pads and the football helmet are found, not made) and tools (bandage and splint kit: +2 Medicine when treating, at a base, in the field or in battle; crowbar and lockpicks: +2 Scavenging at a base; charcoal water filter: +3 water a day at its base; improvised backpack) are weaker than manufactured gear and wear out: each fight, treatment or day of work uses one point of durability and the item breaks at 0. Boiled-rag dressings make meds by hand. Condition comes from the best crafter's skill (+10% for workshop recipes). A few makeshift items show up as loot (houses, garages, campsites, factories, towns, raiders). Teams can make the simplest recipes in the field (`fieldCraftOptions` / `fieldCraft` in `crafting.js`).
- **Production orders (`src/sim/production.js`, controls in `src/ui/production.js`):** every workshop station (Workshops tab, *Order* column) and the drying racks take a standing order: *Forever* (the default, as before), *Until stock reaches N* (stops once the base holds N of its product, starts again when it falls below N − 10%, at least 1, so the operator does not flip every day), *Keep at least N of an input* (pick the input: the station stops when a day's work would take it below N) or *Off*. While an order holds a station its operator idles there, or does the fallback picked on the order (*When it stops*: farm, scavenge, build, guard, craft, rest, medic, or run another free station) and comes back by itself when the order wants work again; pinned people idle. An idle station makes no workshop Heat. The fallback is a hand rule: where the autopilot or a steward (Roles) sets roles, it staffs only the stations whose orders want work. **Standing craft orders** (Crafting tab): *keep N of an item in the armory, at a minimum quality*; every hour an order short of N queues one job (materials paid then; one at a time per order). Quality: only items at the minimum or better count, a job is only queued when someone at the base would make it at the minimum (expected condition from skill, `craftQuality`), and the job only moves while a crafter on the Craft role reaches it, so a low-skill crafter never makes items that would not count. Supplies (dressings) count the stock.
- **Power (`src/sim/power.js`):** a **Generator** (20 scrap, 4 parts, 30 h, Mechanics 3+ to build, up to 2) is a workshop station: with an operator it burns 2 fuel a day for 6 power, only while something draws power (as many generators run as the demand needs; the day's fuel is burned when it starts and lasts to the next midnight), and a running one adds 4 Heat to the base. Powered (need, served in this order while the supply lasts; each can be switched off in the Workshops tab's Power card): Cold store 1 (a freezer: 0.2% a day in any season instead of 1.2% in summer), Radio station 1 (+2 tiles of coverage), Floodlights 1 (no fuel burned when a horde attacks), Machine shop 2 (2 parts a day from 9 scrap instead of 1 from 6), Preserving kitchen 1 (half as much again: 14 food + 5 water + 2 scrap + 6 jars → 12 preserved). Powered stations draw only while their operator works them. The **Ethanol still** (15 scrap, 2 parts, 24 h) is a station too: an operator turns 8 food + 2 water into 3 fuel a day, up to 4 of the food being yesterday's spoiled food (the day's spoilage is kept as mash); give it a Keep-at-least-N-food order. The Workshops tab's Power card and the Buildings tab show supply / demand, generators running, fuel a day and Heat. The governor builds a still (hub of 9+ with surplus food) and a generator (hub of 10+ with a still or 40 fuel, and a machine shop or cold store), runs the still on the surplus only and the generator above its fuel floor (10, Cautious: its fuel reserve).
- **Stewards (`src/sim/steward.js`, Overview tab):** *Hand to a steward* gives one base to the governor's logic (`autopilot.js` `governBase` / `governRoles`, the same per-base code the autopilot runs every hour) in a style you pick (Cautious / Balanced / Aggressive), for the areas you tick (all on by default): *Roles*, *Building*, *Crafting & production orders* (armory gear, crafts, the stations' orders: your own orders stand), *Medical* (treatment with base meds, medics, the meds reserve), *Farming & food* (farmers, plots, stores, water) and *Defence* (guards, lookout, walls and defences). With an area off, the steward leaves its roles as they are (farm = Farming & food, medic = Medical, guard = Defence, operate and craft = Crafting & production, build = Building) and does not build its buildings. **Pin** someone (Overview people list) and no steward (nor the autopilot) changes their role, station or gear. You keep the teams, trips, cards and the other bases. Each steward writes one 🧭 log line a day at 20:00 (role changes, buildings and crafts queued, treatments, gear handed out, orders set, the roles now). *Take over* switches it off and nothing is undone. While the dev autopilot is on it runs the whole holdout and stewards wait.
- **Inventory (paper doll):** every survivor has Hands (main weapon), Off hand (a tool in use: med kit, radio, goggles, crowbar), Head, Body, Arms, Legs and Backpack slots, 4 pockets for small things (knives, pistols, kits, radios, goggles) and, with a pack on, pack slots (improvised backpack 6, assault pack 8; packs also add team carry space, +6 / +10). Armor from every worn piece adds up (cap 5.5; plate carrier alone is 4) and scales with the piece's condition (at 60% it gives 80% of its armor): Kevlar vest or plate on the body, motorcycle / riot / football helmet on the head, magazine arm guards, knee guards or duct-tape shin guards. Items in pockets and packs do nothing until moved to a hand, and do not use the team's carry space; items in a team's cargo take 3 each (a pack counts its contents). Supplies (food, ammo, meds, scrap…) stay pooled per team or base, not per person. Open it from the survivor screen (People tab), the base Armory tab, or a member card in the team panel: click an item then a slot (or drag it), click it twice to send it across, a full slot swaps. At a base items move between the people there and the armory; on a team between teammates and the cargo. `s.gear.weapon/armor/tool` keep their meaning, so combat, medical and crafting read them as before (`src/sim/inventory.js`, `src/ui/inventory.js`).
- **Looting bodies:** after a won battle, dead enemies carry what they fought with (in worn condition), their armor, ammo, sometimes food, meds, scrap, a pack or a spare piece of kit; zombies now and then have something in a pocket; your fallen keep their own things. The result screen offers *Loot the bodies* (bodies list, each body's paper doll next to your survivor and the team's cargo; take items one by one, supply stacks with a click, *Take all* per body or *Take everything*, limited by carry space), *Take all and continue* (Enter) or *Leave the rest and continue*. Whatever is left stays where they fell: a death site on that tile that scavengers strip over the days (a recovery mission can fetch it); your fallen's things are at their death site anyway, and taking them from the body takes them off the site. Base defenses put everything in the armory. Headless and auto-combat battles take everything that fits straight away. After a fight on the map the after-fight card adds nothing of its own: it lists what was taken from the bodies. A fight resolved by the numbers has no bodies, so its haul is rolled on the card, and a bonus item only comes along if there is carry space.
- **Outposts:** found satellites with 15 scrap; a new outpost is named after its site or the nearest known place ("North of Dunmore Outpost") and any base can be renamed on its screen (click the name); production by terrain; supply line strength and backup ETA from distance, hostile land and hordes. Each night an outpost ships its surplus to the hub and keeps a reserve (30 scrap to save for a building); the hub covers its food shortfall. An outpost with no land route to the hub is cut off: nothing moves either way and it lives off its own stock (logged each day). When an outpost is lost, its people flee to the hub, vehicles parked there stay on the tile as abandoned vehicles (marked on the map), supply runs to or from it are paused, and the log lists what went with it.
- **Conflicts on the road:** near hostile land, busy roads and infected ground a team can run into people (or a surge of the dead). A perception roll (Scouting, Keen Eye, stealthy stance) decides who sees whom first. *Forced* conflicts (ambush, roadblock, zealot patrol, checkpoint, challenge, a contested site, surge): run, hide, talk, bribe or pay the toll, invoke a pact, surrender, or fight. *Observed* conflicts (a camp, a robbery, two groups fighting, survivors swarmed by the dead, prisoners on the move): watch for intel, slip away, steal, ambush, talk, or take a side. The approach panel shows who they are, estimated numbers, your team and odds for every option. Fights go through the tactical combat entry point (`combat.js`). Surrender costs what its terms say (traders and communes take a fifth of the supplies and leave gear alone). A payment the team can no longer afford brings the card back. A halted team always has something that will release it (a card, a battle, a search, a trade visit); if not, it checks in for orders within two hours, in or out of radio range. Roughly 0–2 conflicts on a 3–5 day trip.
- **Captives:** surrender, lost fights and failed rescues can leave people held at a group's base (People tab: "Held by …"). Get them back with a Rescue mission (sneak in, storm the base, negotiate, ransom), a ransom during a trade visit, or goodwill (friendly groups let them go). Left too long they may escape, change sides, or (with zealots) die.
- **Infestation hives (`src/sim/hives.js`, numbers in `hiveconst.js`):** the hives on the map at the start (5–6 on a Normal map: four around the outbreak origin, one around each other hot spot; Large and Huge maps add outbreaks of 3 at landmarks, see *World sizes*) begin with 5 resources each and gain more every day by cluster: a hive with n hives (itself included) within 5 tiles gets 1, 3, 6, 8, 10 or 12 a day (n(n+1)/2 up to three, then +2 per hive, capped at 12; stores up to 80). They spend them on hordes: small 10–25 of the dead for 3, medium 50–80 for 8, large 100–200 for 15; and for 40 (from day 200, one at a time per hive and at most one every 90 days) on a **Wallbreaker**, a hive boss that leads a horde against a base. No hordes exist at the start. Each hive first keeps a guard horde at home (rebuilt below 40). Every 120–180 days a site for a new hive is picked on open ground 4–9 tiles from the nearest hive, away from people (one at a time; on a bigger map more of them, and the days between sites and between border hordes divided by its area: a Huge map picks a site every 49–73 days, up to two at once, and gets a border horde every 2–4 days); hives within 10 tiles of it are *supporters* and send up to two hordes each until enough of the dead are gathered or on the way, the rest are *defenders* that send hordes out to roam near home (1 + half their cluster size at a time, and only while the state holds fewer free dead than 1,000 + 250 per hive and their surroundings fewer than 600; medium ones only before day 60, then large ones too) and, more often in the first two months (20% of new hordes at first, 40% on day 30, 65% from about day 70, always with a Wallbreaker), to attack a base: yours by your numbers and distance (weighed 2.5× a group's, from up to 45 tiles), or a group's by its size (within 22). While the hives near a build site feed it, they send out fewer hordes: a lull you can use. Hordes reaching a site gather into one mass; once the hordes delivered add up to a **score of 30** the mass starts turning into a hive (a quarter of it, at least 25 and at most 40, a day), and the hive stands when **1,000 of the dead** have gone into it (up to 150 left over stay as its guard): about four weeks from the first gathering, the window to strike the hives that feed it or thin the mass. Wandering hordes within 6 tiles of a site that still needs the dead are drawn to it. Every 5–9 days a horde of random size (half small, a third medium, the rest large, a little bigger as the year goes on) comes in from off the map on a border land tile at least 6 tiles from your bases, so destroying every hive makes the state safer but never safe. A hive holds the land within 4 tiles: sites there show at least 3 danger skulls, red on the ring 1 tile around the hive, where every one of the dead met (encounters, raids on its sites, culls, horde cards) is much tougher (+3 power; +1 elsewhere in its land). It radiates infection (1 on its tile, 0.1 less per tile out to 6); tiles move toward that or their own level by 0.02 a day up and 0.01 down, and when a hive is destroyed the land within 7 tiles settles toward a low level over the following weeks (the overlay and the hotzone counts follow). **Destroy hive** (mission on a hive tile, also right-click): a card with the hive's guard, then a tactical battle against its brood (10–18 of the dead at power 5+ on its red-skull ground) and the **Broodmother** (300 health), around the hive's flesh mound; win and the hive is destroyed. Or set fires around it (6 fuel) to burn part of its guard and stores. **Destroy every hive with none being built and you win**: a victory screen with *Watch the timelapse*, *Keep playing* and *New campaign* (`world.js checkGameOver`).
- **Jobs for other groups (`src/sim/jobs.js`, Groups tab and the Diplomacy window in `src/ui/diplomacy.js`):** known groups that are not hostile post jobs over the radio (in reach: 6% a day, traders and communes 8%) or at their gates (your team there: 60%, once every 3 days per group); at most one open offer and two jobs under way per group, six in all. An offer is a card (Accept / Maybe later / Decline) and stays on the Jobs list for 4 days (declining or leaving it to expire costs 1 goodwill). Types, each checked by the sim every hour: *clear a horde* within 6 tiles of them (gone or under 40% with your team having come within 1 tile; 12 days), *escort their caravan* to another group (it sets out when your team is at their base, raiders leave it alone while your team is within 2 tiles, done if your team was within 2 tiles for half its road hours; 10 days), *deliver* N of what they want (meds after sickness, food, ammo at war): a team at their base hands it over, or *Ask them to fetch it* and their caravan takes it from your trade base (10 days), *rescue their people* held by a war enemy or raiders (a card at the holders' gates: storm 60% of their guards, talk them out, or pay 40 a head; 14 days), *storm a hive* within 8 tiles of them with their fighters beside you (20 days), *scout a region* (70% of the tiles within 2 of a spot reported after the job; 12 days), *retrieve a package* from a site, a crash site if there is one (picked up by a team on the site, carried to their base or brought home for their caravan; a team lost with it drops it; 14 days). Rewards: 6 + 4 × size goodwill and one or two of supplies from their surplus (about 40 × size), gear you cannot craft, a manual or blueprint you lack (`blueprints.js blueprintReward`), 1–2 recruits (a card), intel (their region, hives and hordes near them) or, for a big job from a group with two or more bases, one of their outposts with two of their people as staff. Missing a deadline: −2 goodwill (small jobs) or −4; a job that can no longer be done through nobody's fault is called off at no cost. Accepted jobs show on the map (a gold *Job* tag, also in the tile tooltip) and in the planner (job targets first in *Move to a site*, a note when the trip's target is a job; the Jobs list's *Plan a trip…* opens the planner on it).
- **Groups and each other (`src/sim/diplomacy.js`, Diplomacy window → World affairs):** a relations matrix between the groups (−100..100: from their types and how close their bases are, drifting 2% a day back toward that; raids outside a war −12; a shared enemy +0.4 a day). Pacts at 28+ (5% a day, broken below 10): pact partners never raid each other and may join each other's wars. Wars at −42 or worse between groups within 18 tiles (1% a day × temper: raiders 1.5 … traders 0.1; three wars at most; 90 days of quiet after a peace) over land, resources or hive ground. Each side raids the other (6% a day, aggressive 9%): sieges as usual (you can answer their call for help), odds att / (att + defence), a win kills 5–10% (never below 8), loots 10%, may take a secondary base (35%) and a captive. Peace when a side is worn out, down to 12 people or after a long war: a lead of 5+ cedes a base (land), 2+ pays 25% tribute, else a white peace; captives go home. News comes over the radio (📻 in the log; the Affairs tab keeps it). A group at war pays 35% more for ammo and meds and 30% more for weapons and armor (both ways) and keeps twice its reserve of food, ammo, meds and fuel; arming one side costs 1 goodwill a deal with the other (3 a day at most). You can stay out, *broker a ceasefire* (both in radio reach, neither hostile, once a week: 20% + 3.5% a Speech point + their goodwill / 400 + weariness / 200; success +8 with both and a thank-you, failure −3), or *join a war* (from the Affairs tab or the card a friend sends when its war starts): the enemy turns hostile (−60) and its war raids come for your bases too, your side +10 and 25% stronger; if your side wins you get half the tribute, or the land it cedes is left free for your outpost for 60 days; afterwards the enemy settles at −35. *Leave the war* costs 8 goodwill with your side.
- **The Calder Coalition (`src/sim/alliance.js`, Diplomacy window → Coalition & goals):** invite a group (Groups tab, radio panel or the Coalition tab) when it is in radio reach or your team is at its gates, its goodwill is 50+, it has a pact with you and you did a job for it (or the council votes it in: members with relations 10+ toward it, a majority). Qualifying groups also ask to join (a card). Members share every horde within 12 tiles and every hive and hive site within 15 of their bases (and the land within 3) each day, send fighters against raids on your bases within 20 tiles (strength × 0.15 each, up to half your own defence), harry a horde heading for your base once (15% each, 30% at most), and send fighters to your hive assaults within 15 tiles (1 per 10 people, 1–3 each; each keeps one of the brood off your squad, 40% at most); they never raid you or each other and do not go to war with each other. A member leaves below goodwill 20, without a pact, or when it attacks another member. The council meets at the start of every season (days 15, 43, 71, …) and asks for supplies for a member (send: +6 / +3 to the rest; refuse: −4 with all), a job (refuse: −3 with all) or a ruling between two members (side with one: +6 / −8 and they fall out; or a compromise, a Speech check).
- **Alliance victory and goals:** the coalition wins when it holds more than half the land (land tiles within 6 of one of your bases or a member's base), every group still alive is a member, and no hive stands or is being built. Since that includes the hives' end, the victory screen then names the coalition's victory instead of the hive victory; after *Keep playing* on a hive victory, reaching the coalition's conditions later brings its own victory screen (once). The Goals card at the top of the Groups tab (and in full on the Coalition tab) shows both: hives destroyed and left (and being built); land held against 50%, members of groups alive, hives left. The timelapse captions the coalition's victory.
- **Wants and surplus, the market board (`src/sim/trade.js` marketLists, Diplomacy window → Market board):** every group's lists are made again every 7 days from its real stock, kind and state (`demand`: a group at war wants ammo, a sick one meds): *wants* (demand 1.25+: the quantity back to comfortable, at least half a usual lot, and what they pay per unit for a usual lot, before Speech) and *surplus* (demand 0.9 or less with 5+ to spare: the quantity and what they charge; never what their kind refuses). Shown on each group's card in the Groups tab and in its radio panel (with arrows against last week). The market board lists every group you know in radio reach (not hostile) for a good you pick, best to sell to or best to buy from first, with arrows, what they want and have spare, and a shortcut to their radio panel. Shortages hit at random (1% a day per group: a lost harvest, sickness, a fuel fire, a lost ammo store; news on the radio) and the lists are made again at once. Their radio offers and requests and the caravans you ask for use these lists.
- **Autopilot and diplomacy:** the governor accepts jobs it can do (deliveries the hub can spare, fetched by their caravan; scouting and packages within 12 tiles with 4+ people at home, sent as recon trips; Aggressive also culls a small horde), keeps in touch with every group it knows in radio reach (a greeting whenever one is due, a scan of the airwaves once a week) and signs the pact each will sign (non-aggression, then trade), builds a radio station from a dozen people (once it has the parts) and a relay at an outpost that would bring a known group into reach, sends envoys to groups out of reach (two people with the hub's surplus as gifts while goodwill is short, a pact, and the invitation at their gates once they qualify; one per group every 20 days), welcomes groups that ask to join and invites every group that qualifies, stays out of wars on Cautious (Balanced and Aggressive join only beside a much stronger friend against a group that is no friend of yours), and on Aggressive sells ammo over the radio to groups at war (once every 8 days).
- **Hordes (`src/sim/hordes.js`):** each has a job (guard, build, mass, roam, attack, border) and a **score** from its size (size^0.8 / 3.4: about 3 small, 8 medium, 16 large, 33 at 400) that sets how hard it is to hide from and how a fight with it goes (horde cards, culls) and what it adds to a new hive. A horde moves at most **2 tiles a day** (12 hours a tile, slower in deep cold and snow: weather.js `hordeSpeedFactor`), along a land path (rivers at bridges). Hordes on the same errand that meet merge (up to 600; the rest stays a horde of its own), very big wandering ones split now and then, and a split always leaves at least 10 in each part. Wandering hordes rot slowly (1.2% a day) and drift toward people (more often as the year goes on). You see hordes only near your bases (further with a watchtower), with teams in radio range, through a team's report (recon, encounters) or a trader's sightings; where you saw one last stays as a faded "N?" ghost. Cull or lure them away; a horde cut below 5 is gone from the map.
- **Defences against hordes (`src/sim/defences.js`):** a base holds back a horde up to its **hold limit**: 15 + 18 × wall strength (hub 69 / 123 / 177 / 231 for wall tiers 0–3, outposts 33 / 87 / 141 / 195), plus 2 per point of combat power on guard duty (everyone at 0.8 for two days after *Man the defences*), times the defence buildings: **barricades** (8 scrap, 10 h, up to 3: +12% each, full cover on the battle map; one is wrecked in each break-in fight), **spike pits** (6 scrap, 8 h, up to 2, one use: the next horde loses 7% + 4 per pit before the walls, and the pits hurt the dead on the battle map), **trench and stakes** (12 scrap, 30 h: +25%, and the dead that reach it lose their turn), **reinforced gate** (14 scrap + 1 part, 20 h: +20% and two more barricades in battle), **floodlights** (8 scrap + 2 parts, 12 h: burns 2 fuel per attack for +10%, and defenders aim +10 at night, +4 by day). A **Wallbreaker** cuts the limit to 60% (two: 35%) and smashes a defence each attack (a barricade, then the gate, else a wall section 40% of the time). A horde at or under the limit is beaten off at the walls (it falls back 4 tiles and is not drawn back to that base for 10 days); a bigger one **breaks in**: the walls still hold back half the limit and the rest (up to 20 on the map, power 1.5 + their number / (5 × units), up to 6, tougher on a hive's land, plus the Wallbreaker) is fought on the base's battle map by the best 8 there, with the barricades, pits and trench on it. In the browser that is the tactical battle; headless and on autopilot the same battle is played out by the AI on both sides, so the odds match. Lose it and the base is **overrun**: the downed have a better chance at home (40%), everyone else there has a 40% chance of a 20–60 wound, a third of the food, water and meds is spoiled, an outpost is lost; a hub with nobody left alive ends the game. The base panel shows "Holds a horde of up to ~N (M with a Wallbreaker)". When lookouts see a horde heading for a base within 6 tiles you get a **warning card** with the same figure: *Man the defences*, *Burn a decoy fire* (6 fuel; 25–80% by its size, 15% with a Wallbreaker) or hold.

- **Seasons and weather** (`src/sim/weather.js`): a year is 4 seasons of 28 days (112 days) and a run starts on day 15 of spring. Every day has one statewide weather (clear, overcast, rain, storm, fog, heat wave, dry spell, snow, blizzard, hard frost) drawn by season from the seed on its own random stream, with warm and cold spells that last a few days, plus ground that carries over: mud after rain and thaw, snow cover that builds up and melts, soil moisture. Averages: spring 13 / 3 °C, summer 26 / 15 °C, autumn 13 / 4 °C, winter 1 / -8 °C. *Travel:* every step's cost is multiplied by the weather of the hour it is taken in: mud slows off-road travel (vehicles about twice as much as walkers), snow cover slows everything (off-road most), storms and blizzards slow everyone a lot (up to ×3) and can hurt someone on the road (1.5% / 2.5% an hour on foot, half in a vehicle), fog and blizzards cut sight by a tile and make it easier to be ambushed, storms bring more road events, and boat mishaps are 1.5–2.5× as likely in wind and storms. The whole year's weather is fixed ahead (rolled day by day from the seed as far as anything asks), so the planner, panels, queue and the map's estimated position use exactly the hours the team will live through, however long the trip; the planner's ETA says how many hours the weather adds. *Forecast:* hover the season and weather next to the clock for the effects and the next 3 days (4 with a watchtower, 5 with a radio station), less sure the further out. *Farming hooks:* growth factor (0–1.3) per crop and day, death chances (frost, hard freeze, blizzard, drought, heat, storm) and rot (wet or snow), growing seasons per crop, rain water per collector and a well factor; also cold / heat exposure risk, a horde speed factor in deep cold and a food-need factor (used by the farming, sickness and horde systems). The *Weather* map layer washes the land white with snow, dark with rain, grey with fog and warm in heat (one fill per frame); the timelapse shows the season by the date and has the same layer. Season changes and severe weather are logged. The autopilot launches no trip on a storm or blizzard day, and no trip longer than a day with one forecast in the next two.
- **Autopilot (dev tool):** the dev panel's *Autopilot* checkbox (or P) hands the holdout to an AI governor (`src/sim/autopilot.js`) so you can watch it play itself; turn it off and you are back in control with everything as it stands. It runs from the hourly tick (`autopilotHour`, daily planning at 07:00 in `autopilotDaily`) and only uses the same calls as the UI and what you know of the map (knowledge, markers, known groups). While it is on, battles resolve by the numbers (auto-combat; restored when you switch off, and a battle already open is played out by the AI), scavenging uses the hour-by-hour search, and cards, searches and trades never stop the clock. Every move is in the log with 🤖 (role changes as one line a day). What it does:
  - *Roles* every hour, per base: patients and the worn-out rest (up to a third), farmers for the plots in use (more when food is short of the food planner's target; the plots get their tenders first while the winter stores fall short; extra plots only while there are hands to tend them), an operator for the preserving kitchen when fresh food would otherwise spoil (stored outside, or the summer and autumn surplus), whoever is left farms too while food is short (base scavenging is a last resort), guards against a spotted raid or a horde at the door (and a standing watch against known raiders or hordes in sight), medics while anyone is hurt, builders while something is queued, operators for stations worth running (ammo, meds, parts, by stock), one crafter while the craft queue has work, the rest scavenge.
  - *Buildings* through `canBuild` / `queueBuild`, saving scrap for what matters: rain catchers and wells when water runs short (cisterns when it overflows), bunkhouses when beds run short, farm plots, food storage before it is needed (root cellars, then a cold store or a storehouse, as soon as the fresh food plus what the fields still bring would fill the room; urgent once food is stored outside), a drying rack and a preserving kitchen for the summer surplus, a greenhouse before winter (saved for when the winter stores fall short), walls when threatened (and when a horde in sight would break in), then barricades, spike pits, a trench, the reinforced gate and floodlights once hordes are a known danger, the hospital tier by tier, watchtower, med station, storehouse, machine shop, radio, garage; outposts get a farm plot, beds and walls. *Crafting*: weapons for anyone without a usable one (crossbow, saw-blade machete, spiked bat, pipe spear), armor, dressings when meds are short, a water filter, a crowbar for scavengers, backpacks, pads; armory gear goes to whoever it helps most. *Medicine*: infected and severe wounds treated with base meds right away, others below 50 when stocks allow.
  - *Food and the winter* (the food planner): the governor plans the year with `winterOutlook` (farming.js): day by day from today to the first spring harvest after the coming winter (`hungryGap`: winter starts on day 71 of each year, the first spring harvest is the quickest crop sown on the first day of spring, about four weeks in), everyone at the hub, the outposts and the teams out eats (the usual food need for the time of year, more in the cold), fresh food first, spoiling by the storage there is at the usual temperature; the crops in the ground come in when they should ripen (times the chance they survive) and every plot then gets the crops the farmers would sow on it (`plannedCrop`, at 85%); seed for the spring and a 15% margin for bad luck are kept back. From mid-summer to the first spring harvest its *food target* (days of food the hub should hold) is that need less what the fields and outposts bring, so a short winter plan means more farmers, a higher food need for trips, trades and radio deals, storage and preserving built in time, and **no newcomers** (strangers, refugees, lone survivors on the road) while the plan with them would fall short. Every week in autumn and on the first day of winter it logs a 🤖 *Winter stores* line: food in store (fresh, preserved, cellar room, outside), what the fields should still bring, what the people need to reach the first spring harvest (eating, spoilage, seed, margin) and the verdict.
  - *Trips* (one a day at most, style sets how many teams, how often, how far, how many stay home and how healthy the crew must be): scavenge the best known site for what the hub needs (loot left as it last knew it, danger, infection, hostile land, distance; when fewer than 3 sites within its range are believed to hold 30%+ loot it looks 8 tiles farther out and allows trips 1.6× as long, logged as "the sites nearer home are picked over"), check spotted caches, camps and wrecked cars, recover the dead's gear or a lost vehicle (only with the fuel to start it), a trade visit (sells surplus, buys what is short, proposes pacts; it asks only for what the team can carry home, and every amount is priced as a whole, the way the trade window totals it), a rescue for captives, or found an outpost when the population allows. Teams take the truck when it can make the round trip; searches stop at the style's Heat or hour limit, when someone is hurt, the cargo is full or the site is dry. Done cards: treat the badly hurt with carried meds before a long way home, sometimes search one more site nearby, else go home. Only one search runs at a time: a second team arriving holds at the site until it is free.
  - *Supply runs* to outposts that are short of hands (a volunteer disbands there) or of scrap for a building (crew sized to the load).
  - *Cards*: conflicts are scored from the odds they show (talk, toll, pact, run, hide, fight, ambush, slip away, steal; style weighs fights and raids), travel events by fixed rules (detour, shelter, spare parts, mark hives on the map, take in loners if the food lasts), raid warnings judged against defense on alert (prepare, pay tribute, or fight in person), horde warnings (a decoy fire when the defences would not hold and no Wallbreaker leads it, else man the defences), calls for help, newcomers (every recruit card: strangers, refugees, loners, people met on the road, a lost teenager, a returning deserter) only with food and beds for them (built, queued or one more bunkhouse; Aggressive allows some crowding), else only the best one, else sent to a base with free beds, else supplies and a handshake when the stores are full, else turned away; hub events and warnings before desertion by a score on each choice (`ai`); a new leader by Leadership; anything else falls back to simtest's rule ("Return home", else the first choice).
  - *Reserves* (Cautious; `reserveStatus(state, base)` → per supply `{ have, target, days, short, low, why }`): a target for every supply that the hub should not drop below, from what you can see. Food is the food planner's (fresh + preserved: `foodTarget` and the winter outlook); water 7 days of drinking (10 in summer, at most 90% of the tanks; short while the wells fall behind on a dry day or under 15 days are left); meds a treatment for everyone sick now (+1 per infected wound) plus a third of the people + 2 (a quarter more in autumn and winter); ammo 2–4 break-in battles (about 6 a defender, up to 8 defenders, plus the 3 a head every horde attack burns; one battle more for each threat: a horde in sight, one the defences would not hold, a horde warning, a hive or hive build site known within 12 tiles, two or more attacks so far); fuel a decoy fire, the floodlights and the truck's next trip; scrap 20 + 1.5 a head + 10 per threat level (up to 70) for repairs and defences; parts 3 (+2 with a vehicle). Below target Cautious acts at once, and safely: a trip every 2 days instead of 4, up to twice as far (trade visits too) with a crew of three and a quarter more food and water, aimed at sites whose loot has what is short (low danger first, never risk above 0.8), two more search hours under the same Heat limit, and a recon first when no known site has it (a look around, then the search if it is worth it); a radio station once there are parts for it, a daily scan of the airwaves and one radio deal a day for the supply furthest below target, paid with what is well above its own target (parts, surplus food, ammo); trade visits that also sell surplus parts and food; the ammo bench and the med station staffed before the plots take every hand (from the scrap there is, down to a day's worth when the stock is low), a med station as soon as meds run short, dressings and a water filter; nothing optional built or crafted from scrap, meds, parts or fuel below target, and trips leave the ammo reserve at home. Risk it cannot afford: no trip while a horde in sight would break in, 5 people stay home while one is in sight or the defences are manned, standing guards before extra farmers, no trip of more than half a day with a storm or blizzard forecast while it is out, a second wall tier once hordes are a known danger. The dev panel's 🤖 line says what is short; hover it for the whole table with the reason for each target.
  - *Styles*: **Cautious** (one stealthy team close to home, short searches, more guards, pays tribute, avoids fights; keeps a reserve of every supply and goes for more as soon as one runs short: see *Reserves*), **Balanced** (default), **Aggressive** (more and longer trips, loud searches, raids hostile land, ambushes camps and robbers, takes in more newcomers, expands fast).
  - *The war on the hives* (every style, from day 30 Aggressive / 90 Balanced / 150 Cautious): a squad of six of the best fighters (health 80+, 12 ammo each) goes for a known hive when it can beat the brood it expects there: the squad's storm value (per fighter: hit chance × damage of the gun they can use, as the battle plays it, more with armor, less when hurt; about 2 for a shotgun and a vest at Shooting 6, 1 for a pistol at Shooting 4) over the brood (`hives.js broodForce` from the guard last seen at the mound, 100 if never seen up close, less one for every fighter coalition members and job givers send: `alliance.js hiveAllies`) must reach the style's margin (1.05 / 1.2 / 1.35: at 1.3 a storm costs about one fighter in three, at 1.1 about one, at 0.9 two or three). Fires around the mound (6 fuel each, up to 3, never the fuel for the drive home) thin the guard first when that is what it takes; the team storms on its next orders. Targets: hives near your bases, then those a group asked you to storm (the governor takes a hive job when it would storm that hive anyway), then the nearest. In the endgame (a coalition member, at most 3 hives left, the land short) it founds up to 3 more outposts where they add the most uncovered land.
  - *Water through a drought*: water filters (ahead of anything else in the craft queue) for what the wells would leave short on a dry, hot day; no newcomers the water could not carry through one; water carried to an outpost about to run dry.
- **Saving and loading** (`src/sim/save.js`, storage in `src/ui/saves.js`, the menu in `src/ui/settings.js`): ⚙ Menu in the top bar (or Esc with nothing open, or ⚙ Menu in a battle's panel). A save is the whole world, written as a graph: plain objects and lists as JSON; Maps, Sets, typed arrays (the timelapse frames, base64), the seeded generators (their exact position) and the hex grid as tagged objects; an object reachable from two places (an item worn by a survivor and listed at a death site, the chronicle's shared frame lists, a battle's supply pools that are the team's own supplies, the battle's rng) is written once and referenced after, so the links survive; the chronicle's WeakMaps keep the entries for the hordes and teams still in the world; undefined, NaN, Infinity and −0 keep their values; the id counter (`world.js` `newId`) is saved, so a loaded world hands out the same ids and plays on exactly as the original (`tools/savetest.js`). Battles, raids, the deploy step, searches and trade windows are saved as they are, mid-turn included. **An open event card cannot be saved** (its choices are code, closures over the moment the card was made): Save is disabled with the card's name until it is answered, and the autosave waits for that too. Storage: up to 12 named saves plus the autosave in the browser's storage (`localStorage`, one key a slot plus an index with the day, hour, hub, survivors, seed and a small picture of the map), gzip-compressed through the browser's `CompressionStream` and packed 15 bits a character (a 365-day world: about 1.7 MB of JSON, about 280 KB stored); without `CompressionStream` the JSON is stored as it is. *Export* and *Save to file* download a `.bsave` file (the save's JSON); *Load from file* adds one to the list. The autosave runs at dawn every day (or every 2, 3, 5 or 7 days), when a hive assault starts (the battle is saved before the first shot) and when the run ends (that save leaves any open card out); at dev fast-forward speeds at most once a minute. *Save and exit* saves to the game's slot and goes back to the new-run screen; loading pauses the game. Saves carry a format version (`SAVE_VERSION`): a save from a newer version, or with no version, is refused with a reason; older versions are upgraded by the migrations in `save.js` (none yet: this is format 1).
- **Timelapse:** the world is recorded from day 1 (`src/sim/chronicle.js`): one frame a day (every 6 hours for the first 3 days, plus a frame a few hours after a death or a base gained or lost) with tile owners and infection (stored as changes against the previous frame, a full keyframe every 60 frames), your bases, the groups' bases, hordes (with their Wallbreakers), hives and hive build sites, parties, your population and hub stocks, and captions: your log's deaths, raids, horde attacks, bases founded and lost, plus what the fog hid (groups taking or losing sites, sieges and how they ended, hordes striking a group, big hordes, hives rising and destroyed, collapses). About 0.25 ms per frame and roughly 300 KB for a year. When every survivor is dead (or every hive is destroyed) the game-over or victory screen offers *Watch the timelapse*; the dev panel has *Timelapse so far* (the game pauses, Close returns to it). The viewer (`src/ui/timelapse.js`) fits the whole map with the fog off and draws each frame with the normal map renderer: land growing and shrinking, infection spreading, hordes and teams stepping toward their next position, bases appearing and disappearing, and graves where your bases stood at the end. The **Diary** (J) keeps every log line of the run, day by day (up to 150 a day, the rest counted): a day list with the season, the weather and a one-line summary (deaths, departures, arrivals, fights, harvest, buildings, trades), the chosen day's lines grouped by kind with the leader's short note for the day, the same filters, a search over the whole run, and the same links. - **Skills that grow, teaching, books and perks** (`src/sim/skills.js`, survivor screen in `src/ui/skills.js`): people gain XP in the skills they use (a day on Farm, Build, Craft or Operate 11; Medic 4 plus 3 a wound treatment, 6 a surgery, 2 an illness, 4 a field treatment; Guard 3 Shooting; Scavenge 6; in battle 1 a shot, +2 a hit, +2 a kill, 3 a patch-up, 1 Stealth a move while the enemy is unaware, 2 Scavenging a container; a fight by the numbers 6, +3 for a win; a search hour 3, +2 Stealth sneaking; an hour on the move 0.5 Scouting; looking around 4; a road talk 5, +5 if it works; hiding 5 Stealth; a trade 6, a radio deal 4 Speech; a vehicle repair 5 Mechanics; the leader 3 and each team's best leader 4 Leadership a day). - **Locks, keys and locked rooms** (`src/sim/locks.js`, raid panel in `src/ui/locks.js`): some raid containers are locked, by area (house cabinets 12% lockboxes, locker rooms and barracks 30–35% lockers, pharmacy and clinic cabinets 25–35% drug cabinets, police evidence lockers 30%, armory and gun-counter gun cages 40–45%, office and control-room safes 8–12%, warehouse, hangar and motor-pool crates 12–20%, crash-site cargo cases 25–45%), and a site may have a locked room: every door of one building with containers is a locked door cell (no walking or seeing through) — police and military 60%, pharmacy, gun store and prison 50%, hospital 40%, university and dam 35%, clinic, warehouse, factory, office, airport and a cargo-plane wreck 30%, elsewhere 15–25%. Found, the wreck is a temporary site (Army helicopter wreck: ammo, military gear; supply plane: food, preserved food, meds; medevac: meds, surgical kits; news chopper: parts; supply drop: food, water, meds, ammo) whose raid map has wreckage and cargo cases (many locked) and its own blueprints. It burns out and is gone 7–10 days after it came down. Groups with a base within 22 tiles race you (raiders 75%, the military 85%, expansionists 65%, zealots 50%, fortresses 40%, traders 30%, communes 20%): a salvage party leaves 12–36 hours after the crash and walks there; one that arrives strips it to 30% of what is left, and if your team is on the tile it challenges you (a conflict card). The noise draws up to two hordes within 8 tiles toward it for 3 days. Alerts and the tile tooltip show the circle and the days left.
- **Things break** (`src/sim/breakdowns.js`, the base's **Repairs** tab): the hub's well pump, wells, the generator, the still, the machine shop, the ammo bench, the med station, the preserving kitchen, the radio station, walls, the reinforced gate and the storehouse wear out. Wear a day: a running station 1.6 (generator), 1.2 (still, machine shop), 0.8 (ammo bench, kitchen), 0.6 (med station), idle 0.05–0.15; the pump and wells 0.9, +2.5 on a frost day, +4 in a storm; the radio mast 0.7, +6 in a storm; the storehouse roof 0.1, +8 in a storm, +1 in rain; walls and gate 0.05 a day and 18 / 22 (±30%) per horde attack (raids do their own damage, below); all × (1 + days since built / 240), × (1.15 − 2.5% a Mechanics point of the operator, at least 0.65), × 0.8 with a Grease Monkey or 0.85 with a Handy survivor living there, × 1.6 while jury-rigged. What the holdout starts with is not new (the hub's pump 30–50 wear, the rest 5–25). Builders with no construction job take 1.5 wear a day off the most worn. A fixture breaks at 100, and from 60 up with a daily chance of ((wear − 60) / 40)² × 30% (most break around 80). Broken: a station stops (the Workshops tab says so), a well gives no water, the pump costs the hub 6 water a day, the radio station is off the air, walls lose their +3 wall strength, the gate its horde bonus, the storehouse its room. A card (an alert, and the Repairs list) offers **Repair** (the best free mechanic with at least the job's Mechanics: generator 10 h, 4 scrap, 2 parts, Mechanics 3; radio 10 h, 4 scrap, 2 parts, Mechanics 4; machine shop 8 h, 3 scrap, 1 part; still, pump and wells 6 h, 3 scrap, 1 part; walls 12 h, 10 scrap; gate 8 h, 6 scrap, 1 part; storehouse 6 h, 4 scrap; benches and the kitchen 4 h, 2 scrap; hours × (1.2 − 3% a Mechanics point, at least 40%), faster with Handy or Master Builder; wear back to 8), **Jury-rig** (about a third of the hours, about half the scrap, no parts; wear left at 65 and 60% faster wear until repaired properly) or **Leave it**. Scrap that is short can be made up with parts (1 for 4). Materials are paid when the job starts (cancel: all back); the repairer keeps their role and works it 08:00–20:00 while at the base and well enough; Mechanics XP when done. The autopilot and stewards with Building on start repairs themselves (repair if they can pay, else jury-rig) and replace a repairer who left. The storm "broken well pump" hub event is this system now (the same card). **Raid damage** (`raidDamage`): when a raid on your base is settled (by the numbers, in person, or raiders from outside) it smashes buildings with a budget of wear = the raid's strength × 3 × (the hours the siege lasted / 8, 0.15–1.5) × the outcome (driven off 1, beaten in person 0.5, a break-in 2.5, overrun 4.5), in hits of 30–70 wear: the outer line first (walls, gate, barricades, floodlights, watchtower: 85% of the hits when driven off, 50% in a break-in, 35% when overrun), else the buildings inside; 40% of the hits fall on the building hit last. Buildings that only wear from raids: barricades, floodlights, the watchtower, bunkhouses, cisterns, cellars, the cold store, the garage, cells, the common room, the chapel, drying racks, rain catchers and the relay (repairs 2–8 h, 2–8 scrap, a part for the floodlights, garage, cells and relay). At 100 a building breaks (no card of its own: the raid's result card lists what was wrecked, what is damaged at 60+ and the bill, with *Start the repairs* / *Later*; the autopilot and stewards start them themselves); an overrun base loses one wrecked building for good (60%, never the walls). A damaged wall section (60+) gives 2 wall strength instead of 3; anything at 60+ can be *patched* with a proper repair before it gives way (the autopilot patches defences while it has 20 scrap to spare). No building or repair work goes on at a base on alert or under siege. The Repairs tab shows the last raid's damage and a *Repair all* button with the bill. Parked vehicles lose 1% condition a frost day (2% in a blizzard); the Repairs tab lists them with the usual *Repair (1 part)*.
- **Roster** (`src/ui/roster.js`, R): every survivor in one table: where they are, role, health, mood and the risk of leaving, conditions, the nine skills (with a bar for the progress to the next point), weapon, armor and traits. Click a column to sort (again to reverse), filter by place (a base, on teams, away), role and status (hurt, sick, unhappy, fit, no contact), change a role in its row, or tick rows (or everyone shown) and set one role for all of them (people away keep theirs). A name opens the survivor screen over the table; health and mood stay hidden for people you have no contact with.

The `sim` folder is kept separate from rendering, so the rules could be ported to another engine (for example Godot with C#) almost one-to-one.

`node tools/raidtest.js 10 7` plays raids with an auto-player per site type and reports extraction rate, deaths, loot and vehicles kept (`node tools/raidtest.js 3 7 all` covers every site type; add `kit` to give everyone bottles, a noise maker, a flashlight and the team a suppressor, and `night` to start the raids at 22:00: `node tools/raidtest.js 10 7 x kit night`). The auto-player ties up the knocked out, takes down an unaware guard next to it, stalks a lone guard from behind and throws things to pull guards away; the line under the table counts throws, takedowns, ties and muffled volleys.

### Stealth, prisoners and the deploy step

- **Takedowns and prisoners** (`src/sim/stealth.js`, `prisoners.js`): *Subdue* on an unaware human (or one at 30% health or less) is a takedown that knocks them out: 30% + 2.5 × Stealth, a spiked bat +20, a blade +10, fists or a gun butt +5, from behind (the side they do not face) +15, from cover +10, Brave or Tough +5 each, the Assassin perk +20, soldiers −15, zealots −5, an aware but staggering target −15 (5–95%). A failed takedown is a blunt blow (60% damage, never lethal) and the target cries out. On an aware enemy Subdue is a non-lethal blow. The knocked out wake in 4–6 turns unless tied up (*Tie up*, 1 action). After a won fight they are prisoners (the result screen: *Take them* or *Leave them*, goodwill +2); in a raid a tied prisoner dragged into an extraction zone leaves with whoever extracts there. On the road they walk with the team (on foot 10% slower each, at least 60% of its pace; in a vehicle they ride), eat 1 food and 1 water a day each and slip away 5% a day (2% in a vehicle). At home: *Holding cells* (16 scrap, 1 part; 3 each) and the base's **Prisoners** tab: 1 food and 1 water a day each; escapes 0.4% a day guarded (someone on the Guard role), 3% unguarded, 8% in a shed when the cells are full, +4% unfed; six unfed days and they die. *Question* (every 3 days, Speech) puts their group's bases, people, stock, parties out and the hives near them on your map; *Ask a ransom* (goods worth 15 + 4 × power; paid in 2–4 days at 40% + goodwill / 200, −15% for aggressive groups; goodwill −4 paid, −3 refused); *Trade back* (goodwill +10); *Release* (+4); *Win them over* (1 a day × Speech, Leadership, the base's mood and who they were, zealots 0.35; at 100, about 2–4 months, a recruit card); *Execute* (mood −6 for everyone there, Brave or Tough −2; goodwill −20 with them, −4 with every known group). Their group may come for them: 1.2% a day per prisoner (×1.5 aggressive, ×2 after a refused ransom, ×0.3 friendly; needs 6+ people within 25 tiles); a warning card 10 hours ahead: defend in person (the deploy battle), man the walls (defence ×1.3 by the numbers) or hand them over (goodwill +3). Lost, they free their people and carry off a tenth of the stores.
- **The stealth kit** (`src/sim/stealth.js`): bottles, noise makers and firecrackers (thrown 7–8 tiles, 1 action) pull every unaware guard and the dead within 6 / 8 / 13 tiles to look for 3 turns (a noise maker keeps ringing 3 turns; firecrackers add 6 Heat in a raid). A guard who sees a body or a tied-up comrade within 5 tiles (3 at night) raises the alarm for its group (+6 Heat); *Drag* moves a body, someone knocked out or a downed teammate 3 tiles for 1 action. People see the way they face: from behind a guard only hears you (half the detection range). An *Oil-filter suppressor* (workshop, Mechanics 4) carried by someone with a pistol, hunting rifle or assault rifle halves a shot's noise (Heat and earshot) and wears a shot at a time (12). Night raids (20:00–06:00): enemy sight 7 (the dead 5), detection ×0.6; your people see 7, 9 with a *Flashlight* in the off hand (seen from 3 tiles further), 13 with night vision; searching without a light takes both actions. Recipes: noise maker (2 scrap, field), firecrackers (1 scrap + 8 ammo, field), flashlight (2 scrap, 1 part), suppressor (4 scrap, 1 part, workshop); found in kitchens, houses, stores, garages, barracks, diners, gas stations and hardware stores.
- **Deploy before a break-in** (`src/sim/deploy.js`): a horde breaking in, raiders defended in person and a rescue raid open on the base's map with what was built: with walls, a wall line the defenders shoot over, the gate and the breaches the dead made (1, +1 per Wallbreaker, +1 at 120 and 250 inside; raiders cut 1), barricades behind it, the trench and spike pits in front, and a watchtower's two towers (full cover, +5 aim). The map is turned so the attack comes from the side the horde (or the raiders, or their home) lies on the State Map. Place each defender on a green cell; whoever stands in the gate or a breach holds it (blocks it, and the battle AI keeps them there, as on a tower); floodlights (lit) are aimed at a third of the approach (+12 to hit at night, +5 by day in the light). *Default positions* (also headless and the autopilot): with 7+ defenders the toughest close fighter holds the gate (two with 9+), the best shots take the towers, the rest stand well back so the dead cross open ground (calibrated with hivetest's break-ins).

## Known gaps

- Hives: the autopilot destroys hives only on Aggressive and rarely (its best six seldom reach the combat power it waits for, and it knows few hives); it does not cull the dead gathering at a build site. Base sprites do not show the defence buildings (they show on the battle map). Hives can rise on top of each other's land; a hive build site cannot be attacked directly (cull the mass gathered there). Hordes on the map do not march on your teams' camps; a camp's Heat draws a horde within 3 tiles in as a bigger surge card instead (the horde itself stays where it is).

- Raid balance is untested with real players; the auto-player loses people often.

- Field: the hub's estimated "?" position of an out-of-contact team on a plan follows the way out, waits there for the plan's time and walks back along the same road (it does not trace the nearby searches). The plan watches for a horde, a rich site and low supplies; survivors met on the road come through the usual event and recruit cards. The autopilot launches no plans (it keeps its own Done-card rules); it does not camp, and gives up waiting at a busy site past Heat 60. Orders queued from the team panel after a plan's Return home step are dropped when the team heads home (put them before it). The People tab's base Treat is outside the radio rule (it is a base action).

- Balance is first-pass.
- Saving: a save cannot be made while an event card waits for an answer (answer it first; the autosave waits); Esc does not open the menu in a battle (Esc cancels the battle action there; use ⚙ Menu in the panel). Saves live in the browser's storage for this site (or the desktop app's profile): clearing the site's data deletes them, so Export the ones to keep. The desktop app keeps saves in its browser storage too, not as files in its folder. A save does not keep the interface: open windows, the camera, camera spots, the selected unit and an open card's result screen; the game-over autosave leaves open cards out. Older save formats have no migrations yet (there is only format 1).
- Alerts, diary and roster: the diary's categories come from the wording of each line (a few lines land in a neighbouring category); links are found by name when the line is written, so a team's link only works while the team is out, and a base renamed later keeps its old name in old lines; alerts do not cover teams short of food or water in the field, or vehicles out of fuel; the search box searches when you press Enter (not while typing); camera spots are kept for the run, not between sessions.
- Sounding the retreat is not offered in raids (they keep their extraction zones) or in base-defence battles without an evac strip; there is no smoke on the battle map, and night counts only from the hour the battle started. The AI's retreat only runs in battles it plays on the map (autopilot hive assaults, battles played out headless): fights resolved by the numbers (auto-resolve, the autopilot's other fights) have no retreat outcome. Prisoners are not on the map, cannot be moved between bases or handed to Diplomacy's jobs, and other groups never take your knocked-out people (your captured people are still conflicts.js captives). Enemy guards have no vision cones drawn; the base map has no ramparts (defenders stand behind the wall, not on it).
- Outposts cannot be abandoned by hand.
- Sites running dry: groups know the true loot of sites near them (they live there; they do not keep stale knowledge like you); their scavenging parties find no gear, books or keys, never search land held by you or another group, never go by vehicle and never return a second time in one trip; a party you drive off loses its haul but the site keeps what it already lost; radio news needs a radio station but not the group to be in radio reach; the base's own surroundings (Scavenge role) are a separate number from the site's loot (stripping a site next to a base does not lower it, and vice versa).
- Trade routes cannot swap escorts (end and recreate the route).
- Radio and trade: groups' caravans go to the hub when they can reach it (else your nearest reachable base); you cannot pick the base. Groups do not trade gear with each other. Your trade caravans start from the hub only, and the planner's estimate assumes 6 hours at each stop. A caravan of theirs caught by raiders or a horde on the way just never arrives (a log line only if you can see it). Maps you sell a group do not change what they know or do.
- Captives are not shown on the map (only in the People tab and tile tooltips); convoys on trade routes and supply runs do not get conflict cards (their road trouble is auto-resolved by the escort table).
- Held supply convoys do not help defend the base they are parked at.
- Timelapse: parties are only seen at frame times (a trip shorter than a day can be missed) and move in straight lines between frames, not along roads; death sites, markers and battles themselves are not drawn (deaths and fights show as captions and pings); the recording is part of the save (a loaded game's timelapse starts from day 1).
- Crafting: no lethal throwables (Molotov, pipe bomb) or torches; makeshift gear is not traded.
- Production and power: station orders count only the base's own stock (not the other bases, teams or trade caravans); the drying racks share one order; a craft order counts the armory only (not gear people wear) and has one job in the queue at a time; the hospital, ammo bench, med station and outposts' relays take no power; generators need an operator (no unattended daily check); power is per base (no lines between bases). Stewards do not answer cards (horde or raid warnings, hub events, desertion warnings), plan trips or supply runs; their style is one of the governor's three.
- Weather: one sky for the whole state (no regional weather, no rivers freezing over); arrival estimates use the real weather ahead, so they "know" more than the forecast the player is shown; AI groups move at the same weather-adjusted pace but do not plan around it; convoys on trade routes and supply runs are slowed but not hurt by storms.
- People: hub events come only at the hub (not at outposts); deserters never come back with a raider group, only alone at the gate; a lost teenager is an ordinary survivor with low skills (no children); people walking to a base are not on the map and cannot be met or rescued on the way.
- Relationships and mood: opinion is one number per pair (the same both ways); hub events, crowding and the warning card change mood without a line in the breakdown's *Lately* list except the ones relations.js and morale.js note (grief, breaks, services, rough trips, warnings, stories by the fire); outposts get no hub events, so no memorial event there (the wall's service works at any base); books count as comfort whatever they teach; kills in fights resolved by the numbers are spread over the fighters by strength; there are no children, families or jealousy, and no mood effects from gear, clothes or temperature indoors.
- Economy: crops have no pests, fertility or crop rotation; no salting (no salt source), and storage room is per base (outposts ship their surplus to the hub daily rather than keeping a winter store); the winter outlook plans by the usual weather and the current farmers, so a drought or a lost crop can still catch it out, and it does not count loot or trade; water is never rationed (food is); illness gives no immunity after recovery; vehicle fuel use per tile is unchanged (only fuel income was cut). The Balanced autopilot survives the first month and the food and water pressure of the first winter on most seeds, but the new hordes still break in on many of its year-long runs.
- Autopilot: fights and raids are always resolved by the numbers (never the tactical or raid maps); it does not set up trade routes, cull or lure hordes, build boats (a team cut off by water waits for you), or deliberately scout unknown land (unknown sites are scavenged on estimates). It re-sets roles every hour, so roles you set by hand while it is on are overwritten (except pinned people, and teaching or reading, which it leaves alone); it staffs stations through their standing orders (Until for ammo, meds and parts, so operators no longer flip at the threshold). Stewards run the same per-base code but never answer cards, plan trips or supply runs, or trade: those stay yours. Screens you open yourself (base, planner, survivor, inventory, help, timelapse) still pause the clock. The style resets to Balanced with a new campaign.
- Skills: no XP from hub events, morale cards or mediating quarrels (morale.js and the relations code give none yet); Pathfinder's +10% is not in the planner's estimate before the team leaves (it is once the team is out); Seed Saver is not counted in the autopilot's crop plan; the autopilot teaches and reads only with hands to spare after farming, building and guarding, so it seldom does early on; earned traits ("Bitten and Lived", "Road-Wise") are not in yet.
- Exploration: keys cannot be bought or sold (they live on the key ring, not in cargo), and other groups never use keys; no blowtorch or explosives for safes. Locks are only in raids on the map and the hour-by-hour search (not in conflicts or base fights). Blueprints are global once studied (any base can build), and studying happens on the Read role (no separate "Study" role in the roster's role list). Crash sites: AI salvage parties never fight hordes at the wreck, and a team already raiding the wreck when a salvage party arrives is not interrupted (the group strips what is left). Breakdowns: no greenhouse, rain catcher, cellar or vehicle-part failures; the repairer's role work is not reduced while they repair; the autopilot does not hold scrap back for repairs (short scrap is made up with parts).
- Inventory: sprites draw the weapon and body armor (or arm guards) but not helmets, leg guards or packs; enemies in battle do not wear helmets (soldier and raider bodies can carry one as spare kit); no picking things up from downed units during a battle (loot comes after a win, not after a retreat or defeat); raid maps keep their own body containers (Search); supplies are pooled per team, not carried per person; death sites store leftovers as a flat list, not per body.
- Outsiders: raids on other groups and robberies of caravans are resolved by the numbers (never a battle you watch); outsiders do not appear in conflict-type cards (conflicts.js) but in their own cards; your trade-route and supply convoys are hit by the numbers (no card); merchants never sign pacts or run trade routes; explorers who stay are ordinary recruits; a merchant's trade window says "reopen it from the Bases tab" though it reopens from the Groups tab; the radio call goes out once per caravan.
- Diplomacy: groups' wars are resolved by the numbers (their raids on each other are never a battle you watch unless you answer a call for help), and joining a war adds abstract patrols to your side's score rather than missions against the enemy's bases; a job's escort does not fight beside the caravan (it keeps raiders away; hordes still wear it down); a rescued group's people and a package are not items in your inventory; a job's horde that merges into another before your team arrives is called off; alliance land is a radius around bases (6 tiles), not the territory colors on the map; the coalition victory needs its land and members on top of the hive victory's condition, so it ends a run that would also be a hive victory (it is named instead), or comes later after *Keep playing*; there is no peace treaty between you and a group you fought except through their war's end.

## Desktop app

The game also runs as its own window (Electron), from `desktop/`. Double-click **`Play Broken State.cmd`** in the repo root (the first run installs Electron into `desktop/node_modules`, about 360 MB, once), or from `desktop/`: `npm install` then `npm start`. It always plays the current files in `prototype/`, so game changes need no rebuild (Ctrl+R reloads). The game is served on the fixed origin `app://brokenstate/`, so browser-saved settings (zombie look) stay between launches; the window remembers its size, position, maximized and fullscreen state. Keys: F11 fullscreen, Ctrl+R reload, Ctrl+Shift+I DevTools, Ctrl+= / Ctrl+- / Ctrl+0 zoom (zooming out shows more map and costs frame rate), Alt shows the menu.

- Saves and options live in the app's browser storage (`%APPDATA%\Broken State`), like the zombie look; the menu adds *Save and quit to desktop* and *Quit* (they close the window, which quits the app). *Export* opens a save dialog for the `.bsave` file.
- `npm run dist`: a portable build in `desktop/dist/Broken State-win32-x64/` (`Broken State.exe`, about 330 MB, copy the folder anywhere) with a snapshot of the game; run it again after game changes.
- `npm run perf` (or `"Broken State.exe" --perf`): new game on seed 42, Reveal map + Autopilot at 200×, 60 s each at 1920×1080, maximized, and maximized at 100% scaling; prints frame-time and memory JSON and quits (`--perf-seconds=N`, `--perf-out=file.json`). `npm run perf:browser` runs the same test in the installed Chrome/Edge for comparison.
