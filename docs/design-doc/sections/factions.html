AI survivor groups

The State of Calder is shared with other survivor groups that live without you: they eat, grow, scavenge, expand onto new sites, raid each other and you, go to war, sign pacts and fall apart. There are seven group types, each with its own starting attitude, strength, appetite for land and taste in trade goods. Everything you do with other people (trade, diplomacy, the coalition, conquest) is aimed at these groups.

How many, and where Built

The number of groups is a new-run setting, limited by the world size. Every run has at least one raider gang, one military remnant or fortress hub, and one trading group or farming commune; the rest are random types, shuffled.

  • A group starts on a named site outside the cities (never an urban zone), at least 8 tiles from your hub and 8 tiles from every other group, and within 30 tiles of the hub on a Normal map (scaled by the square root of the area elsewhere). It prefers sites that suit its type (table below).
  • Each group starts with 14–28 people, goodwill toward you of its type's value ± 8, and stores of food (6 a head), ammo (10 a head × its arms factor), fuel (1 a head), meds (1 a head), 2 parts, scrap (3 a head) and preserved food (half a head).
  • Every group has its own map color. 18 colors exist (10 for Normal, 8 more for Large and Huge); none is blue, because blue is yours.
  • Groups start unknown: the map and the log do not name them until you meet them (your team sees them, they call you, you scan the airwaves, they raid you, or they ask for help).

Their parties on the map

Every group type has its own look for its people on the move, tinted in the group's color: raids (weapons up, standing on a red ring; a siege pitches a camp next to the target with crossed swords over it), people walking (convoys on foot), scavengers with sacks, and a vehicle for convoys and caravans (raiders: a spiked pickup; military: a humvee; expansionists: an armored truck; traders: a covered wagon with a mule; commune: a tractor and hay wagon; zealots: a procession cart under a sun banner with torches; fortress: a box truck with a shield crest and sandbags).

Groups scavenge too: the shared, shrinking map Built

Site loot never grows back. Your teams lower it, and so do the groups. This is why a site that looked rich on day 10 may be picked clean by day 60.

  • Parties: a group with 6+ people sends one scavenging party at a time, every 6–10 days, when its biggest need (1 − stock / want; wants per head: 10 food, 20 × arms ammo, 6 scrap, 2 fuel, 2 meds, 1 preserved, parts 3 + 0.2 a head) is 25% or more. The party (strength 12% of the group, at least 2, on foot) walks to the best site within 10 tiles: the site's loot table weighted by need × loot left ÷ (1 + distance / 5). Never a base, hive, crash site, a tile with a horde, land held by you or another group, a site below 0.12 loot, or one another party is working.
  • Search: 8 hours, taking 3% of the loot an hour (× strength / 3, between 0.5 and 1.5) down to a floor of 0.03, at 80% of what your own team would find. Then home, and the haul goes into their stores.
  • Their own land: every site inside a group's land loses 0.3% of its loot a day into their stock (at half efficiency).
  • Losses: walking into a horde costs a quarter of the party's strength and a quarter of its haul; a raider gang ambushes a party carrying a haul within 2 tiles of its base (12% a tile) and takes it; a party below strength 1 is gone.
  • Contested sites: if their scavengers reach a site your team is on, you get a "contested site" conflict card (unless the group is friendly, allied or has a pact with you: then they keep to their side). Win and they leave, dropping their haul into your cargo.
  • What you learn: only what you see. A site stripped since your last look still shows its old loot. When a party takes 0.12+ loot (or drops the site a band) within 15 tiles of one of your bases, the log says so if you can see it, or "On the radio: …" if you have a radio station (once a day at most, a site at most every 10 days); the radio line updates the loot you know there.

The build has the seven types only; each type's numbers (arms, food, expansion, raids, trade tastes, war temper) carry its personality. The 5–10 group count is now the Normal range; the other world sizes have their own.

Relations and attitude

Every group has one goodwill number toward you, from −100 to +100, and the groups have relations with each other on the same scale. Goodwill decides whether a group trades, talks, helps, joins your coalition or raids you; it drifts with what you do and with how close and strong you are.

Note on files: relations.js is about relationships between your own survivors (friends, rivals, partners, grief); it is covered with the people systems. Group attitude lives in factions.js, group-to-group relations in diplomacy.js.

Drift: what moves goodwill on its own Built

  • Pressure from your expansion: a peaceful group (military, fortress, traders, commune) that is not yet hostile loses goodwill every day when a base of yours is within 5 tiles of one of theirs, your strength is more than 0.6 × theirs, and you have not done any diplomacy with them for 10 days: −1.5 a day for the neutral types (military, fortress), −0.7 for the friendly types (traders, commune). Greeting them on the radio, trading, gifts, pacts, jobs and the like all reset the 10-day clock.
  • Cooling off: a peaceful group you provoked into hostility recovers +0.1 a day once no base of yours is within 5 tiles and nothing new has been done against it for 30 days.
  • Trade routes: an active trade route with a group adds +0.3 a day, plus +1.5 each time a convoy is delivered.
  • Bought peace fades: tribute paid to an aggressive group (+6) drains away again at 0.5 a day.
  • Infamy: once your conquest infamy is above 20, every known peaceful group that is not a coalition member loses (infamy − 20) / 200 a day.

Memory: what groups remember Built

  • Grudge: every raid of theirs you beat off (or fight they lose against you) adds a grudge point; each point makes your bases 50% more attractive to their raids (counted up to 4) and their raids razing more likely. Grudges fade 1% a day.
  • Escalation: every raid you drive off makes their next raid 25% bigger, up to 2×; a raid that gets in resets it.
  • Betrayal: attacking their caravan starts a 60-day vendetta: 12% a day for a raid on a base of yours within 24 tiles while they are hostile and have 6+ people, whatever their type.
  • Last offence: any goodwill loss with a reason restarts the 30-day clock before a provoked group starts to cool off.
  • Tribute: how much bought goodwill is still fading; prisoners you hold; when they were last raided or lost a base (both raise their prices for weapons and defences).
  • Your survivors remember too: friends of someone a group killed want revenge: +5 aim against that group for 60 days, and +6 mood when they win a fight against it (see People).

Calls for help Built

  • When a peaceful group that is not hostile to you is besieged (by another group, a horde or outside raiders) and one of your bases is within 7 tiles of the base under attack, a call comes: at once over the radio if a radio base reaches within 4 tiles of them, otherwise by a runner after max(4, 2 × distance from your hub) hours. A siege on a group lasts 18 hours, so a runner can arrive too late ("A call for help, too late").
  • The call makes the group known and offers: Send a squad (the best 3 fighters at the hub march there; a big goodwill gain if they arrive in time: +20 if the defenders hold, +8 if they fall, +3 if the fight was over; one helper may be hurt, 50%; helpers add 1.4 × their combat power to the defence), Send 40 ammo (the attackers lose 20% of their strength, +6 goodwill), or Ignore it (−14 for friendly or allied groups, −8 otherwise).

Treaties, demands and war between groups Built

  • Pacts between groups: two groups at peace with relations 28+ sign a pact (5% a day); it breaks below 10 or in a war. Pact partners never raid each other, and each may join the other's war (40% when it starts).
  • Wars between groups: declared when relations are −42 or worse, no pact, nearest bases within 18 tiles, the attacker at least 0.6 × as strong and both big enough (attacker 10+ people, defender 8+): 1% a day × the attacker's temper (raiders 1.5, expansionists 1.4, zealots 1.2, military 0.5, fortress 0.3, traders and communes 0.1). At most one war per group (two for aggressive types), three in all, none for 90 days after a peace between the same two, and never between two coalition members. The reason is land (bases within 9 tiles), resources (the attacker short of food or ammo) or a hive between them.
  • War raids: each side raids 6% a day (aggressive 9%) while none of its war raids is out, with strength × 0.3–0.45 (× 1.25 if you fight on its side). The defenders win with odds defence / (attack + defence), defence = 4 + strength × 0.5 + helpers. A win kills 5–10% of the defenders (never below 3), takes 10% of their food, ammo, meds and scrap, may take a secondary base (35% × the wish to keep it) and a captive (25%). A failed raid costs 1–2 people. If you are in the war, the enemy's war raids may target your bases within 20 tiles.
  • Weariness and peace: 0.4 a day (0.8 below 70% of starting people), +4 a failed raid, +5 a raid suffered, +8 a base lost. Peace: at once when a side is down to 6 people; after 10 days 25% a day once a side's weariness is 55+; after 75 days 10% a day; otherwise 1% a day. Terms by war score (2 a won raid, +2 for a base, 1 a raid held): a lead of 5+ against a loser with 2+ bases cedes the base nearest the winner (land); a lead of 2+ pays 25% of food, ammo, meds and scrap (tribute); otherwise a white peace. Captives go home.
  • Your share: if your side wins, half the tribute reaches your hub and ceded land is left free for your outpost (no group may expand onto it for 60 days). The beaten enemy settles at −35 goodwill.
  • Demands on you: raids ask for tribute on the warning card (30% of the food and 30 ammo); outside raiders ask a toll on the road or a pay-off at your gates (Visitors); the coalition council asks for supplies.
  • Shortages: 1% a day per group, a store is lost (food 45% to blight or rot, meds 60% to sickness, fuel 60% to a fire, ammo 40%); news on the radio and their wants list is redone at once.

Alliances and the Calder Coalition

The Calder Coalition is your alliance: groups you invite keep their own identity but share sightings, defend your bases, join your hive assaults and sit in a seasonal council. It is also one of the two ways to win the game.

Joining Built

  • A group can be invited when it is alive and known; reachable (in radio reach, your team at its gates, a base of yours within 8 tiles of one of its bases, or a coalition member's base within 12 tiles of it); not hostile; its goodwill is 50+ (for peaceful groups 50 + infamy / 2, at most 90); it has a pact with you; it is not at war with a member; and you have done at least one job for it or the council votes it in (each member votes yes when its relation with the candidate is 10+; a majority carries it).
  • A group that qualifies may ask to join on its own: 3% a day, no more than once every 20 days ("Welcome them" / "Not now").
  • On joining: +5 goodwill with you and +10 relation with every member.
  • A member leaves when its goodwill falls below 20, its pact with you is gone, it starts a war against another member, you attack it (every other member −5), or you expel it (−15).

The council Built

Once you have a member, the council meets at the start of every season: day 15, then every 28 days (43, 71, …). It asks one thing:

  • Supplies for a member that is short (need 1.3+) of food, meds, ammo or fuel: send 30 food / 8 meds / 60 ammo / 10 fuel (+6 with it, +3 with the others) or refuse (−4 with all).
  • A job for a member (an ordinary job card; declining costs −1 with it and −3 with every member).
  • A ruling (two or more members) over farmland, a well, salvage rights or stolen livestock: side with one (+6 with it, −8 with the other, −10 between them) or propose a compromise (Speech check 25% + 4% a point + (goodwill A + goodwill B) / 400, 5–90%: works +3 with both and +5 between them, fails −3 with both).

Conquest

You can storm any group's base with a team ("Assault their base"). A captured base becomes a strong outpost that holds land like your hub; take a group's last base and it is finished. Groups can do the same to you, and you can take your bases back.

Assaulting a base Built

  • Ordering it: plan a trip with the mission "Assault their base" to any group's base. The planner and the arrival card warn hard if the group is not hostile, is a coalition member, has a pact with you or has pact partners.
  • The defenders: the group's people are shared over its bases, the main base counting double. 60% of the people at that base fight (at least 3, at most 16), each with power 2.6 + 1.6 × arms (commune 3.56 … military 5.16). Walls multiply their strength by 1 + 0.05 per wall point: fortress and military bases 7 points (× 1.35), a group's main base 4 (× 1.2), any other base 1 (× 1.05).
  • The odds the card shows: 0.5 + (your power ÷ their power − 1) × 0.6, between 5% and 95%; a team with less than 3 ammo a head fights at 60%.
  • The battle: a tactical battle on the "baseAssault" map: their palisade with a gate and 1–3 breaches (more the weaker the walls), barricades and sandbags behind it, half the defenders at the wall. You can fall back from the left edge. By the numbers (autopilot, auto-resolve) the walls multiply their strength.
  • Costs at once: −30 goodwill, any pact ends, a coalition member leaves (every other member −5).

When you win

  • The base is yours: a new outpost on the tile with their palisade (battered, wear 70: two sections if they had strong walls, else one) and their bunkhouse (6 beds, wear 30). It holds land like your hub (9 tiles on Normal), which makes captured bases the fastest way to the coalition's land share. If you had no hub left, it becomes your hub.
  • Their stores at that base (its share of the group's stock): half is yours, the rest is spoiled or carried off.
  • The weakest fighter stays as first staff if the team has 3+ people (the autopilot keeps its squad together; with no hub left, everyone moves in).
  • Their people there: 35% die; of the rest 40% flee to their other bases (none if it was the last); of those who give up, a quarter ask to join you (a recruit card, at most 3, quality 0.8) and a fifth become your prisoners at the new outpost (at most 3). Your people they held there walk free (all of them if it was their last base).
  • Their main base (not their last): the leaders fall with it and half of the group's remaining people scatter.
  • A group left with no base, or with 3 people or fewer, is broken: it collapses (its parties vanish, its land is nobody's, refugees may come to you).

When you lose

The team falls back; the group loses 60% of the defenders you killed; a beaten team with more than one member has a 30% chance that someone is caught and held captive.

Infamy and fallout

  • Every base you take raises infamy: +2 from a hostile aggressive group (nothing more for finishing it), +15 from anyone else (+10 more for finishing them). Infamy fades 0.1 a day, max 100.
  • Above 20, known peaceful non-members lose (infamy − 20) / 200 goodwill a day, and peaceful groups want goodwill 50 + infamy / 2 (at most 90) before they join the coalition.
  • Groups with a pact with the victim: −8 (−15 when you finish it); its friends (relation 28+): −4 (−8); its enemies (−42 or worse): +2 (+4).

Losing your own bases Built

  • A raid that overruns a base of yours (or breaks into an outpost, at 35% of the usual chance) may keep it: the chance depends on the group type (raiders 0.8, expansionists 0.9, zealots 0.7, military 0.55, fortress 0.5, traders and communes 0.3), distance from their bases (full within 7 tiles, falling to 0.4 at 25), the base's wealth (0.8 + stock value / 2000, at most 1.4) and their base cap; your hub × 0.35. They carry off half its stock and leave a garrison of 60% of the raid (at least 2), which grows back 0.2 a day; each broken building has a 5% chance a day to be repaired, wear falls 1 a day, so a lost base is easiest to retake at once.
  • Your people there leave as a team for your nearest base with what they can carry (water and food for 4 days, 15 scrap, ammo, meds, more food, a vehicle if one is parked). Retreat on the raid warning card does the same before the raid arrives (the raiders walk in without a fight).
  • Aggressive groups may instead raze it (raiders 22%, zealots 12%, expansionists 5%, × 1.25 a grudge point up to 4, × 1–2 for a base far from theirs; your hub × 0.4; an empty base × 0.5): every building, its stores and parked vehicles burn, and the tile is a ruin.
  • If the hub falls, your nearest base becomes the hub. With no base left, your survivors become exiles: a team on the road that can found a new hub (Build outpost) or retake the old one. The run ends only when nobody is left.
  • If the group holding your base is broken and nobody else takes it, the base comes back to you, empty.

Is there a conquest victory?

Verified in code: no separate conquest victory exists. checkGameOver knows only the hive victory and the coalition victory. Conquest counts toward the coalition victory: a broken group no longer has to join, and every base you capture holds 9 tiles of land, so "break everyone and hold more than half the land" wins as the coalition, with the line "no group is left standing against you".

Changed from Draft 1: of Draft 1's four ways to take over a faction (force, shadow, economy, diplomacy), force is built (assault, capture, absorb some survivors as recruits and prisoners). Planned: shadow operations (infiltration, removing leaders, sabotage), economic takeover (starve them, buy them out) and merging a group by diplomacy (the coalition keeps groups separate instead). Absorbed groups do not hand over their routes or specialists.

Trade

All trade is barter, and every group prices every good its own way: by what kind of group it is, the state it is in and how much of it it already holds. The player never sees a base value, only what this group pays or charges and why. One price model serves team visits, caravans at your gates, radio deals, trade routes and outsiders.

Ways to trade Built

  • A team at their gates (mission "trade", or a trade caravan of yours stopping there): trade from what the team carries; they sell from their stock and their gear shelf.
  • Their caravan at your base (asked for over the radio, or a radio deal): trade from the base's stores and armory for what the caravan carries; it stays 24 hours.
  • Radio deals: name what you want and what you pay; if fair, their caravan delivers.
  • Their radio offers: each day a known group in reach that is not hostile may call with an offer or a request: 15% a day for traders, 10% for a commune, 6% for others, at most one every 6 days per group, never while one of their caravans is out.
  • Trade routes: a fixed load swapped on a schedule (below).
  • Outsiders: merchants and travellers from beyond the border (Visitors) and the peddler hub event.

How prices work

  • Supplies: base value × need × kind × state × spread. Need = 2 − (stock per head ÷ reference), between 0.5 and 2.2 (reference per head: food 6, water 5, fuel 2, meds 1, ammo 10, parts 0.5, scrap 3, preserved 2, jars 1). Starving or thirsty groups pay up to 60% more again for food or water. Every unit of a deal is priced halfway through the deal (stock + q/2 when they buy), so big sales pay less per unit and selling then buying back always loses.
  • Gear: item worth (base value × (0.2 + 0.8 × condition) × uses left for makeshift gear; a pack adds its contents) × kind × state × how much like it they hold: 1.15 with none on their shelf, falling 0.5 per comfortable amount, down to 0.25. Books and manuals count by title.
  • Spread: they pay you 0.75 + Speech bonus + goodwill bonus, and charge 1.3 − both. Speech bonus 0.008 per point of your best Speech (max 0.12; the Haggler perk adds 5 Speech); goodwill bonus goodwill / 1000 (−0.1 to +0.1). Traders and outside merchants add a 10% margin to what they charge.
  • Reserve: a group never sells below a reserve per head (food 2, water 1.5, fuel 0.5, meds 0.3, ammo 3, preserved 0.5); at war, twice that of food, ammo, meds and fuel.
  • Fair deal: what you give, at their buying price, is worth at least what you take at their selling price. "Balance with" ammo, scrap or food fills the gap automatically. Radio deals are honoured at the agreed amounts.
  • Carry limits: a team must be able to carry what it gets; their caravan what it takes.
  • What the window shows: no numbers about worth, but "why" lines (▲ wanted, ▼ little use, ✕ will not touch) and a mood line (their tastes and current state).

The market board Built

Every 7 days each group makes a wants list (goods with demand 1.25+, enough to bring them back to comfortable, at least half a lot) and a surplus list (demand 0.9 or less with 5+ to spare). Lot sizes: food and water 20, fuel 10, meds 6, ammo 50, parts 3, scrap 20, preserved 10, jars 10. With a radio station, the Market board ranks every known, non-hostile group in radio reach by who pays most or charges least for one good, with arrows against last week and a war marker.

Trade routes Built

  • Need a trade pact with the group. Set what to send and how much, what to receive and how much (checked against their prices that day: at most what the load is worth to them), how often (every 2+ days, default 5), the escort tier (light, standard, heavy; suggested 1, 3, 5 people) and the escorts.
  • On schedule a convoy leaves the hub: a car at the hub (condition above 20) if it can be fuelled for 2.2 × the one-way fuel, otherwise hand carts; food for the round trip, water, and 8 / 20 / 40 ammo by escort tier.
  • At the partner it hands over the load and gets the agreed amount at most, never more than the load is worth to them that day or than they hold (the log says when it is less). +1.5 goodwill per delivery, +0.3 a day while the route is active.
  • A route pauses when the pact is torn up (their goodwill falls to hostile), when no escort is left alive, or ends when the partner is gone. Convoys face the escort table (Caravans).

Caravans and supply runs

Goods move physically across the map: groups' caravans walk to your gates, your trade caravans tour a list of destinations, trade-route convoys shuttle to a partner, and supply runs move stock between your own bases. All of them can be hit on the road.

Their caravans at your gates Built

  • Sent when you ask for one or agree a radio deal, or as aid. They walk (speed 0.9) with the group's convoy sprite from their main base along a land route to your hub (or your nearest reachable base); no land route, no caravan.
  • Guards: 2 + 1 per 12 people (2–5), each as strong as 2.2 + 1.6 × arms. Carry space 60 + 15 a guard. When you just ask, they bring about half of what they can spare from their surplus list (and other goods they have plenty of) up to about 160 in value, plus 4 items from their shelf (traders) or 2 (others).
  • At your base the trade window opens (or queues behind another); the caravan stays 24 hours, then goes home with what it has. A radio deal not done by then costs 3 goodwill. Aid caravans just unload and leave.
  • Attacking it (trade window or Bases tab, with a confirmation): a battle at your base against its guards. Win and its goods go into the base's stores; either way the group is set to goodwill −70, its pact is torn up, every known peaceful group loses 5, and for 60 days the group raids you whatever its type. The group loses 70% of the guards you killed. The autopilot only ever does this in the Aggressive style, against a lightly guarded caravan of an aggressive group.

Your trade caravans Built

  • Planned in "New trade caravan…": a crew from the hub, on foot or by car, a stance, goods to trade (carried as cargo), supplies to live on (auto-fill: food and water for the route plus 25%, and fuel), items from the armory, and an ordered list of stops (known groups' bases and your outposts). The planner shows hours per leg (6 hours at each stop) and what each group on the route would give for the load.
  • At each stop a card offers: trade with them (the trade window), unload the goods (your outposts), stay a day, go on to the next stop (with ETA), return home, the team panel, or other orders.

Supply runs between your bases Built

  • A saved job: origin and destination base, crew, an optional vehicle parked at the origin, goods to deliver, goods to pick up, a stance, a schedule (once, or every X days) and what to do on arrival: Come back (unload, load the pick-up, come home), Hold there (wait as a parked convoy, eating 1 food and 1 water a person a day from the base or its own supplies; hunger costs 6 health, thirst 8), or Disband (the crew joins that base, the vehicle parks there).
  • On departure it takes food and water for the whole trip plus a day, 5 ammo a head, and fuel for the round trip + 15% (jerry cans for what the tank cannot hold); 4 hours at the far end. It is blocked (and the log says why, once per kind of problem) when the crew is missing, stock is short (unless "send what is there" is ticked), the load is too heavy, or there is no road or fuel (it then goes on foot with hand carts).
  • Outposts keep back the stock that runs will send or pick up from their daily surplus shipping to the hub. A run that vanishes ("never arrived") points you at a death site or abandoned vehicle on its road.

Radio

The radio is how you reach other groups without walking there: it lets you meet groups, talk, trade, hear the market and world news, broker peace, invite allies and get live reports from your teams. Without a radio station, everything happens by runners and teams at the gates.

The network Built

  • A radio station reaches 8 tiles around its base (+2 on generator power).

  • Groups whose base is within your coverage plus 4 tiles (they have radios too) can be called.
  • Your teams inside coverage report live and can be redirected; a team carrying a military radio stays in contact 4 tiles further out.

A band (named like "the Ash Riders") of clamp(3 + day/30 + 0–2, 3, 14) people, each with power 2.6 + min(3.4, day/80): clubs and old pistols early, assault rifles and plate in the second year. They pick targets by loot against defence and never attack anything stronger: your bases (only when they reach 0.6 of its defence), your teams in the field, convoys, other groups' bases, caravans, rich unclaimed sites (loot 0.45+). They leave when they carry 10 × their strength in loot, lost half the band, were paid off or their days are up.

At your base
Warning card at 4 tiles if seen or heard: prepare defences, pay them off (4 × their strength in goods), talk them down (Speech: 10% + 3.5% a point, max 75%, for half the price), defend in person, or hold. After 8 hours at the walls: held at 1.2× their strength, break-in from 0.8× (20% of the stock, 10% with a storehouse), else overrun (50%, 25%; an outpost is lost). They never keep a base.
On the road
They found you: fight, pay the toll (35% of what the team carries, at most 3 × their strength), talk, or run (Scouting, vehicle, stealthy pace). You saw them: slip away (Stealth), ambush, or lie low.
Other groups
A 12-hour siege, then the usual raid odds; they keep 60% of what the group lost. Sites lose 10–18% of their loot.
Settling
A band of 8+ carrying 250+ worth of loot may dig in (3% a day) at a site or ruin within 6 tiles: it becomes a hostile raider gang.

Merchants from outside Built

"The Kessler caravan": 2 traders and 3–5 guards (+1 every 90 days, max 7; power 3.5 + min(2.5, day/120)) walking with a gold-canvas wagon and oxen. They carry what the state lacks: fuel 15–40, meds 8–22, preserved food 20–50, parts 3–9, ammo 60–160, a manual or blueprint (75%, a second 25%), 1–3 books, 2–4 pieces of good gear and 1–2 valuables; they want food, water, scrap and jars.

Route
Up to 3 groups' bases (never raiders or zealots), trading by demand; one of your bases if invited by radio, or unannounced 55% of the time.
At your base
24 hours; the trade window opens. A team that meets them can trade on the road, ask for news, rob them or move on.
Reputation
Robbing them: merchant reputation −45 (new merchants start at goodwill 15 + reputation, hostile at −40, skip your bases below −30; it recovers 0.25 a day), known traders −15, communes and fortresses −8.

Travellers: scouts Built

40% of travellers. 2–3 armed scouts (power 3.2) with binoculars, carrying 20–40 ammo and 1–3 meds. They come to your hub 30% of the time.

Offer
News of other states and the hordes they passed; their maps for sale (2 tiles either side of their way); trade. Rarely one asks to join (10%).

Travellers: refugees Built

35% of travellers. 2–5 people with a handcart of bundles; 75% come to your hub.

Offer
Their news (the tiles along their way, as they were), a little trade, and 1–3 of them ask to join (a recruit card).

Travellers: wanderers Built

25% of travellers. 1–2 walkers with staffs; 45% come to your hub. Half the time one asks to stay.

Offer
News, a little trade, perhaps a recruit.
  • Travellers walk between 2–3 ruins away from the groups' grounds, searching each 4–8 hours (the site loses 3–6% of its loot). 8% are robbers in disguise: they ambush a team ("Robbers on the road") or rob the base that shelters them.
  • At your gate: hear their news (free), buy the scouts' maps, trade, hear who wants to stay (recruit card), shelter them for 3 days (1 food and 1 water each a day; everyone at the base mood +3; afterwards a gift half the time, and 35% someone asks to stay for good), or send them on.
  • On the road: swap news, trade, hear who wants to join, share supplies (team mood +2 and they share their map), or move on.

The living world

The state changes whether or not you watch: groups expand, strip sites, go to war, burn each other's bases, collapse and are replaced by new gangs, while things fall out of the sky. The chronicle records all of it, without fog, for the timelapse at the end of a run.

Groups rising and falling Built

  • Raids between groups: aggressive groups raid neighbours within 14 tiles (traders and communes weighted 1.2, others 0.6), with 25–40% of their strength, never pact partners or fellow coalition members (war enemies get war raids instead). Defence = 8 + 0.8 × strength + helpers. Clearly outmatched (1.6×) at a secondary base, the defenders may give it up without a fight (50%) and lose 10% of their stores. A raid at 1.1× the defence kills 20–35% of the defenders, takes 40% of their stores (half carried home), and with a secondary base 50% of the time decides the base's fate: keep it, raze it, or loot it and go. A winning aggressive group gains a fighter 50% of the time. A held raid costs the attacker 1–3 people. Each ordinary raid sours their relation by 12.
  • Collapse: a group at 3 people or fewer collapses. Its parties vanish, its land becomes nobody's. The group that broke it takes over its bases (nearest first, up to its cap), 2–4 of its people and half its stores; if hordes or you broke it, the strongest aggressive group within 15 tiles does so 50% of the time. 60% of the time 1–3 refugees reach your hub and ask to join.
  • New gangs: from day 60, whenever fewer than 2 aggressive groups are left hostile to you, a gang from beyond the border settles a free site 12–30 tiles from your hub (2% a day): 12–18 people, hostile (raiders 50%, zealots 25%, expansionists 25%). Raider bands from outside can also dig in (Visitors). The state is never empty of predators for long.
  • Wars, pacts and peace between groups, shortages, and takeovers come over the radio as news.

Ruins Built

A base burned to the ground (one of yours or a group's) becomes a ruin: a scorched yard with charred wall stubs and a lone chimney; one of yours flies a torn blue rag. A ruin keeps a little loot (0.25) to scavenge, and an outpost built on it costs only 5 scrap instead of 15. Groups and settling raider bands prefer to build on ruins.

Crash sites and supply drops Built

Every 10–20 days (the first between days 8 and 14) something comes down: an Army helicopter (33%), a supply plane (22%), a medevac (20%), a news chopper (13%) or a parachute supply drop (12%). A base (the hub counts 3×), a team in the field or the hub's radio witnesses it: a card and a search circle on the map (radius 4; 3 from a base with a watchtower; 5 heard on the radio) that holds the wreck, not at its centre.

  • Finding it: a team passing within 1 tile sees the smoke; a look around finds it within 2 tiles (3 with Scouting 8+); a look that finds nothing narrows the circle. The planner's "Search the area" step walks the circle (up to 8 looks) and then searches the wreck.
  • The site: a temporary rich site (military gear, meds, blueprints in locked cargo cases) that burns out 7–10 days after the crash.
  • The race: groups with a base within 22 tiles saw it too and most send a salvage party 12–36 hours later (raiders 75%, military 85%, expansionists 65%, zealots 50%, fortress 40%, traders 30%, commune 20%). One that reaches the wreck strips it to 30% of what is left; if your team is there, it challenges you. The noise draws up to two hordes within 8 tiles toward it for 3 days.

The chronicle and the timelapse Built

The chronicle records the whole world without fog: one frame a day (every 6 hours for the first 3 days, plus a frame soon after a death or a base gained or lost), with tile owners and infection, your bases, the groups' bases, hordes, hives and build sites, parties, ruins, your population and hub stocks. It writes captions for notable events: bases founded and lost, groups collapsing, taking over or losing places, hives destroyed or rising, hordes massing (120+) or striking a group, sieges, outsiders crossing the border and bands settling, and the victory or the fall. The timelapse viewer (from the game-over screen or the dev panel) plays it back at 1, 2, 5 or 10 days a second with map layers, a population chart and the latest captions.
