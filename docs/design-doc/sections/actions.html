Player actions reference

Every order, mission and decision the player can give in the prototype, grouped by where it is given: the State Map, a team in the field, a base, a tactical battle or raid, diplomacy and trade, prisoners, and the decision cards. Each table says what the action does, what it costs or needs, and where to find it. Numbers are taken from the code (sim/orders.js, parties.js, plans.js, markers.js, locks.js, deploy.js and the UI files).

Design intent: the game starts paused, and giving an order never changes the speed. An order given while paused is armed and starts when you unpause (the pause banner counts the waiting orders). Every card stops the clock until it is answered. Out of radio contact a team follows its plan and can only be given orders through the cards it raises (the radio rule, plans.js commandable).

1. State Map: sending a team from the hub Built

Right-click any land tile (or pick an action in the tile panel) and choose a mission marked (from hub). This opens the trip planner with that tile as the target. Only missions that fit what you know about the tile are offered: hidden sites nobody has found are bare ground, and nothing is offered on open water or on your own base.

5. Map markers you act on

Death site Built

Where your people fell, with their gear. Scavengers strip it a piece a day, most valuable first (faster by roads, in a group's land and in towns). Action: Recover bodies.

Abandoned vehicle Built

A red jerry can when it needs fuel, smoke when badly damaged. Passers-by siphon and strip it over time. Action: Recover vehicle (2 fuel to restart).

Supply cache Built

A hidden stash spotted by a team's perception roll (12% + 3% per Scouting point, Keen Eye and Spotter +15%, ×0.6 at night). Action: Investigate find.

Wrecked car Built

Restore it into a sedan with parts and fuel, or strip it. Up on blocks once stripped.

Abandoned camp Built

Supplies and notes that put a group's bases or a nearby hive on your map. Collapsed once picked through.

Team status badges on the map: ! waiting for orders (icon/alert), ? an estimated position out of contact (icon/unknown), a wrench for work on the spot, a walkie-talkie for radio contact, crossed swords for a siege.

Saving and loading Built

  • Game tab: Save (to this game's slot), Save as new (named), Save to file…, Save and exit, Exit without saving, New campaign; in the desktop app Save and quit to desktop and Quit. On the new-run screen: Continue (latest save) and Load game….
  • Slots: up to 12 named saves plus the autosave in the browser's storage, gzip-compressed (a 365-day world is about 1.7 MB of JSON, about 280 KB stored), each with a map thumbnail; Load, Save here, Export (.bsave), Delete; Load from file adds a .bsave.
  • What is saved: the whole world as a graph (shared objects written once), the seeded random generators and the id counter, so a loaded game plays on exactly like the original. Battles, raids, the deploy step, searches and trade windows are saved mid-turn. An open event card cannot be saved: answer it first. A save does not keep the interface (open windows, camera, camera spots).
  • Saves carry a format version (format 1); newer or unknown versions are refused with a reason.

Art and presentation

Everything is drawn in code: 2D top-down procedural pixel sprites with no image files. The State Map gained terrain decals (style B) and rippled water (style A) in October 2026, and a map chunk cache keeps the desktop app at about 120 fps on a 4K screen.

Procedural pixel sprites Built

  • Battle sprites (ui/sprites.js): survivors built from their appearance (build, skin, hair style and color, facial hair, top, pants, accessory) plus worn armor and weapon; zombies as walkers, runners, bloated and brutes; enemy humans in raider / soldier / zealot kits in their group's color.
  • Map sprites (ui/mapsprites.js): your teams (pickup, sedan, walkers with packs, scouts with binoculars, a relief squad behind a banner, a rowboat or raft with a wake, convoys with crates or drums), other groups' parties in their own look, about 40 site sprites on plates in their zone's color (faded when picked over, danger skulls up close), hordes as crowds of 2–7 figures, siege camps, ruins, markers and status icons.
  • Hive sprites (ui/hivesprites.js): hives by level, build sites, growths, the Wallbreaker on the map and the two bosses for the battle map.

State Map terrain: style B decals with style A water Built

Four tile styles were prototyped (docs/tile-designs): A pixel ground, B scattered decals, C painterly with edge blending, D tactical map. The user picked B's decals with A's rippled water, now in ui/terraindeco.js.

  • Flat colors stay underneath; each tile gets a few small outline-free pixel sprites: forests 4–6 round trees and 2–3 pines (broadleaf trees turn gold and red in autumn and stand bare in winter), farmland crop rows, fences and hay bales near farms, hills mounds and rocks, mountains peaks, suburbs houses (about a third burnt out), city blocks, rubble and wrecks, cars beside roads, reeds on river banks.
  • Every decal has a snow-capped version; decals come pre-shaded for live, reported and unknown ground.
  • Placement is seeded per tile from the world's seed, never from the game's random numbers; decals keep off roads, bridges and the clear zone around sites, bases and hives.
  • Flat below zoom 0.6, fading in up to 0.75. Menu → Options → Map detail: Flat draws the old map at every zoom.

Performance work Built

  • Map chunk cache (ui/render.js): the map is drawn into cached chunks per zoom level; a frame only copies chunks. Changed chunks are redrawn within a 3 ms budget a frame (mapTuning.budgetMs). Overlays (Known area, Coalition land) are baked into the chunks and worked out only when their data changes.
  • Decal cost: the first full 4K screen at zoom 1 takes about 49 ms detailed against 33 ms flat; panning over cached chunks costs the same either way (0.2–0.6 ms of script a frame). Textures total about 1.7 MB; the atlas and water are ImageBitmaps (sub-images of a canvas cost about 30× more per draw in Chrome).
  • Panels: above 3× the panels redraw about 4 times a second instead of every tick, and the top bar redraws each piece only when its text changes.
  • Simulation: headless, a day takes about 15–20 ms on every world size with the autopilot; a Huge map is generated in about 120 ms.

Desktop app Built

An Electron window from desktop/ (launcher Play Broken State.cmd). It always plays the current files in prototype/ on the fixed origin app://brokenstate/, remembers its size, position, maximized and fullscreen state, and keeps saves in its own browser storage. Keys: F11 fullscreen, Ctrl+R reload, Ctrl+Shift+I DevTools, Ctrl+= / Ctrl+- / Ctrl+0 zoom. npm run dist makes a portable build (about 330 MB). npm run perf runs a new game on seed 42 with Reveal map and the autopilot at 200× for 60 s at 1920 × 1080 and maximized, and prints frame-time and memory JSON. After the map-cache work it runs at about 120 fps at 4K.

Changed from Draft 1: Draft 1 planned low-poly 3D with a tilted camera, dynamic lighting and audio. The engine decision of 2026-10-02 moved to the browser with 2D top-down art. There is no audio yet; lighting is gameplay only (night sight, flashlights, floodlights, flares).

Developer tools and testing

The simulation (src/sim) has no DOM, so over fifty headless Node tools play it without a browser: smoke tests, rule checks, determinism and save checks, and balance censuses that run hundreds of autopilot campaigns on worker threads. In the browser, the dev panel lifts the fog, fast-forwards and hands the holdout to an AI governor.

Dev tools panel Built

  • Open: ` or the Dev button (ui/devtools.js).
  • Reveal map (no fog of war; display only, your intel is unchanged), Infection overlay, Show FPS (frame rate, frame time, script time and simulation ticks a second in the map's corner).
  • Fast forward 60× / 300× / 1200× (1200× is a day in about 0.4 s); Space goes back to your last normal speed.
  • Autopilot (Shift+P) with a style: Cautious, Balanced or Aggressive, and a 🤖 line saying what is short (hover for the reserve table).

The autopilot Built

An AI governor (sim/autopilot.js) that uses only the same calls as the UI and what you know of the map. Every hour it sets roles; at 07:00 it plans buildings, crafts, trips, supply runs, outposts, trades and radio; it answers every card by rules or a score; it plans the winter food and the summer water; it keeps reserves (Cautious); it wages the war on the hives with a squad of six, and conquers bases by style. While it is on, battles resolve by the numbers, scavenging uses the hour-by-hour search and cards never stop the clock. The same per-base code runs the stewards, and crews use its judgement (crewAI).

Known gaps (README)

  • Hives: the autopilot rarely destroys hives and never culls a build site; base sprites do not show defence buildings; hives can overlap; a build site cannot be attacked directly; hordes do not march on camps.
  • Balance: first pass; raid balance untested with real players.
  • Field: the estimated position of an out-of-contact team follows the way out and back only; the autopilot launches no plans and does not camp; orders queued after a plan's Return home are dropped.
  • Saving: no save while a card is open; saves live in browser storage (Export to keep them); the interface is not saved; no migrations yet (format 1).
  • Alerts, diary, roster: categories from wording; links found by name; no alerts for teams short of food or water or vehicles out of fuel.
  • Retreat and base battles: no sounding the retreat in raids or base defences without an evac strip; two maps rather than one continuous base; the dead batter only doors, windows, the gate and wall sections; night only in base battles and raids.
  • Outposts cannot be abandoned by hand; trade routes cannot swap escorts; your caravans start from the hub only; groups do not trade gear with each other.
  • Captives are not shown on the map; convoys get no conflict cards; held convoys do not help defend.
  • Timelapse: parties only at frame times, straight lines; markers and battles not drawn.
  • Crafting: no torches; makeshift gear is not traded. Power: per base, generators need an operator; the hospital and outposts' relays take no power.
  • Weather: one sky for the whole state; estimates "know" more than the forecast.
  • People and mood: hub events only at the hub; no children or families; opinion is one number per pair.
  • Economy: no pests or crop rotation; water never rationed; illness gives no immunity.
  • Autopilot: fights always by the numbers; no trade routes, boats or deliberate scouting; it overwrites hand-set roles.
  • Skills: no XP from hub events or mediating; earned traits not in yet.
  • Exploration: keys cannot be traded; no blowtorch or explosives for safes; locks only in raids and the hour-by-hour search; blueprints are global once studied.
  • Inventory: sprites show weapon and body armor only; no picking things up during a battle.
  • Outsiders and diplomacy: their raids on groups and wars are resolved by the numbers; joining a war adds abstract patrols; no peace treaty with a group you fought except through its war's end.
