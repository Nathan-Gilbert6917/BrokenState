Survivors

Survivors are the scarcest resource in Broken State. Every one is a named person with nine skills, one or two traits, health, mood, relationships and a paper-doll inventory. You play as the group, not as an avatar, and the run ends only when nobody is left.

Appearance Built

Every survivor is a procedural pixel sprite drawn in code from an appearance record. Looks never use the world's random numbers: the same seed always gives the same faces, and the new-run screen can edit or re-roll each look without changing the world.

Making a survivor

  • Skills: each of the nine skills rolls 0–9 times a quality factor, then one or two specialties get +3 to +7 (capped at 20). The first starting colonist (the leader) rolls at quality 1.3, the others at 1.0, newcomers at 0.85.
  • Traits: one trait (60%) or two (40%); never Brave and Coward together.
  • Start values: health 100, mood 60 (newcomers: health 60–100, mood 45–65).
  • Starting group: six colonists at the hub. Starting weapons are a 9mm pistol, a machete, a hunting rifle, two kitchen knives and a second pistol; the leader also wears a Kevlar vest.
  • Starting jobs: by skill, not list order: the two best farmers farm, the best scavenger scavenges, the best shot guards, the best mechanic builds, anyone left farms. Nobody starts on the ammo bench (the hub opens with plenty of ammo and little food).
  • New-run screen: per colonist you can rename, randomise the name, move skill points within a fixed total, edit or randomise the look, change traits and pick the leader.

Needs Partial

The build tracks four hard needs and a soft one:

  • Food: one ration a day each (×1.1 below 0 °C mean, ×1.2 below −8 °C). See Hub economy for rationing and starvation.
  • Water: one a day each, times the season's thirst (spring/autumn 1, summer 1.3, winter 0.9, at least 1.6 on a hot day); dysentery adds 1.
  • A bed: beds come from the base (hub 4, outpost 2) and bunkhouses (+6 each). Overcrowding costs mood every day and spreads flu.
  • Health: see Health, injury & medicine.
  • Mood: the sum of many daily factors (rest, company, comforts, weather, grief). See Morale & comforts.
Changed from Draft 1: Draft 1 listed food, water, rest, safety, comfort and purpose as needs. Rest, comfort and company are mood factors in the build rather than separate meters; "safety" and "purpose" are not modelled.

Names

Names come from 36 first names and 25 last names (Maria Alvarez, Kenji Okafor …). Messages use first names, so nobody gets a first name that is already in use while another is free, and a full name is never repeated in a world. The player can rename anyone; an empty name or the full name of someone else alive is refused.

The leader Built

  • One survivor leads (the first colonist by default, or the one picked on the new-run screen). The leader works, travels, fights and dies like anyone else.
  • At the base where they live the leader lifts everyone's mood by 0.15 × Leadership a day (capped at half the crowding penalty while that base is overcrowded).
  • The leader gains 3 Leadership XP a day at a base, is the first choice to talk round someone who wants to leave, pulls friendships toward them, and never deserts.
  • If the leader dies, a card asks you to pick a new one from the six best free (not captive) people by Leadership; everyone loses 15 mood. A daily safety net asks again if the group ever has no leader.

Visitors joining

Outsiders (explorers, refugees and wanderers from other states) pass through the map. At your gate you can hear their news, buy maps, trade, take them in (a recruit card) or shelter them for 3 days (1 food and 1 water each a day, +3 mood at the base; afterwards half the time a gift, a third of the time someone asks to stay). 8% of explorers are robbers in disguise who rob the base that shelters them. The rest of the outsider system is described with the map's other groups.

Changed from Draft 1: Draft 1 planned a 25–50 person colony cap with squads, role presets, auto-assign and base foremen. The build has no hard cap (beds and food are the limit), named work roles and crews instead of squads (see Work), and stewards instead of survivor foremen.

Skills

Nine skills from 0 to 20 decide what each survivor is good at. They grow with use, by teaching and by reading, and the milestones at 5, 10 and 15 each give a perk chosen from a pair. Skills also gate gear: using an item below its required skill makes it weaker or unusable.

Levelling

Going from level L to L+1 takes 40 + 0.32 × L³ XP. A full day of one job is worth 11 XP; use XP is capped at 15 a day per skill and study XP (teaching and books) at 25, so busywork never snowballs. A 3 becomes a 7 in about a season of daily use; 15 → 16 takes about a year (112 days).

Health, injury & medicine

Health runs from 0 to 100 and death is permanent. Wounds heal slowly by themselves and much faster in a staffed hospital; named illnesses and work injuries cost meds and medic time to treat. Medicine is a logistics problem: meds, beds, medics and time.

Wounds and damage

  • Damage: wounds are reduced by worn armour (all pieces add up to a cap of 5.5) and by the Tough trait (−25%). Hunger, thirst, illness and infection are "raw" damage that armour and Tough ignore.
  • Deep cuts from wounds: a hit of 10+ health that does not kill has a 25% chance to leave a deep cut that needs dressing.
  • Severe wounds: below 30 health. They heal at 40% speed without a bed, at the hospital tier's speed in one.
  • Passive healing at a base: 4 health a day + 0.4 × the best Medicine there; in a hospital bed add the tier's heal + 0.5 × the best medic on duty (+6 with Chief of Medicine). Nobody heals while their base is out of food or water.
  • On the road: no passive healing to speak of; the team uses its carried meds. Field treatment heals 8 + Medicine × 0.6 (Field Surgeon: 12 + Medicine × 0.5) and takes an hour; a team automatically doses any illness of severity 45+ or pneumonia from its meds.

Infection, bites and the worm

  • Infected wounds: at a base, someone below 40 health has a 5% chance a day of infection (not in a Field hospital bed or better); on the road 8% a day. An infected wound halves healing and costs 2 health a day more at a base, and drains 3 health a day on the road.
  • Clearing: on its own 10% a day once health is 50+; a treatment with 1 extra meds cleans it with a chance of 25% + 15% per hospital tier + 3% per point of the treater's Medicine and kit (max 90%), or always from the Clinic up.
  • Untreated cuts become infected wounds 3% a day.
  • Bites: a bite is only a wound: nobody turns from one (see What the dead are: the worm). People turn from the worm. A travel event, The worm (on badly infected tiles, teams of 2+), finds that someone on the team has swallowed its eggs: "Purge it" (2 meds and a Medicine 10 check: survives at −50 health, else turns and dies), "End it quickly" (dies; everyone −8 mood) or "Hope it passes on its own" (turns 75% of the time, hurting a teammate).

  • Kits: a bandage and splint kit in the off hand adds +2 Medicine to every treatment (wears a use each time); a surgical kit +4 (full at Medicine 10, nothing below 3).

Death

  • At 0 health the survivor dies. Causes include battle wounds, hunger, thirst, illness, infected wounds, accidents and events.
  • At one of your bases: their things go into that base's armory; no death site inside the walls.
  • Elsewhere: a death site stays where they fell with the body and everything they carried; teammates know where. Death sites can be recovered by a team (or a crew).
  • Friends and partners grieve (see Morale); a dead leader triggers the leader card.
  • Game over: the run ends when nobody is left alive (deaths, desertion and a captive changing sides all count; captives still count as alive).

Morale & comforts

Mood (0–100) is how a survivor is holding up. It sets how much work they do, and if it falls too far they break down or walk out in the night with their gear. Beds, food, rest, friends, a good leader and small comforts keep it up; crowding, hunger, grief and long winters drag it down.

Warnings, breaks and desertion

  • Below 30: "talks about leaving" (log line and a card for the worst case at each base each day). Below 20: "close to leaving" (second warning). Warnings re-arm once mood is back to 40.
  • The warning card: Talk to them (the leader there or the best Speech: 30% + 3.5% per point, up to 95%; +25 mood, else +5), Give them 3 days' rest (+5 now, Rest role, then back to work), A gift from the stores (4 food, 1 meds: +20, Greedy +30), or Let them be.
  • Desertion: below 15 mood, only after the second warning on an earlier night: 8% a night + 0.6% per point below 15. Leaders never desert. A deserter keeps everything they carry and takes 2–4 food, 2–3 water and maybe 1–2 meds and 5–12 ammo (never more than a quarter of a stock).
  • Breaks: below 12 mood, 10% + 2% per point below 12 a day (5-day cooldown): sits out a day, binge eats, wanders off or picks a fight; +8 mood afterwards; a card for a fight or a second break within 10 days.

Relationships and grief

Every pair who share a base, a team, a job or a battle has an opinion from −100 to 100 that drifts daily toward a target set by compatibility, shared history and the leader's pull. Friend at 40+, close friend at 70+, rival at −30 or below; partners come from the "two survivors grow close" hub event. In battle each friend fighting alongside gives +3 aim (up to +6) and each rival −2 (down to −4). A friend or partner who dies costs −(6 + 14 × bond) mood at once and leaves them grieving for 3 + 7 × bond days. Friends of someone killed by a group get +5 aim against that group for 60 days and +6 mood when avenged.

Buildings & base plan

The hub and every outpost run on the same building list. Buildings are queued from a menu (not placed on a grid): materials are paid up front, builders burn down the work-hours, and the base's map sprite grows with what is built. Upper tiers need a studied manual or blueprint.

How construction works

  • Cost is paid when the job is queued. Cancelling an untouched job refunds everything; a started one gives half back (rounded up).
  • Build time is in work-hours. Each person on the Build role adds 1 + 3% per Mechanics point an hour (× work factor, ×1.2 Handy, ×1.4 Master Builder). Builders work the first job in the queue; construction stops while the base is on alert or under siege.
  • Limits: each building has a maximum per base; skill requirements need a survivor with that skill at the base when queued.
  • Breakdowns: many buildings wear and break (see Production, breakdowns table). A broken building does nothing until repaired.
  • The hub starts with 2 farm plots, an ammo bench, a bunkhouse and a wall section; its own well (12 water a day), cellar (200 food room), 4 beds, 6 crop plots and wall strength 3 come free.

On the map, your base sprite shows its walls (0–3 sections), a watchtower, a radio mast, the hospital (a red-cross building from the Clinic up, lit windows for the Trauma hospital), farms and a workshop.

Farming & food/water

Crops do not give food every day: each plot is sown, grows for weeks with the weather, and is harvested in one go. Water is the faster killer. Food must be stored, preserved and rationed through a 28-day winter, so farming is a year-long planning game.

The year

A year is 4 seasons of 28 days (112 days); a run starts on day 15 of spring. Spring is mild and wet, summer warm and dry (everyone drinks 30% more, 60% in a heat wave; wells give less), autumn cooling and wet, winter freezing (nothing grows outdoors; people drink 10% less but eat more in deep cold).

Plots and yield

  • Plots: hub 6, outpost 2, +2 per Farm plot building (up to 10), +2 sheltered plots per Greenhouse.
  • Yield = base yield × care × ground. Ground: farmland or a farmstead 1.3, forest 0.9, hills 0.85, else 1.
  • Care (averaged over the days the crop stood) = tending × skill. Tending by farmers per planted plot: 0 → ×0.55, 0.5 → ×1.0, 1 → ×1.23, 2 → ×1.4, 3 → ×1.44. Skill of the tending farmers: 0 → ×0.75, 7 → ×1.0, 10 → ×1.1, 20 → ×1.45. More and better farmers give more food, never a faster crop.
  • Work: each full-time farmer harvests 3 ripe plots and sows 2 a day. Sowing costs seed food (never the last two days' food). Ripe crops left past their window rot.
  • Weather damage: frost, hard freezes, blizzards, storms, heat and drought (on unwatered plots) can kill a standing crop; wet weather rots a ripe one.
  • Planning: farmers choose crops by the usual climate for the time of year (12 simulated years of weather, never the real days ahead), so they stop sowing tender crops into the first frosts and plan for the hungry gap.

Cooking and preserving

There is no separate cooking job: the Preserving kitchen (Farming) jars food, and its presence gives "hot meals" (+1 mood a day). Preserved food (jarred, dried, smoked, grain and dry beans) barely spoils (0.1% a day), needs no cellar room, weighs 0.35 kg a ration instead of 0.5, and sells well. Eating takes fresh food first. A jar holds 2 rations and 85% of jars come back when the food is eaten at a base. Weeks of nothing but preserved food cost mood.

Power

Power is a small mid-game step: generators burn fuel to run a handful of buildings better, and an ethanol still turns surplus or spoiled food into that fuel. Machines still need people, and a running generator is loud.

  • Generator (workshop station, up to 2): with an operator, 6 power for 2 fuel a day. It only starts when something draws power, and only as many generators run as the demand needs. The day's fuel is burned when it starts and lasts to the next midnight. +4 Heat at the base per running generator.

  • Paper doll: Hands (main weapon), Off hand (a tool in use), Head, Body, Arms, Legs, Backpack; 4 pockets for small items; pack slots from the pack. Items in pockets and packs do nothing until equipped (throwables and the suppressor work from a pocket or pack).

Hub economy

Every base runs the same daily economy: water in, scavenging, stations, drinking, crops, eating, spoilage, healing and morale. Food, water, meds, fuel, ammo and scrap are consumed constantly, which pushes you out onto the map to scavenge, trade and expand. Trade between groups is covered in Trade.

The daily tick (each base, at midnight)

  1. Water comes in (well, spring, stream, wells, rain catchers), up to capacity; summer evaporation above it.
  2. Base scavenging, hunting and fishing; Rest and in-bed mood.
  3. Workshop stations run (generators burn fuel first).
  4. People drink; then crops are watered and grow.
  5. People eat (fresh food first, then preserved); rationing or starvation.
  6. Drying racks, then spoilage.
  7. Infections, morale, warnings and desertion.

Shortages

  • Short rations: under 7 days of food everyone eats 60% and loses 3 mood a day (no health loss).
  • Starvation: a hub out of food: everyone loses 6, 8, 10 … up to 20 health a day and 10 mood; nobody heals. About a week without food kills.
  • Thirst: a base out of water: everyone loses 10, 14, 18 … up to 25 health a day (scaled by how short, at least 35%) and 8 mood; nobody heals. Thirst kills faster than hunger.
  • Illness: hunger and thirst also bring fever and dysentery (6% a day).
  • Stocks never go below zero; armour and Tough do not help against hunger or thirst.

Base scavenging

Rare by design: each scavenger has 7% a day of a small find at Scavenging 0 (+4% per point and crowbar point, ×1.15 Greedy, ×1.3 Scrounger, ×1.2 on a site or in a city, ×0.8 elsewhere, at most 50%). The surroundings get picked over (−0.4% per scavenger a day, down to 20%) and never come back. Finds come from the site's own loot table plus scrap: scrap 2–5, food 1–3, water 2–4, ammo 3–8, meds 1, fuel 1–2, parts 1, preserved 1–3, jars 2–6.

Outposts and supply lines

  • Each outpost has a supply line to the hub whose strength (25–100%) falls with distance and ×0.7 each for hostile land or a horde on the way; no land route means it is cut off.
  • An outpost short of food is covered from the hub (losing the line's share on the way); otherwise it starves.
  • Every day an outpost ships its surplus to the hub, keeping a reserve: food 3 a head + 6, water 7 a head + 5, ammo 6 a head, scrap 30, parts 3 while building, fuel 10, meds 2, plus what its supply runs need.
  • Supply runs: saved convoy jobs between your own bases (crew, optional vehicle, goods to deliver and pick up, a schedule once or every X days, and at the far end come back, hold or disband into that base). Convoys travel like any team: they eat, meet events, burn fuel and can die.
