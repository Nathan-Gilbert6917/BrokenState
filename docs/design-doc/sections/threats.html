Enemy types: the dead

The dead are the constant pressure of Broken State: they swarm out of the ground on the road, guard every hive, roam the State Map as hordes and batter your walls. Ordinary dead get tougher with the infection of the ground they stand on. From their first months the hives also breed special dead (Carriers first, then runners, brutes, spitters, screamers, crawlers) that each need a different answer. Two bosses top the threat: the Wallbreaker that leads hordes against bases, and the Broodmother inside every hive.

Ordinary dead Built

Every zombie on a battle map is one unit type. Its strength is a single number called power (p). Power is set by where you meet it:

  • On the road (a "surge of the dead" conflict card): power 1, +1 in a hive's land, +3 on the red-skull ring around a hive.
  • At a search site (raid mode): power 2 + 3 × the tile's infection, +1/+3 for hive ground.
  • Noise reinforcements and raid Heat waves: power 2 + 3 × infection.
  • A horde breaking into a base: 1.5 + (dead that broke in) / (5 × units on the map) + 0.5 × hive toughness, between 1.5 and 6.
  • A hive's brood: 5 + horde score / 30 (always on red-skull ground).
  • A growth's guard: 2 + 0.6 × hive toughness (2.6 to 3.8).

All dead have 2 actions a turn, sight 9 tiles (5 at night), no armor, and ignore cover: shots at the dead never count cover and get a 15% crit chance. They do not flee. The special dead (hive-bred) Built

A hive that has grown the right structure (see Hives & spread) puts special dead into every horde it makes. Each special is an ordinary zombie of the same power with multipliers on top. In battle they get a coloured ground ring and a letter. In fights resolved by the numbers each counts by its weight; at your walls each presses by its wall weight. The numbers below are at power 2 (a typical break-in).

Enemy types: the living

People are the other threat. The map's AI groups (raider gangs, zealots, expansionists, the military remnant, fortresses, traders and communes) meet your teams on the road, guard their sites and bases, and raid your bases once they turn hostile. Raiders from outside the state cross the map looking for loot. They all fight with real kits on the tactical map: guns, armor, the loot they carry, and the will to run when a fight turns.

Fighters by kit Built

Each group type fields one battle kit. A human enemy at power p has health 55 + 5p (+ kit), aim 39 + 4p (+ kit), melee aim 65 + 2p, a damage multiplier of 0.7 + 0.05p, 5 tiles of movement and 13 tiles of sight. At power 7+ half of them carry the kit's "strong" weapon. A Kevlar vest makes them take 88% of a hit, a plate carrier 76%. Only three outfits are drawn: soldiers (olive, helmet, armband in the group's color), zealots (robes, hood, sash) and everyone else in the raider outfit (leather, denim, a bandana or cap in the group's color).

How the living attack

  • On the road: conflict cards (ambush, roadblock, zealot patrol, checkpoint, challenge, contested site). See Conflicts on the road.
  • At their sites: a group's base you search has 3–7 guards (pop / 6) at power 4 + pop / 15; hostile land has a 40% chance of a 2–3-strong patrol at power 4; anywhere else a 15% chance of 2 rival scavengers at power 3.5.
  • At your bases: raids and sieges (see Raids & sieges), rescue raids for their people you hold (see Captives & prisoners), war raids when you join a war.

Building damage and repairs

When a raid on your base is settled, the raiders smash what they reach. The damage is a budget of wear points: raid strength × 3 × (siege hours / 8, between 0.15 and 1.5) × the outcome (driven off 1, beaten in a stand-up fight 0.5, break-in 2.5, overrun 4.5). It is spent in hits of 30–70 wear: on the outer line first (walls, gate, barricades, floodlights, watchtower: 85% of hits when driven off, 50% in a break-in, 35% in an overrun), the rest on buildings inside; after the first hit, 40% of hits fall on the building hit last. At 60 wear a building is damaged (a damaged wall section gives 2 wall strength, not 3) and may break any day; at 100 it is broken and does nothing until repaired. An overrun also tears down one broken building outright (60%; never the walls). Nobody repairs or builds while the base is on alert or under siege. See the repair table under Base defence.

Land, infection and the red skull Built

A hive holds the land within 4 tiles: sites there show at least 3 danger skulls. The ring 1 tile around it is red-skull ground: the skulls are drawn in blood red, and every one of the dead met there is +3 power (+1 elsewhere in the hive's land). That covers road surges, search sites, horde cards, growth guards and the hive's own brood. The hive radiates infection: 1.0 on its tile, 0.1 less per tile out to 6 tiles; tiles move toward that by +0.02 a day (and fall 0.01 a day).

Spread: build sites Built

Every 120–180 days a site for a new hive is picked (one site at a time on a Normal map; on bigger maps the days are divided by the map's area and more sites are allowed). The site is open ground 4–9 tiles from the nearest hive, not within 3 tiles of anyone's base, not within 4 of another site, weighted toward tiles many hives can support and toward infected ground.

  • Hives within 10 tiles become supporters: each sends up to 2 hordes there until the gathered and arriving dead cover 110% of what is missing. While they feed the site they send fewer roamers: a lull you can use.
  • Wandering hordes within 6 tiles of a site that still needs the dead are drawn to it (50% a day).
  • Hordes reaching the site merge into a mass. Once the delivered hordes add up to a score of 30, building starts and the site glows.
  • Each day a quarter of the mass (at least 25, at most 40) goes into the hive. The hive stands at 1,000 dead: about four weeks from the first gathering. Up to 150 left over stay as its guard; the rest roam.
  • A site nobody supports with nothing gathered is given up after 90 days.

Every 5–9 days a border horde also comes in from off the map (50% small, 35% medium, 15% large, × 1 + day / 730 up to ×1.5), so a state without hives is safer but never safe.

Growths: the hive tech tree Built

A hive grows structures on free land within 2 tiles of itself (not water, a bridge, a site, a ruin, a base, a hive or build site, or another growth). It grows one at a time, at least 12 days apart, at most 10 per hive. Each growth costs resources when started and takes days to finish. A growth has 100 flesh; fire burns it down and it regrows 5 a day. Growths you know of show on the map (small and faint while growing, grey husks once their hive is gone). One finished out of sight at a hive you know of reaches you as a rumour 40% of the time.

Hordes

Hordes are crowds of the dead that move on the State Map. Hives build them and give each one a job; border hordes wander in from off the map. A horde that reaches one of your bases attacks it, so reading, thinning and luring hordes is a constant strategic job.

Size and strength Built

A horde's score = size0.8 / 3.4: about 3 for a small horde, 8 for a medium one, 16 for a large one (20 at 200, 33 at 400). The score sets how tough it is in a fight and how much it adds to a new hive. On the map a horde is drawn with 2–7 figures by size (2 under 15, 3 under 25, 4 under 40, 5 under 60, 6 under 90, else 7), with its head count on a red tag, and a Wallbreaker at its head if it has one.

  • Build: walks to a build site and becomes part of its mass.

Movement and life Built

  • 12 hours a tile (2 tiles a day) over land, crossing rivers only at bridges. Weather slows them: ×0.5 in a blizzard or below −8 °C, ×0.7 below −2 °C, ×0.85 with snow cover; never below ×0.4. 10%+ runners: ×1.25.
  • Hordes on the same tile with the same errand merge (up to 600). A horde over 300 that wanders splits in half 10% of the time. Every part keeps at least 10; a horde under 5 is gone.
  • Wandering hordes (roam, border) rot away 1.2% a day (at least 1). Hordes with a job are kept up by their hive.
  • Each day a wanderer either drifts toward the loudest, closest people within 8 tiles or picks a spot up to 4 tiles away. The pull of people grows over the year: 15% of days at the start, 45% from about day 180. Your base's pull is its base heat / (1 + 0.35 × tiles): people there + 3 per manned station + 2 for a radio + 3 for the hub + a running generator (+4) + hunters' gunshots.
  • A horde entering a group's base fights its walls (size × 0.12 × 1.6 with a Wallbreaker × the special dead's wall weight): held, 45% of the horde is left; not held, 30%.

What hordes do to bases Built

When a visible horde heads for one of your bases and is within 6 tiles, the lookouts warn you once with a card: its size, score, special dead and Wallbreaker, and whether the base can hold it. On arrival the base's hold limit decides (see Base defence): held at the walls, a break-in battle, or overrun. Every horde attack spends 3 ammo per person at the base. Crawlers can slip past walls that held.

What hordes do to parties

A horde within 2 tiles adds to a team's chance of a road conflict; a team camped near one can be found by it (a surge scaled up to ×2.2 by the horde's size). AI war parties and convoys that walk into a horde lose a quarter of their strength.

Changed from Draft 1: Culling (killzones), diverting (fire lures, decoy fires, crash noise) and containment (walls, bridges as the only river crossings) are built. Planned: Draft 1's radio towers and car alarms as player-placed lures, blowing bridges, and "cleared zones stay safe only while patrolled".

Raids & sieges

Hostile groups and raiders from outside come for your bases. A raid party marches across the map, is spotted 4 tiles out, sits 8 hours at your walls and then the numbers (or your own battle) decide. Your decision is on the warning card: prepare, pay, fight in person, or give the base up.

Who raids and when Built

  • Only a hostile group raids you. Aggressive types roll every day once their cooldown (8–16 days) is over and they have 8+ people: raiders 10.5%, zealots 7.5%, expansionists 6%. A peaceful group you provoked into hostility raids only you, rarely: military 4.5%, fortress 3%, traders and communes 1.5%.
  • Reach: 30 tiles from the group's nearest base. They also raid other groups within 14 tiles.
  • Target weight for your base: 2.5 × greed (stock value / 1,500, 0.4–3) × land (+20% per outpost) × grudge (+50% per raid you beat or fight they lost, at most 4, fading 1% a day) × size (strength / 35, 0.6–2) ÷ (1 + tiles / 12).
  • Raid size: 0.6–1.0 × your base's defence as if everyone were at a post, × their escalation, at most the group's whole strength, at least 4. Every raid you drive off makes their next one 25% bigger (up to ×2); a raid that gets in resets it.
  • Outsider raiders: see Enemy types: the living. They attack when their strength is at least 0.6 of your base's defence.

The siege Built

A raid party reaching your base pitches a siege camp beside it (tents and a banner in the attacker's color, a spiked barricade, a fire). It stays 8 hours; a strong defence drives it off sooner: each hour counts as defence / strength / 1.2 hours (1 to 4). Building and repairs stop at a base under siege. Sieges between groups last 18 hours; you can answer a group's call for help with a relief squad (it fights beside them: +20 goodwill and 6 meds or 60 ammo if they hold) or by sending 40 ammo (the raid ×0.8, +6 goodwill); ignoring the call costs 8 goodwill (14 with a friend).

Tactical battles

Every fight with people or the dead can play out on a turn-based grid map in the style of XCOM: two actions per unit, cover, overwatch, line of sight and fog of war. The same battle can also be resolved instantly by the numbers (the Auto-resolve button before you act on turn 1, headless tools, the autopilot), and both paths give the same kind of result.

How a battle starts Built

  • Road conflict cards (fight back, open fire, ambush, take a side, a failed run, sneak or steal).
  • Engaging a horde, a hive (storm) or a growth's guard.
  • A rescue mission at a group's base, an assault on a group's base (conquest).
  • Defending a base in person against raiders, outsiders or a rescue raid; a horde breaking in; crawlers slipping inside.
  • Searching a site in the browser: raid mode (see Stealth).

The State Map clock pauses while a battle is open. Only one battle runs at a time; others wait in a queue. A battle whose team is gone is dropped.

The battle map Built

Ambush and the turn Built

If you have the drop on them, enemies start unaware in 1–3 clusters; gunfire wakes them (they cannot react that turn), melee is silent, and an unaware enemy notices a mover within its detection range. If they ambush you, each of their shooters fires an opening volley (+15) and your people start with 1 action. Enemy order: people first, then the dead.

Downed, bleeding and what happens after Built

  • A survivor at 0 health is down and bleeds out in 3 turns unless someone uses meds to stabilize them.
  • After a win the downed live if stabilized, else 50% + 2% per point of the best standing medic's Medicine. After a loss: 35% against people, 10% against the dead, 40% at your own base (+30% if stabilized). A Miracle Worker's patient always lives. Survivors come back with their battle health.
  • After a retreat the downed carried out (or lying on the strip) live if stabilized, else 50% + 2% per Medicine point of the medic who got out; the rest are left behind and lost (or taken prisoner by people: 50% in a break-off by the numbers).

Morale in battle Built

  • Friends alongside: +3 aim each (at most +6); rivals −2 each (at most −4); revenge on the group that killed a friend: +5. By the numbers: ±3% odds per friend or rival pair (−6% to +9%).
  • Brave +5 aim and melee aim, Coward −5.
  • Enemy people break and run when reduced to a third and outnumbered 2 to 1 (zealots never).
  • The player side's AI (auto turns, headless) sounds the retreat by style: Cautious below a strength ratio of 0.45 or with a third down and losing, Balanced 0.3 / half down, Aggressive 0.18 / two thirds down. Defending a base, every style holds on like Aggressive.

Planned: panic, suppression and morale breaks for your own people are not built.

Retreat and break-off Built

Sounding the retreat gives every standing unit an escape check: 100 − 3 a tile to the strip (4.5 carrying) − 25 per enemy next to them − (6 a shooter in sight + 4 a chaser within two moves, at most 35) − up to 30 for wounds − 2 per armor point + 1 per Scouting point + 10 at night + 20 in or near smoke − 10 carrying; 5–95%, or certain when nobody threatens them. Each downed teammate next to someone is carried by the one with the best odds. Caught: one blow of 18–32 per enemy next to them (at least one); at 0 they are down and left, otherwise they tear free. The battle ends as a retreat (a defeat if nobody got out).

Noise Built

Every shot adds its weapon's noise (a suppressor halves it). In an ordinary battle a loud shot wakes everyone; a muffled one only those within (9 + 2 × noise) × the muffled share. On infected ground (10%+), each enemy turn noise may draw more of the dead from the north, south or east edge: chance min(50%, infection × noise / 18), 1 + 0–round(infection) at a time, power 2 + 3 × infection, at most round(5 × infection) a battle. These reinforcements do not count toward "enemies killed" and attack anyone, raiders included.

Field Heat (on the map) Built

Stealth

Stealth lets a team pick its fights: slip past guards, take them down silently, lure them with noise, and leave with prisoners and loot before the alarm goes up. It matters most in raids (searching a site on the battle map) and against unaware enemies in any battle.

Detection Built

  • An unaware enemy notices one of your people within max(3, 8 − Stealth / 4) tiles with line of sight (the dead: at most 5). Light Step and Ghost perks shrink it (Ghost: at most 2).
  • People look the way they face: from behind they only hear you, at half the range. The dead have no front.
  • Night (raids and base battles): enemy sight 7, the dead 5; detection range ×0.6 (at least 2), +3 if you carry a flashlight. Your people see 7, 9 with a flashlight in the off hand, normally with night vision goggles or a thermal monocular; binoculars and scopes add range.
  • A guard that sees a body or a tied-up comrade within 5 tiles (3 at night) raises the alarm for its group (raid Heat +6).
  • Any hit that leaves a sleeper alive wakes its group, however quiet the weapon.

Base defence

Your bases are defended in three tiers: the wall, the yard and the buildings. Against a horde, the walls and defences first set a hold limit; a horde above it breaks in and is fought on the base's own battle map, built from what you have actually built. Raids by people use the same three tiers, by the numbers or in a battle you play.

The armory's defence kit Built

Roll of barbed wire

+5% hold a roll (up to 3); 4 wire cells a roll on the battle map stop the dead for the turn. One roll is torn up per horde attack. Yard tier +1.5% a roll.

Sandbags

+4% hold a bundle (up to 4); 3 half-cover cells a bundle on the battle map. Yard tier +1.5% a bundle.

Bear trap

Tier 1: the hold limit against hordes Built

Hold limit = (15 + 18 × wall strength + 2 × guard power) × (1 + barricades 12% each + trench 25% + gate 20% + lit floodlights 10% + wire 5% a roll + sandbags 4% a bundle) × Wallbreakers (one 60%, two 35%).

  • Wall strength: hub 3 + 3 a section (hold 69 / 123 / 177 / 231 for 0–3 sections); outpost 1 + 3 a section (33 / 87 / 141 / 195).
  • Guard power: the combat power of everyone on the Guard role (in bed: not counted); after "Man the defences" everyone else counts 0.8; with a tripwire alarm and no warning, 0.5.
  • Before the walls: spike pits (used up), then bear traps. Special dead press by their wall weight (the horde counts as size × the mix's weight). Brutes: every 12 smash a defence (at most 2, with the Wallbreakers).
  • Held (horde at or under the limit): 8 + 0.8 × guard power + 12% of the horde are cut down; if it was over 70% of the limit, 40% that someone takes 10–30. The horde falls back. Crawlers may still slip in.
  • Break-in: the walls still hold back half the limit; the rest (at least 30% of the horde) come inside. With nobody at an outpost it is wrecked (half the food, water and meds spoiled, buildings battered); with nobody at the hub it is overrun.

The break-in battle: wall map, then yard and buildings Built

The dead that broke in become 6–20 units (one per 7 inside), at power 1.5 + (inside) / (5 × units) + 0.5 × hive toughness (at most 6), with their special dead, their Wallbreakers and elite status. The map is turned so the attack comes from the side they came from on the State Map.

  • Deploy step: place your defenders by hand on the green cells (inside the walls, the walkway, the towers, the gate or a breach), aim the floodlights left, center or right, or take the default posts. Defaults: with 7+ defenders the toughest close fighter holds the gate (two with 9+), the best shots take the towers, the next best the walkway, close fighters wait ~3 tiles behind the openings, other shooters stand back ~8 tiles.
  • Tier 1, the wall map (18 × 13, wall at column 12): wall sections with hit points (a hub with one section 112, three 184; an outpost with one 88), a 2-cell gate (100 / 180) barred until battered open, the walkway behind (height 1) up ladders, towers, barricades behind, spike pits and the trench in front, wire, traps and sandbags from the armory. Breaches already open: +1 at 120 inside, +1 at 250, +1 per Wallbreaker (at most 4); the wall is pre-battered by 0.2% per dead inside (up to 85%). Whoever stands in the gate or a breach holds it. The dead batter the weakest point (a wall section broken becomes a breach and the walkway behind it falls).
  • Tier 2, the yard (26 × 16): when 3+ attackers (or a quarter of them) are inside the wall at the start of your turn, or any are and nobody holds the wall line, or you order "Fall back", the fight moves to the yard and buildings map. Everyone keeps health, ammo and roughly their position; a short redeploy follows (within 12 tiles of where they stood, at least 3 from an attacker). The attackers still outside come in at 3 + 2 per opening a turn. The yard has your real buildings, barricades, sandbags, wells, tanks and stacks as cover.
  • Tier 3, the buildings: once attackers in the yard are 1.5× your people still outside the buildings (at least 3), the yard is lost: last stand indoors. Doors have 45 hit points (60 wide), windows 20; bigger buildings have an upper floor to shoot down from, flat roofs a ladder.
  • Way out: on the wall map the back gate lets one person slip away; sounding the retreat there means falling back. On the yard map the evac strip abandons the base: against the dead your people hide outside while the dead tear through (half the food, water and meds spoiled, damage as a break-in) and come back after.

Results: held, the horde loses the dead that broke in, its Wallbreakers die, it falls back and one barricade is wrecked. Lost: overrun: each person at the base has a 40% chance of 20–60 damage, a third of the food, water and meds spoiled, an outpost lost; a hub with nobody left alive ends the game.

Garrisons Built

Anyone on the Guard role counts fully on the walls against hordes (2 hold per combat power) and 1.5× against raids; they also keep prisoners from escaping. Everyone else counts only when the base is warned (0.8× against hordes when manned, 1.3× against raids when prepared). Bases you took from groups and left empty are land you hold: raiders loot them and the dead wreck them, but they stay yours.

Captives & prisoners

People knocked out in a won fight become prisoners: a source of intel, ransom, recruits, or trouble. Your own people can be taken by other groups too, and getting them back is its own mission.

Taking prisoners Built

  • Anyone knocked out (takedowns, Subdue, a non-lethal blow) is a prisoner after a won fight (in a raid: tied up and dragged into the zone you leave from). You can choose to leave them tied up behind (+2 goodwill with their group).
  • Fights by the numbers take none, except the Aggressive autopilot wanting intel. Defenders who surrender when you take a group's base become prisoners there (a fifth of those who give up, at most 3).
  • On the road they walk with the team: each slows a team on foot 10% (at least 60% of its pace; in a vehicle they ride), eats 1 food and 1 water a day, and slips away 5% a day (2% in a vehicle).
  • At a base they go to the Holding cells (3 per cell building, up to 2 buildings: 16 scrap, 1 part, 20 hours); the rest sit in a shed. A day costs 1 food and 1 water each (water × the day's thirst). Escapes a day: in a cell with someone on Guard 0.4%, unguarded 3%, in the shed 8%, +4% unfed; six unfed days in a row and they die.

Rescue raids on your base Built

A group may come for its people: 1.2% a day per prisoner it has in a base (×1.5 aggressive, ×2 after a refused ransom, ×0.3 friendly; it needs 6+ people and a base within 25 tiles). It arrives 10 hours after the warning: defend in person (the deploy battle), man the walls (by the numbers, your defence ×1.3), or hand them over (goodwill +3). Lost: every prisoner of theirs there is freed and they carry off a tenth of the stores.

Conflicts on the road

A team travelling the State Map can run into people or a surge of the dead. A perception roll decides who sees whom first: forced encounters (they found you) give limited, tense options; observed ones (you saw them first) let you watch, slip away, steal, ambush or take a side. Every option shows its odds on the card.

When a conflict happens Built

  • Rolled for each tile a team enters (never within 1 tile of your base; at most one per team every 20 hours). Chance = 0.1 × people nearby + 0.06 × the dead + 0.08 × traffic, ×1.25 at night, ×0.75 stealthy / ×1.2 rushed, at most 35%.
  • People nearby: each group with a base within 5 tiles (hostile 1, aggressive 0.5, military or fortress 0.3, others 0.12; ×0.5 with a pact; less with distance; ×1.4 on its land), +0.6 per AI party within a tile. The dead: 2 × (infection − 0.25) + 0.4 with a horde within 2 tiles.
  • Spotting first: the team's perception + 10%, +25% stealthy, −10% rushed, −10% in a vehicle, − fog, +15% at night with a Night Owl or night vision, + optics; 5–90%.
  • Teams that stay put trigger the same cards through Field Heat.

Skill checks use (best skill + 10 − DC + 1) / 10, clamped 5–95%: a skill of 5 against DC 10 is 60%.
