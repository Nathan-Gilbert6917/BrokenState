Overview and design pillars

Broken State is a top-down zombie survival strategy game. You lead a holdout of survivors across the procedurally generated State of Calder. You run the hub like RimWorld, deal with other groups like a 4X game, and send teams on raids where every person and every bullet counts. This chapter describes the game as the browser prototype builds it today (October 2026), not as Draft 1 imagined it.

Scale

  • Your group: six starting colonists. Population grows through recruits, refugees, captives won over and outsiders who stay. In the 100-run balance census the median peak population was 17 (Cautious autopilot), 23 (Balanced) and 29 (Aggressive). Draft 1 aimed for 25–50.
  • AI groups: 3–18, chosen on the new-run screen. The range follows the world size (Small 3–6, Normal 5–10, Large 7–14, Huge 10–18), and there are 7 group types. Aggressive groups recruit drifters while they have fewer than 45 people, and expand to up to 2 + people/10 bases (at most 6; other types at most 4).
  • World: four sizes, from 48 × 32 hexes (1,536 tiles) to 100 × 66 (6,600 tiles). Normal is 64 × 42 (2,688 tiles) with about 186 sites. See Starting a game.
  • Threats: 5–6 infestation hives at the start on a Normal map. They grow to hundreds of the dead per horde and build new hives, while outsiders keep crossing the border.

Design pillars

People are the resource Built

Every job draws on the same people: farming, operating stations, guarding, teaching, reading, travelling, raiding and defending. Work priorities and crews decide who does what each hour.

Every trip costs something Built

Food and water drain hour by hour (more in summer heat and under a heavy load). Cars burn fuel by the tile and wear down. The weather slows you, and each tile you enter can bring an event card or a conflict.

Greed has a timer Built

Raid Heat rises with every turn and every noise, and each stage brings more of the dead. Field Heat builds while a team stays on one tile. Extract in time, or lose everyone still on the map.

The state lives without you Built

Groups scavenge the same finite sites, trade, go to war, take and raze each other's bases, and collapse. Hives grow and spread, and raiders, merchants and travellers cross the border. You see this only through your bases, your teams and the radio.

Loss creates goals Built

The dead leave death sites with their gear, and passers-by strip them. Abandoned vehicles wait to be recovered. Lost bases can be retaken and captives rescued. Ruins can be rebuilt for 5 scrap.

A new state every run Built

Each seed builds a new map, outbreak origin, set of groups and year of weather. The same seed always plays out the same way, and a separate random stream keeps the weather, rumours and hive choices from shifting the rest of the world.

Information is old Built

Geography is known, current conditions are not. You see live only what your bases, watchtowers and teams in radio contact see. Everything else is a snapshot with its age, and some sites stay hidden until someone finds them.

Setting

The game is set in the fictional State of Calder, six years after an outbreak collapsed the United States. Calder is a composite of American regions: familiar enough to read at a glance, and free of any need for accuracy. The tone is grounded and bleak with moments of warmth, closer to The Walking Dead and State of Decay than to camp horror.

The land

  • North: highlands. Elevation is biased northward, so the hills and mountains gather in the north, and every river rises there and runs south to the map's edge (3–4 rivers on a Normal map, more on wider ones).
  • Centre: farmland, forest and river valleys, with small towns spread at least 8 tiles apart.
  • South: Calder City, a cluster of about 50 city and suburb tiles. A smaller second city sits far from it (Granite City, Fairmont, Kingsbridge, Port Ashby or Easton). Large and Huge maps add a third and fourth city.
  • Landmarks: at least one National Guard base (sometimes an Army depot too) off-road in the hills or woods, a state prison, a dam and power plant on a river, a regional airport near Calder City, and factories near towns.
  • Towns: 13 on a Normal map, with names such as Harlow, Pine Ridge, Millbrook, Cedar Falls, Dunmore, Stillwater and Cold Spring. Each has a police station, an auto shop and a few shops.
Changed from Draft 1: there is no coastline, lake shore, rail line or jammed interstate site, and no stadium or grain elevator hubs. The jammed highway survives as the "Blocked highway" road event. The hub always starts in a small town, as "<Town> Holdout".

The outbreak

Each map picks an outbreak origin at random from its city centres, the university, the National Guard bases and the hospitals. Infection peaks at 100% there and fades over 30 tiles. The other city gets its own hot spot (80%, over 12 tiles), and Large and Huge maps add hot spots at far-off landmarks (75%, over 10 tiles). City tiles get +15% infection and suburbs +6%, and nowhere is below 5%. Four infestation hives stand around the origin from day 1 (3 on Small, 5 on Huge), plus one at each other hot spot (three at each extra hot spot on Large and Huge maps). The hub is placed far from all of it.

The dead are not one kind. Walkers come first. As the hives grow, they breed runners, brutes, spitters, screamers and crawlers, and finally the Wallbreaker, a boss that smashes walls. See the chapter on the dead.

What the dead are: the worm Lore

Nobody in Calder turns from a bite or a scratch. The dead carry a parasite: a pale tapeworm that survivors simply call the worm.

  • Eggs in the land. The dead shed its eggs by the million in everything that leaks out of them. The eggs lie in the soil and the water for months. A tile's Infection is how thick they lie there, which is why the red wash spreads from the hives and fades once a hive is gone.
  • How people catch it. By swallowing the eggs: water drunk unboiled from an infected stream, greens and roots grown or gathered on infected ground, game that grazed there and was not cooked through, hands that handled the dead and then a meal.
  • The gut stage. The eggs hatch and the worm grows in the gut for weeks. The carrier eats like two people and still gets thinner, and pale segments show in the stool. Strong medicine can purge it at this stage.
  • Turning. Left alone, its larvae leave the gut, travel in the blood and lodge in the muscles and the brain. When enough reach the brain the person turns. What walks afterwards is the worm driving a body: always hungry, and spreading eggs wherever it goes. A carrier who dies of anything else rises soon after, because the worm does not need its host alive. Someone who was clean stays dead, which is why most bodies on the road are only bodies.
  • The bite myth. In the first weeks everyone believed the films. Bitten people did turn, because they had drunk the same water as everyone else, and a bite from a rotten mouth often goes bad. An infected wound can kill you, but it never makes you one of them. A lot of arms were cut off and a lot of friends shot for nothing, and people still check each other for bites out of habit.
  • The hives. Where the dead crowd together, the worms find each other. A hive is a colony: bodies fused around a mass of worm grown far past any one host. The Broodmother is its queen, a host swollen around the colony's breeding worm with her back hung with egg sacs. Its growths and its special dead are the colony reshaping its hosts. Kill her and the colony dies.
  • Heat kills the eggs. Boiled water and meat cooked through are safe, and fire is the one thing that cleans a hive's ground.

In the game now Built (the rules: The worm under Health):

  • Catching it from water drunk unboiled from infected ground (a base's wells, springs and streams; a river a team fetches from), from game, fish and snares taken on infected land and eaten raw, from crops grown and food foraged on land at 40%+ Infection and eaten raw, from camping on badly infested land, and from the hands that search a site on infected ground (washed by a fire that night or safe water at home). Below 10% Infection the land is clean enough. Rain water is clean.
  • Heat kills the eggs: a Water boiler (0.2 wood a head a day while the water has eggs, cold and unsafe when the base cannot pay) or a water filter makes a base's water safe, a Cook fire (or a preserving kitchen) cooks and boils everything a base eats, drying racks and a smoke house dry the infected meat first, a camp fire boils a team's water and cooks its game and forage, and a shelter's fire does both.
  • A hidden week, then the gut stage (an extra ration a day, a slow loss of health), which a medic at a base spots (better Medicine sooner) and a team notices later (the The worm card, now driven by the infection itself, not a roll of the dice). A dewormer (new loot, and a med-station recipe) cures it for sure; a purge with meds usually does.
  • The brain stage: a few days of fits, then they turn. Only surgery in a staffed Clinic saves them. Unseen at a base they go for the nearest people; locked away, nobody is hurt.
  • Infested animals: game on badly infected land catches it too. Herds of grey, gaunt deer and hogs wander out of the dead land and seed the ground they pass with eggs. They do not attack; hunting them gives a quarter of the meat, all of it with eggs, and thins the herd.
  • The other groups catch it the same way: a base on infected ground loses people at the rate a base of yours that size would (less the half they cure, by type: military and fortress hubs boil their water, communes farm the infected land), boils its water after its first loss, and never falls below 4 people to it; parties on the move lose one now and then on a herd's land. You hear of it on the radio and read it in the chronicle.
  • Bites in battle do damage and can leave a wound that gets infected, as before. Carriers rising after they die of something else: built (the Carrier, under Threats).

Core loop and campaign arc

The game runs on loops at several time scales, all sharing one clock and one small pool of people. A raid feeds the hub, the hub equips the next trip, trips find groups to trade with or fight, and the hives and outsiders make sure the pressure keeps growing.

A typical day

  1. Morning: the alerts stack shows what needs you (a team waiting for orders, low water, idle stations, a horde near a base). The forecast says whether to travel today.
  2. A team leaves with a plan: go to a site, search it, maybe search the best sites nearby, then come home. Packed supplies and weight decide its pace.
  3. On the road, the team raises cards: a storm, the dead ahead, stranded traders, a camp by the road. A card stops the clock until you answer.
  4. At the site you play the raid on its own map, or let it run as an hour-by-hour search.
  5. Back at the hub, the haul goes into stock. People heal, eat, gain XP, quarrel and make friends. Crops grow and stations produce.
  6. Now and then something comes to you: a raid warning, a horde heading for the walls, a merchant at the gate, a helicopter coming down.

How pacing works

  • Interruptions: every card stops the clock in the browser. The pacing review (42 runs) counted about 2.4 cards a day overall (1.4 Cautious, 2.1 Balanced, 3.6 Aggressive). 57% of cards come from teams on the road, so the more teams you send out, the more cards you get. At today's 8 minutes a day at 1×, that is about one card every 3–4 real minutes.
  • Seasonal rhythm: summers are busiest (about 3 cards a day), winters quietest (about 1.5). Teams stay home in storms and blizzards.
  • Escalation on the world's clock: hive tiers open by calendar day and hive age, new hive sites come every 120–180 days, border hordes every 5–9 days, outsider raider bands more often as the year goes on, and late gangs settle when fewer than two aggressive groups are left. The pressure grows whatever you do. Turtling was the losing strategy in every census.
  • Lulls you can use: while nearby hives feed a build site, they send fewer hordes. A horde beaten off at the walls stays away from that base for 10 days.
Design intent: a calm build-up, then a rising RimWorld-style rhythm of raids and hordes, with real decisions concentrated in people cards, conflicts, crash sites, radio and jobs (pacing review, "GREAT" cards).

Engagements and the shared clock

Tactical battles and interactive raids are turn-based, and the State Map waits while they are open. When a raid ends, the hours it took (about 6 turns an hour) are played on the map clock straight away, so the world catches up. The weather of every future day is fixed ahead from the seed, so the planner's ETAs, the queue and the estimated position of an out-of-contact team all use the hours the team will really live through.

Timelapse Built

The whole world is recorded from day 1: one frame a day (every 6 hours for the first 3 days, plus a frame a few hours after a death or a base gained or lost). A frame holds tile owners and infection, every base, horde, hive and party, your population and hub stocks, and captions, including what the fog hid from you (groups taking sites, sieges, hives rising). The cost is about 0.25 ms a frame and 300 KB a year. The viewer fits the whole map with the fog off and plays at 1, 2, 5 or 10 days a second, with a scrubber, event ticks, charts and infection and territory toggles. It opens from the victory and game-over screens and from the dev panel.

Starting colonists

  • Six colonists. Each has nine skills from 0 to 20 (a random spread of 0–9 plus one or two specialties of +3–7) and one trait (60%) or two. The first is generated a bit stronger (×1.3) and leads by default.
  • Per colonist you can rename them (or roll a random name), move skill points within a fixed point total, edit or randomize their look, randomize name, skills, look or all, and make them the leader. Next to a skill, the screen shows what it counts as in play when a trait changes it ("5 →3" for Stealth with Loud).
  • No two colonists share a first name while a free one is left, and renaming someone to a full name already in the group is refused.
  • Starting gear: pistol, machete, hunting rifle, knife, knife, pistol (in crew order), and the first colonist wears a ballistic vest.
  • Starting jobs are set by skill when the run begins: the two best farmers farm, the best scavenger scavenges, the best shot guards, the best mechanic builds and the sixth farms. The ammo bench starts with no operator.

Choosing a leader

The leader is an ordinary survivor who works, travels and can die. Their Leadership lifts morale at their base and they earn Leadership XP each day. If the leader dies, a card offers the six best free survivors by Leadership, and everyone loses 15 mood. A daily check asks again if the group is ever left with no leader.

Goals, victory and loss

There are two ways to win: destroy every hive, or have the Calder Coalition hold the state. Each can be won once. After the first victory you can keep playing for the other. The run is lost only when every survivor is dead or gone. Between the start and either ending, the game sets goals through the Goals card, the seasons and the threats.

Victory conditions

Every infestation hive is destroyed and no new hive is being built (a build site where the dead have started gathering counts as being built). This is checked every hour and the moment a hive dies. The win line reads "Every hive is destroyed. The State of Calder is not safe, but it is yours to win back."

The holdout has fallen Built

  • Seasonal goals: stock food for winter (the food forecast shows days left and the next harvest), store water before summer (everyone drinks 30% more, 60% in a heat wave), harvest and hunt in autumn.
  • Threat goals: burn hive growths before they breed special dead, thin the mass at a hive build site during its four-week window, man the defences when a horde is spotted, retake lost bases soon after they fall (they are patched up 5% a day).

The State Map

The State Map is the strategy layer: a hex map of the whole State of Calder where teams, convoys, AI parties, outsiders and hordes move tile by tile on one clock. Each tile holds its terrain, roads, rivers, site, loot left, infection, owner and hidden find. What you see of it depends on where your people are and how old their news is.

Generation Built

A new map is made per seed (mapgen.js), in this order:

  1. Terrain from two layers of smooth noise for elevation (biased higher in the north) and one for moisture: mountains above 0.78, hills above 0.63, then forest where it is moist, farmland everywhere else.
  2. Rivers (3–4 on Normal, scaled by width) start in the northern hills and mountains, at least 12 tiles apart, and run downhill to the south edge. River tiles become water.
  3. Cities: Calder City in the south (radius 4: a city core with suburbs around it), then the second city (radius 3) at least 24 tiles away (18 or 12 if there is no room). Each city is filled with urban sites in a fixed order (police first, then auto shop, hospital, pharmacy…) and named by district ("Calder City North Precinct", "Calder City Riverside Pharmacy").
  4. Towns (13 on Normal): at least 8 tiles apart and 9 from a city. Each gets a police station and an auto shop next to its centre, 0–2 extra shops, and 1–2 satellites 2–3 tiles out.
  5. Landmarks: National Guard base(s) off-road in the woods, at least 12 from a city and 5 from a town (a 50% chance of an Army depot as well), a state prison, a dam beside a river, an airport 5–12 tiles from a city, and 2 factories 2–4 tiles from a town.
  6. Countryside sites, at least 3 tiles from other sites: 12 farms, 6 barns, 4 ranches, 3 country churches, 7 cabins, 3 lodges, 4 campgrounds, 3 ranger stations, 3 radio towers, 2 quarries and 2 old crash sites (counts for Normal, scaled by area).
  7. Roads (below), then 3 truck stops and 4 gas stations along the highways.
  8. Minor loot on open ground: city 0.4, suburb 0.3, farmland 0.12, forest and hills 0.08.
  9. Hidden finds: a wrecked car on 3.5% of road tiles, a cache (60%) or abandoned camp on 3% of forest and hills tiles, and a random find on 1.2% of the rest.
  10. Infection and hives around the outbreak origin and hot spots (see Setting).

A Normal map draws exactly the random numbers it always has, so old seeds and saves build the same world. Every tile's decals come from a separate per-tile seed, so drawing the map never changes the simulation.

Roads, rivers, bridges and crossings

  • Highways join every city, town and big landmark (but not the military bases) through a spanning tree routed by terrain cost (road 0.4, farmland, suburb and city 1, forest 2, hills 3, water 7, mountains 9, military tiles 14). Shortcuts are added where the roads between two neighbouring settlements make a detour longer than 1.8× the straight distance.
  • Streets: every city and town site is joined to the nearest road within 4 tiles.
  • Dirt spurs: a countryside site gets a road to the nearest road within 3 tiles only some of the time: farms and ranches 60%, country churches 70%, lodges 50%, campgrounds and ranger stations 60%, quarries 70%, and never cabins, barns, radio towers or crash sites. The rest stay off-road, and many of those are hidden from the map.
  • Bridges: where a road crosses a river, the water tile becomes a bridge, drawn as a railed deck along the road. Hordes cross rivers only at bridges.
  • Boat crossings Built: a team on a shore can build a one-use boat and cross one water tile to the land straight across. See Travel.

Towns, cities and sites on the map

  • Each site is a pixel sprite of its type on a plate in its zone's colour: city (salmon), small town (amber), countryside (green), landmark (violet).
  • The sprite fades in steps by the loot left as you last saw it: rich (60%+ of what it held untouched), some (30%+), little (10%+), picked clean.
  • Zoomed in, a row of skulls under the name shows the site's danger. On a hive's land every site shows at least 3 skulls, and the ring 1 tile around a hive shows a red skull on every tile.
  • Zoomed out, small sites become dots. Only cities, towns, landmarks, group bases and your own bases keep their names, and where names overlap the more important one wins. You can zoom out until the whole state fits.
  • The tile panel and tooltip show the site's zone, danger, likely loot, and the loot left as you know it ("now" when in view, otherwise its age, with a warning once it is 3 days old, because other groups strip sites too).

Fog of war and the known map

  • Geography is known, current conditions are not. Terrain, roads and public sites are on the map from day 1. Hordes, loot, owners, hives and hidden sites are known only as snapshots.
  • Live view covers 3 tiles around the hub and 2 around an outpost, +2 with a watchtower, and +1 with a telescope in the armory (+2 with a watchtower). It also covers what a team in radio contact sees right now.
  • A team's sight is 1 tile, +1 with Scouting 10+, +1 with a Keen Eye, +1 with the Lookout perk, plus optics (binoculars +1 by day, a thermal +1 at night and in fog, a spotting scope only while the team stays put). Fog and blizzards cut it by 1, but never below 1.
  • Reports: out of radio contact, everything a team sees is held until it gets home, and each tile is stamped with the hour it was seen, not the hour the team returned. Radio news, traders, explorers' maps, coalition members and prisoners questioned also put snapshots on your map.
  • Shading: live tiles are full brightness, reported ones are dimmer (0.72) and tiles you have no news of are darkest (0.42). A horde last seen is a faded "N?" ghost.
  • Radio coverage: a station covers 8 tiles, and a relay at an outpost 6, linked in a chain. Coverage circles are drawn on the map (red when not linked or off the air).
Changed from Draft 1: intel does not fade back toward fog as it ages. Instead each snapshot shows its age and warns once it is stale. Dead teams' notes are not recoverable from their death site.

Forecast

Summer water Built

  • Water a person drinks a day = 1 × the season's thirst (summer 1.3, winter 0.9), and at least 1.6 on a hot day (a heat wave, or a high of 30 °C or more).
  • The same factor applies at every base, to teams in the field (hour by hour, with the load bands on top), to prisoners and guests, and to every estimate. The planner packs more for a trip into a heat wave.
  • Rain catchers, wells and cisterns at bases read the weather. Wells give 60% in a dry spell.
  • The autopilot plans water from three weeks before summer. In the old census, thirst peaked in summer (803 thirsty run-days against 214 in spring) and was the #2 killer. In the latest census it is 4% of deaths.

Other weather hooks

  • Farming: a daily growth factor (0–1.3) per crop from season, temperature, soil and sky, and death and rot chances for each crop (frost, hard freeze, blizzard, drought, heat, storm, wet, snow).   • Breakdowns: well pumps wear +2.5 on a frost day and +4 in a storm, radio masts +6 in a storm, storehouse roofs +8 in a storm and +1 in rain.
  • The map: the Weather layer washes the land white with snow, dark with rain, grey with fog and warm in heat. Season changes and severe weather are logged.
  • The autopilot launches no trip on a storm or blizzard day, and no trip longer than a day with one forecast in the next two.

It adds to the dead at the site: a raid fields 1 + infection × 5 (+1 on a city tile) + danger (+2 on hive land), give or take 1, at most 14. Each one has power 2 + infection × 3, plus the hive land's toughness. A site at a group's base adds 3–7 of its guards. On hostile land there is a 40% chance of a patrol of 2–3, and otherwise a 15% chance of 2 rival scavengers.

Groups within 22 tiles race you for it: raiders 75%, military 85%, expansionists 65%, zealots 50%, fortresses 40%, traders 30%, communes 20%. Scavenging

Arriving at a site with the Scavenge / raid mission opens one of two ways to search it.

The interactive raid Built

A turn-based raid on a 28 × 18 map built from the site's areas. Search containers next to you (S) for loot from that area's table, scaled by how picked over the site is and by Scavenging. What does not fit stays inside. Guards patrol and the dead wander, unaware until they spot you (Stealth) or hear gunfire. Heat rises each turn and with noise, and each stage brings more of the dead. Leave from a green extraction zone (X). A vehicle you brought waits in its own zone, and extracting anywhere else leaves it at the site, marked. With nobody out, everyone left on the map is lost. Each container searched lowers the site's loot by 0.07, and the raid's hours then pass on the map clock. See the battles chapter for the raid rules.

The hour-by-hour search Built

The abstract search used by crews, the autopilot and headless tools. You pick an approach, and each hour rolls finds, Heat and trouble:

  • Supplies: each roll picks a supply by the site's weights. The amount is 4–9 × loot left × approach × (1 + Scavenging/15), × 6 for ammo, × 2.5 for scrap, × 2 for jars and × 0.7 for fuel (fuel is scarce on purpose).
  • Gear: 7% × loot × (1 + Scavenging/15) × approach per hour, from the site's gear list. Books, small finds (valuables, comforts, smokes, throwables), hunting finds (snare wire, rods, nets), keys, locks and blueprints each roll on their own dice.
  • Heat is the approach's rate × (0.6 + 0.6 × infection), −2 at night, with camp kit and the Quiet Hands perk (half) reducing it. The stages are Quiet (0), Stirring (25), Swarming (50) and Overrun (75), with a 5 / 15 / 35 / 60% chance of a fight each hour (+15% at a guarded base, −30% with Sixth Sense).
  • Depletion: each hour lowers the site's loot by 0.05 × approach (×0.6 with the Salvager perk), down to a floor of 0.03.

Loot that fits the place (the loot overhaul) Built

Every site type, raid area and item carries theme tags (home, shop, police, military, medical, hunt, crime and so on). A test checks that nothing turns up where it does not belong: no military radios in a university lab, no sledgehammers in a pharmacy. Locked containers hold more (supplies ×1.5) and blueprints are 4× as likely in them. Keys are named for a site and hang on the hub's key ring.

Depletion: sites run dry Built

  • Site loot is one finite stock for everyone, and nothing grows back after the map is made. The only new loot comes from crash sites and drops, which burn out. A shore's driftwood washes up again at 1 scrap every 72 hours.
  • Other groups scavenge too: each group with 6+ people sends one party at a time, every 6–10 days when it is short of something. The party goes to the best site within 10 tiles of its bases, searches for 8 hours (0.03 loot an hour × strength ÷ 3) and walks home. Sites inside a group's own land lose 0.3% of their loot a day into its stock.
  • Your team at a site a group party is working gets a Contested site card. Win the fight and they leave, dropping their haul.
  • You learn a site was stripped only when you see it, or hear on the radio: "Red Hand has been stripping the Calder Mall" (needs a radio station and the site within 15 tiles of your bases).
  • Measured over a year: about 68% of site loot is left on day 120, 54% on day 240 and 48% on day 365 (93% without scavenging groups).

Hidden off-road sites and discovery Built

Only what a normal person knew before the fall is on the map from day 1: towns and cities with every shop and service in them, truck stops, landmarks and the National Guard bases. Farms, barns, ranches, cabins, lodges, campgrounds, ranger stations, radio towers and quarries are public only within 1 tile of a road. Old crash sites and the wrecks of crash events are always secret. A hidden site is not drawn and shows as bare ground. You cannot scavenge it or plan for it, and crews and the autopilot never pick it. That lasts until your people have intel on its tile: seen from a base or watchtower, seen by a team, or reported (radio news, an explorer's map, bought intel, a job reward, a prisoner questioned, the coalition). Once found, a site stays on the map for good. About 14–19% of sites are hidden: 18 of 128 on Small, 33 of 186 on Normal, 54 of 282 on Large and 82 of 419 on Huge, with about 5 within 12 tiles of the hub. Each find gets a log line, for example "🧭 Move here team (Cole, June) found a hunting lodge deep in the woods: Pine Hollow Hunting lodge."

Survey missions and optics Built

The Survey mission sweeps the land around a tile (radius 2–4, 6–48 hours) for hidden places and finds. Its route is worked out when it starts: each next spot clears the most ground not yet looked at, the route always ends back at the centre, and the estimates are exact. A step takes 2 hours on foot (×1.4 in hills, forest and city, ×2 in mountains, ×0.7 on a road), 1.5 hours on bikes and 1 by car, ×1.35 stealthy and ×0.8 rushed.

Each hour it rolls for every unknown hidden site within reach: 12% × Scouting (0.5 + 0.05 a point; +0.15 with Keen Eye or Spotter) × team size (+8% a person, at most ×1.3) × ground (farmland ×1.35, suburbs ×1, city ×0.85, hills ×0.7, forest ×0.6, mountains ×0.55) × distance beyond the optics' reach (0–1 tiles ×1.6, 2 ×1, 3 ×0.45, 4 ×0.18, 5 ×0.06) × night ×0.35 × weather (fog ×0.5, blizzard ×0.4, storm ×0.55, rain ×0.85, snow ×0.8) × car ×0.6 or bikes ×0.8 × stealthy ×1.2 or rushed ×0.75 × optics × a rumour ×3, at most 80% an hour. Heat builds at half the rate of standing still. An hour on hive land has a 3% chance (8% on the red-skull ring) that the dead find the team.

Rumours: radio chatter (5% a day with a station), travellers and merchants at the gate (+15% a day each) and other groups' news speak of an unfound hidden place within 30 tiles. It shows as a dashed amber circle of 3 tiles with a vague place ("a hunting lodge somewhere north of Dunmore") for 20 days, at most three at once. A survey centred in it has ×3 odds for that place.

Travel and expeditions

Everything that leaves a base is a party on the State Map: your teams, trade-route convoys, supply runs, trade caravans, and the AI groups' raids, convoys and scavengers. Travel is stepped hour by hour through the terrain, the roads, the weather and the team's load. Each tile entered can bring an event or a conflict, and every hour costs food and water.

The planner shows each person's load, band, pace and rations, the car's load % and fuel multiplier, and warns about anyone Heavy or Overloaded. A launch over the carry limit is refused.
  • Plan section: the order queue the team runs by itself. It starts with the trip and its mission, followed by steps: look around, scavenge here, scavenge the best N sites within R tiles (picked when the step starts), camp N hours, gather, fetch water (2 h by a river: every free container filled), wait, move to a known site, survey the area, hunt, fish, set or check snares, and return home.
  • Presets: Recon and return, Scavenge and return, Survey trip, Explore (recon, then the best 2 sites within 3 tiles, then home), Camp N days then return, Hunting trip, Fishing trip, Custom.
  • Estimates step every leg hour by hour like the real trip (searches count 6 hours, Look around 2), including the weather ahead and the load. The planner packs food, water and fuel for the plan and shows a Heat look-ahead for camp and wait steps.
  • "If … then come home" rules: anyone hurt below N health, food below N days, or cargo full. These are checked every hour, and a "Coming home" card says why the team turned back.
  • Plan cards (once each per trip): a horde of 15+ in sight, a rich site in sight that is not on the plan, food short of the way home.

Supplies and weight on the road

  • Food: one ration per person per day, eaten hour by hour (fresh game first, then packed food, preserved food, then found food). Out of food: −0.6 health an hour per person (raw damage: armour and Tough do not help).
  • Water: one per person per day × the day's thirst (summer 1.3, hot day 1.6, winter 0.9). Out of water: −1 health an hour.
  • Load bands by felt load: Light ≤ 18 kg (full pace), Medium ≤ 28 (−10% pace, +15% food and water), Heavy ≤ 38 (−25%, +35%), Overloaded above that (−45%, +60%). Max load is 40 kg (28 with a Bad Back), plus the pack's carry, plus 8 with Packrat. The team shares its load out evenly and moves at its slowest member's pace. Extra rations count only on the move.
  • Weights: food 0.5 kg, water 1, fuel 1, meds 0.1, ammo 0.02, scrap and parts 2, preserved food 0.35, jars 0.4. Fresh game weighs 1 kg a ration.
  • A team that loses people drops what the rest cannot carry on the spot (marked as "Cargo left behind"). A team whose last member dies leaves a death site with everything it carried, and its vehicle stands abandoned there.
Changed from Draft 1: there is no medical, ammo or parts drain table by distance. Meds are used for treatment and events, ammo in fights, and parts for repairs and breakdown cards. Draft 1's horses, foraging along the way and supply caches on common routes are not built. Fishing, hunting and the fetch-water step are the built ways to live off the land.

Road events Built

Each tile a team enters (away from your bases) rolls, in order:

  1. A horde on the tile always forces a Horde of N card.
  2. A worn vehicle may break down.
  3. A conflict with people or a surge of the dead (below).
  4. A travel event with a chance of 5% + infection × 10% + traffic × 40% (+12% on hostile land, +6% on other owned land), × stance × 1.2 at night × 1.15 in storms and blizzards.

There is one card per team per hour. A card is dropped if its team is gone or the person it is about has died or left. Costs are checked against what the team carries when you choose. Skill checks succeed with (best skill + 11 − difficulty)/10, between 5% and 95%.

Hazards

Blocked highway (cars on roads): clear a path (4 h, noisy, 30% chance the dead come), detour off-road (3 fuel), or squeeze through (Mechanics 8). Storm: shelter 6 h, or push through (40% chance of a fall, 5–20 damage). Bad footing (on foot in hills, forest or mountains): treat it (1 meds, 1 h) or keep moving (8–18 damage). Breakdown (by condition).

The dead

The dead ahead (infection 20%+; 4 + infection × 14 of them, +4 at night): sneak around (Stealth check), fight (2 ammo each) or go the long way (3 h). Bitten (infection 40%+): amputate (2 meds, Medicine 10), end it, or hope (75% they turn). A hive (within 2 tiles of one): set fires at its edge (5 fuel, Stealth 10) or mark it. Horde of N: hide 6 h (Stealth against the horde's score), outrun it (safe by car with 4 fuel; a coin flip on foot), or fight (40 ammo, 1–4 rounds of wounds; the team may break off by its retreat policy).

People

Stranded traders: help (2 h, 2 meds, +5 goodwill with traders), trade 10 food for 3 parts, rob them (−12 goodwill, −4 mood) or leave. A survivor alone: a recruit card. <Group> patrol (on a non-hostile group's land): share news (+2 goodwill, they mark their bases) or gift 5 food (+6).

Discoveries

Abandoned supply truck (roads): search it (2 h; 12 food, 5 fuel, 15 scrap, 3 meds or 30 ammo; 20% something waits inside). Radio signal: a group's bases are noted. Crashed military vehicle (infection 30%+): search it (Stealth 11) for military gear and 30 ammo. A dead hiker (open ground): a hiking pack or rucksack with 1–3 items inside. Prepper cache (forest or hills): 8 food, 2 meds, 20 ammo.

Crew

Tempers flare (more often between rivals): step in (Leadership 7) or let them sort it out. Stories by the fire (friends on the team): a night of old stories. Cold feet (a Coward): convince them (Leadership 8) or send them home alone (70% make it).

Conflicts (conflicts.js)

Near groups' bases (within 5 tiles), passing AI parties, infected ground and hordes. Chance = 10% × people pressure + 6% × the dead + 8% × traffic, ×1.25 at night, ×stance, at most 35%, at most one per 20 hours. A perception roll decides who sees whom first. Forced (they found you): ambush, roadblock, zealot patrol, checkpoint, challenge, a surge of the dead. Observed (you found them): a camp by the road, a robbery, prisoners on the move, two groups fighting, the dead swarming survivors. Covered in the conflicts chapter.

Trade-route and supply convoys do not get cards: their road trouble is resolved by the escort table (Light: dead ×0.6, small raids ×1.5; Heavy: dead ×1.5, small raids ×0.4, large raids ×1.6).

Changed from Draft 1: event families match Draft 1's five (hazards, zombies, humans, discoveries, crew). Its "small playable local map for major events" became conflicts that open tactical battles. Known routes do not get fewer events, and there is no fast travel inside your territory.

Orders, contact and ETA

  • Team orders: when a job is done, the team waits with a "Done" card: act on that tile, move anywhere, return home or hold. The team panel queue covers move, return, scavenge, look around (2 h), camp, roadside repair, treat, field crafting, boat crossings, hunting and fishing. A waiting team starts an order at once, while a moving team adds it to its queue.
  • Radio rule: a team inside coverage (a station's 8 tiles or a linked relay's 6) takes new orders at any time and reports live. Out of contact it follows its plan, and you can only steer it through the cards it raises. A holding team checks in every 12 hours (an open-ended camp once a day). Your own base tiles count as contact.
  • Estimated position: an out-of-contact team on the map is a faded sprite with a "?" badge along its planned road.
  • ETA: arrival estimates in the planner, panels and queue step through the same hour-by-hour movement as the simulation, including the weather ahead, the load and the stance. A team stopped by a road fight resumes its queued trip.

Camps and field Heat Built

A team can camp for 8 h, 24 h or until broken off. The hurt heal slowly, infected wounds still need meds, and a camp with no food or water heals nobody. A camp is drawn as a tent in the team's colour with a campfire.

A team that stays on one tile (waiting, holding, camping, gathering, building a boat, looking around, crafting, repairing, treating) builds Heat from noise, smoke and smell. Per hour: activity (waiting, looking around, treating 1; gathering, crafting, repairs 1.5; boat building 1.8; camp 2; hunting 1.2; a survey sweep 0.5) × place (countryside 1, suburbs 1.4, city 1.8, × 1 + 0.6 × infection) × hive land 1.4 (2 on the ring) × team size (0.7 + 0.1 a person) × night (a camp fire 1.3, otherwise 0.8) × stealthy 0.8, capped at 100. Camp kit lowers it: camo netting ×0.75 while staying put, a ghillie wrap ×0.85. Heat falls 8 an hour on the move and is 0 within a tile of your bases.

Each hour on the spot, the chance of trouble is 12% × (Heat/100)1.5 × danger. Danger is 0.15 + 3 × the road's conflict chance there, +0.3 city or +0.15 suburbs, +0.3 hive land or +0.8 next to a hive, +0.2 with a horde within 3 tiles, capped at 2. Trouble is a conflict card (a horde within 3 tiles is drawn in as a bigger surge) and halves the Heat. A quiet countryside camp for a night reaches about Heat 25 with about 1% risk. A night in an infected city is about a coin flip.

Design intent: Draft 1's "greed has a timer" extended from raids to the whole map. Lingering anywhere dangerous should feel like a clock running.

Hunting and fishing

Food from the land itself: hunts, snare lines and fishing by teams in the field, and hunting cabins and fishers at bases. Fresh game is heavy and spoils fast, every gunshot is noise, and the land runs out of game if you overhunt it. The tile panel says what a tile holds: "Game: plenty / some / scarce", with the expected haul of an 8-hour hunt.

Hunting Built

  • The order: Hunt on any land tile for 2–24 hours (team panel: 4/8/12 h; right-click Hunting trip; the planner's Hunting trip preset: go, hunt 8 hours, come home).
  • Land: forest 1, hills 0.8, farmland 0.55, mountains 0.5, suburbs 0.12, city 0.03. Then ×1.1 by water, ×0.8 on a site, × (1 − 0.6 × infection), hive land ×0.15 (red-skull ring ×0.05), your base tile ×0.3 and next to a base ×0.6.
  • Season: spring 0.8, summer 0.9, autumn 1.25, winter 0.95 (×1.2 with snow on the ground for tracks).
  • Time of day: dawn and dusk (05–07, 17–19) ×1.3, day ×1, night ×0.35. A storm or blizzard halves it.
  • The team: its three best hunters count 1, ½ and ¼. Each is weapon × skill gap × (0.55 + 0.09 × (0.5 Shooting + 0.35 Scouting + 0.15 Stealth)) × 1.25 with the Hunter perk. Each hand without a weapon adds 0.08 to big-game odds by driving game (up to two). A team with no hunting weapon cannot hunt and sets snares instead.
  • Noise: each kill with a firearm is 1 shot, with a second shot (a miss first) 40% of the time for big game and 20% for small. Each shot uses 1 ammo and adds 6 Heat (scaled by the gun's noise), and each wandering horde within 3 tiles turns toward the shots 25% of the time. Bows and crossbows are silent and use no ammo. A rifle hunt in good country fires 5–8 shots in 8 hours (Heat about 40–50 from the shots alone).
  • Expect: two decent hunters with a rifle and a pistol bag about 23 food in 8 hours of autumn forest, about 13 on farmland and 15 in spring.

Overhunting, carcasses and spoilage

  • Game level: every tile starts at 1. Each kill lowers it (big game per 100 rations, small game per 60), and it grows back toward 1 with a half-life of 20 days: weeks, not days.
  • Carcasses weigh 1 kg a ration (packed food weighs 0.5). A deer on foot moves a pair of hunters into a heavier load band. A car carries it at no cost to the riders, and bike racks take part of it. What does not fit is left where it fell, and the estimates count only what is really kept.
  • Fresh game spoils in the field: 35% a day above 24 °C, 22% above 12 °C, 12% above 4 °C, 6% around freezing and 2% in a freeze. A team eats its fresh game first. At a base it is eaten first and the drying racks take it first. On top of the stores' own rate it loses 5% a day (10% in the heat, 2% in the cold, almost nothing frozen), ×0.4 with a cold store and ×0.6 with a hunting cabin.

Snares Built

  • Set a line (2 h): 8 snares, using a coil of snare wire (it stays with the line and comes back when the line is lifted) or 2 scrap of fence wire. It is quiet: Heat ×0.8 while setting or checking.
  • A line catches about 1 small game a day × land × level × season. Check it (1 h) to collect the catch, or lift it (1 h) to take it up.
  • Caught game keeps 3 days (2 in the heat, 6 in the cold). From the third day without a check, each day has an 8% chance that the catch is robbed or sprung (more near towns, on hive land or with a horde near), and 30% of the time the line goes with it. Lines left 30 days are gone.

Fishing Built

  • The order: Fish for 4 or 8 hours on a river bank with a fishing rod or net carried. The planner's Fishing trip takes the hub's gear along.
  • The water: a deep river bend (6+ water tiles within 2) ×1, a river ×0.85. The code also knows a lake shore (×1) and a pond (×0.6), but the map generator makes only rivers.
  • Modifiers: season (spring 1.15, summer 1, autumn 0.9, winter 0.75), dawn and dusk ×1.4, night ×0.5, skill (0.6 + 0.08 × (0.6 Scouting + 0.4 Stealth)), and the spot's level. It is quiet: Heat ×0.7.
  • Ice: on frozen days you need a crowbar, machete, hatchet or axe to cut the ice, and the catch is halved. A fished-out spot comes back in about 10 days.

Items: fishing rod, fishing net and snare wire, found at cabins, lodges, campgrounds, ranger stations, hardware and feed stores and farms. (The deer call found at lodges is a stealth lure for battles, not a hunting tool.)

At a base

  • Hunting cabin (12 scrap, at any base with game land within 2 tiles, abundance adding up to 3): up to 2 people on the Hunt work type or role hunt the land within 2 tiles for 3 hours a day at dawn and dusk, with their own weapons and the base's ammo (never the last 3 a head + 10, which are kept for the walls). They use the same rolls and depletion as a team, and a hunter without a weapon tends snares. Two hunters in autumn forest bring in about 11 food a day. The cabin smokes up to 6 fresh game a day into 5 preserved food.
  • Fish work or role: a base on a river with a rod or net in its armory, one fisher each, 3 hours a day.
  • The default Hunter role, the Hunting party crew, and crews' Hunt and Fish trips run it all without you.
  • The autopilot hunts (and fishes, and sets or checks snares on the way) when the winter outlook falls short, food is under its target, or in autumn to stock up. It builds a hunting cabin where the land is good.
